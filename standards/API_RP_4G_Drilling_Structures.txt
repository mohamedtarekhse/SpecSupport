[STANDARD: API RP 4G]
[TITLE: Inspection and Maintenance of Drilling and Well Servicing Structures]
[EDITION: 5th Edition]

[CLAUSE: 1.1 — Scope]
This recommended practice covers the inspection, maintenance, and repair of masts, derricks, and substructures used in drilling and well servicing operations. It applies to: portable masts, jackknife derricks, standard derricks, and their associated substructures. It does not apply to offshore drilling units (covered by API RP 2D) or mobile offshore drilling units.
PURPOSE: Establish minimum inspection requirements to maintain structural integrity and safe operation of land drilling and well servicing structures.
---

[CLAUSE: 3.1 — Definitions Critical to Inspection]
MAST: A portable derrick capable of being erected as a unit, distinguished from a standard derrick by its portability.
DERRICK: A framework structure over a well to support drilling equipment. Standard derrick is assembled piece by piece at the well site.
SUBSTRUCTURE: The foundation structure that supports the derrick or mast and the drawworks, and provides clearance below the rig floor for BOP equipment.
PRIMARY STRUCTURAL MEMBER: Any member that is part of the main load path from hook load through the structure to the ground. Failure of a primary member would cause collapse or significant deformation.
SECONDARY STRUCTURAL MEMBER: Bracing, girts, and other members that provide stability but are not in the primary load path.
CRITICAL WELD: A weld joining two primary structural members or a primary member to its connection point.
---

[CLAUSE: 4.1 — Inspection Classification System]
ALL DRILLING AND WELL SERVICING STRUCTURES SHALL BE CLASSIFIED INTO ONE OF FOUR CONDITIONS:

CLASS 1 — NO REPAIRS REQUIRED:
Structure is in like-new or well-maintained condition. All primary and secondary members are intact and undamaged. No cracks, no significant deformation, no dangerous corrosion. All welds visually sound. All connection hardware (pins, bolts, locking devices) present, secure, and undamaged. Paint or coating in good condition providing corrosion protection. Full rated hook load capacity is maintained.
ACCEPTANCE: All items verified as Class 1. Full rated capacity available for use.

CLASS 2 — REPAIRS REQUIRED BEFORE FURTHER USE:
Defects exist that require correction before the structure is returned to drilling/well servicing operations. Structure shall NOT be used for drilling or well servicing until all Class 2 items are repaired. However, the structure may be transported to a repair facility without load (without the weight of tubulars or hook loads). Examples of Class 2 defects:
- Weld cracks in secondary members
- Localized corrosion pitting (5% to 15% wall thickness loss)
- Minor bowing or bending of secondary bracing members (≤L/250 lateral displacement)
- Missing or loose bolts or pins in secondary connections
- Damaged but structurally adequate ladder, walkway, or monkey board
- Paint failure with surface rust (no pitting yet)
- Small dents in members not exceeding 5% diameter or section depth
ACCEPTANCE: All Class 2 items documented in writing. Repairs completed and verified before next operation.
REJECTION: Using structure with documented Class 2 items without completing repairs.

CLASS 3 — MAJOR REPAIRS REQUIRED. STRUCTURE SHALL NOT BE MOVED OR USED:
Defects are severe enough that the structure shall not be used OR transported until major repairs are completed and the structure is re-classified by a qualified inspector. The structure shall be stabilized in place if possible. Examples of Class 3 defects:
- Cracks in primary structural members or primary welds
- Corrosion causing >15% wall thickness loss in any primary member
- Corrosion causing >10% cross-sectional area loss in any primary member
- Buckled or collapsed primary members
- Failed connections (pins sheared, bolts sheared) in primary load path
- Visible permanent deformation of mast legs or substructure legs
- Missing primary members (fallen or removed)
- Crown block support structure damaged or cracked
- Drawworks mounting structure cracked or deformed
ACCEPTANCE: None for use. Structure stabilized in place. Repair plan developed by qualified engineer. Repairs completed and structure re-inspected before reclassification.
REJECTION: Any movement, loading, or use of Class 3 structure before completion of repairs and re-inspection. Partial repairs that do not address all Class 3 defects.

CLASS 4 — RETIRED FROM SERVICE:
Structure is beyond economical repair, has suffered catastrophic damage, or the cost of repair exceeds value. The structure shall be permanently removed from service. Class 4 structures shall be:
1. Marked permanently as "Retired — Not for Service."
2. Cut into sections to prevent re-use, OR
3. Disposed of per company procedure.
REJECTION: Any attempt to return a Class 4 structure to service under any circumstances. Sale or transfer of Class 4 structure for drilling use without full disclosure and engineering evaluation.

[CLAUSE: 4.2 — Inspection Frequency Requirements]
PRIOR TO EACH WELL (MINIMUM FREQUENCY):
A visual inspection shall be conducted before placing the structure in service at each new well location or before re-entering a previously drilled well. Minimum scope: all primary members visual check, all connection pins and locks verified, crown block support visual, guy wire/dead line anchor check (if applicable), mast raising mechanism check.

ANNUAL INSPECTION:
A complete visual inspection of the entire structure covering all accessible primary and secondary members, all welds, all connections, and all appurtenances. Complete the inspection on Form API 4G-1 (or equivalent). This inspection establishes the Class 1/2/3/4 rating.

DETAILED INSPECTION (Every 3 to 5 Years or After Any Incident):
Complete visual inspection PLUS:
- MT or PT examination of all primary welds and heat-affected zones
- MT or PT of all crown block attachment welds and pins
- MT or PT of all substructure leg-to-rig floor connection welds
- Dimensional verification of critical pin clearances
- Load test documentation review
ACCEPTANCE: All inspections performed on schedule. Records on Form API 4G-1 or equivalent, signed by qualified inspector.
REJECTION: Structure placed in service without current inspection record. Annual inspection overdue. Detailed inspection interval exceeded without documentation of extension by qualified engineer.

POST-INCIDENT INSPECTION:
A detailed inspection (minimum) shall be performed after any of the following before return to service:
- Dropped load or tubular impact on structure
- Uncontrolled mast movement (upending or laying down incident)
- Collision with another structure or equipment
- Wind or weather event exceeding design criteria
- Any incident where structure loads may have exceeded design loads
ACCEPTANCE: Post-incident inspection completed and documented. Classification assigned.
REJECTION: Structure returned to service after any incident without post-incident inspection.

[CLAUSE: 5.1 — Primary Member Inspection Requirements]
MAST LEGS AND CHORD MEMBERS:
Visual: Check for cracks, bowing, denting, corrosion, paint condition.
NDE (Detailed Inspection): MT or PT at all welded joints, splice connections, and at any point where visual inspection raises concern.
Straightness: Maximum allowable bow in any primary leg = L/500 where L = unsupported length between connections.
ACCEPTANCE: No cracks. No dents >5% of section depth. Bow ≤ L/500. No corrosion >5% wall loss.
REJECTION: Any crack. Dent >5% section depth. Bow >L/500. Corrosion >15% wall loss (Class 3).

DIAGONAL BRACING:
Visual: Check for buckled members, missing bolts, cracked welds.
Allowable bowing: ≤ L/250 for secondary bracing. Any bowing in primary bracing: Class 3.
ACCEPTANCE: No buckled members. All connection bolts present and torqued. No visual weld cracks.
REJECTION: Any buckled primary bracing member. Missing connection bolts in primary load path. Cracked welds in bracing-to-chord connections.

CROWN BLOCK SUPPORT (WATER TABLE / CROWN DECK):
This is the highest criticality area — failure results in crown block falling.
Visual: Every inspection interval.
MT or PT: Every detailed inspection AND after any impact or shock load on crown.
ACCEPTANCE: No cracks in any welds or members. All gussets intact. Crown block attachment pins undamaged, pins and retainers in place.
REJECTION: Any crack in crown deck structure. Missing or damaged attachment pins. Corrosion in crown structure welds without PT/MT clearance.
---

[CLAUSE: 5.2 — Substructure Inspection]
SUBSTRUCTURE LEGS AND SILLS:
Visual check for: corrosion at base (most common failure area — moisture traps at ground level), cracks at welded joints, deformation, settlement.
Detailed inspection includes: thickness measurement of base sections using UT if corrosion is suspected, MT of all critical welds at leg-to-sill connections and at corners.
ACCEPTANCE: Wall thickness loss ≤10% of nominal at any location. No cracks. Base plates level within 1/8 in (3mm) across full base.
REJECTION: Wall thickness loss >15% at any location. Any crack in leg or sill. Base settlement >1/8 in. Uneven base causing mast misalignment.

SETBACK AREA (FINGERBOARD / MONKEY BOARD):
Inspect for: damaged fingers that would trap tubulars, cracks in welds, damaged or missing safety latches.
ACCEPTANCE: All fingers intact. Welds visually sound. Safety latches functional.
REJECTION: Bent or missing fingers in setback area. Missing safety latches. Cracked welds at fingerboard attachment to mast.

STABBING BOARD:
Inspect before each well: attachment to mast intact, safety cable present and sound, platform surface non-slip, hinges and locking mechanism functional.
ACCEPTANCE: All attachment points secure. Safety cable ≤10% broken wires in any strand.
REJECTION: Any damaged attachment point. Safety cable with >10% broken wires. Missing or non-functional locking mechanism.
---

[CLAUSE: 5.3 — Weld Inspection Criteria for Drilling Structures]
All primary structural welds on drilling structures shall meet these visual acceptance criteria as a minimum:
CRACKS: None permitted of any size or orientation in primary welds.
UNDERCUT: ≤ 0.8 mm (1/32 in) depth, ≤ 50 mm (2 in) aggregate length in any 300 mm.
POROSITY: No individual pore >3 mm (1/8 in) diameter. No cluster porosity.
INCOMPLETE FUSION: None visible at weld surface.
WELD SIZE: Fillet weld size shall not be less than specified on drawing or repair procedure.
WELD REINFORCEMENT: Maximum 5 mm (3/16 in) for primary welds.
MT/PT CRITERIA (for detailed inspection):
- No linear relevant indications (any length)
- No rounded relevant indication >5 mm (3/16 in)
- No more than 4 relevant indications in any 38 mm × 150 mm area
ACCEPTANCE: All above criteria met on all primary welds.
REJECTION: Any crack. Any linear indication on MT/PT. Undercut >0.8mm. Cluster porosity. Weld size below minimum.
---

[CLAUSE: 5.4 — Pin and Connection Hardware Inspection]
PINS (Crown block pins, deadline anchor pins, mast hinge pins, substructure pins):
Visual: Scoring, galling, pitting, bending, missing cotter pins or snap rings.
MT (preferred) or PT: All major pins at detailed inspection interval.
Dimensional: Measure pin diameter with calipers. Measure bore diameter.
Maximum clearance = 1% of nominal pin diameter. Example: 100 mm (4 in) pin — maximum clearance = 1 mm (0.040 in).
ACCEPTANCE: No cracks on MT/PT. Clearance ≤1% of nominal diameter. No scoring >0.5mm depth. All retaining devices (cotter pins, snap rings, locking pins) present.
REJECTION: Any crack. Clearance >1% nominal. Bent pin. Scoring >0.5mm depth. Missing retaining device.

BOLTED CONNECTIONS (Secondary members, platforms, ladders):
Visual: Missing bolts, loose bolts (check with torque wrench if suspect), corroded bolts.
ACCEPTANCE: All bolts present. No visible corrosion through-thread. Torque verified per specification.
REJECTION: Any missing structural bolt. Any bolt with through-thread corrosion. Bolts loose (under-torque) at primary connections.

GUY WIRES (Where used for mast stabilization):
Visual: Broken wires, bird-caging, kinking, corrosion.
ACCEPTANCE: No more than 2 broken wires in any lay length. No kinks or bird-caging. Adequate tension.
REJECTION: >2 broken wires in any lay length. Any kink or bird-cage. Corroded wire without breaking strength verification.
---

[CLAUSE: 5.5 — Corrosion Assessment Criteria]
PITTING DEPTH CLASSIFICATION:
Minor Pitting (Class 1): ≤5% of nominal wall thickness. Record and monitor.
Moderate Pitting (Class 2): 5% to 15% of nominal wall thickness. Repair coating. Measure at next inspection. Consider repair.
Severe Pitting (Class 3): >15% of nominal wall thickness at any point. OR: Cross-sectional area loss >10% at any section. REMOVE FROM SERVICE until repaired.

MEASUREMENT METHOD: Pit depth gauge or calibrated UT thickness measurement. Visual estimate alone is not acceptable for Class determination in corroded areas.
ACCEPTANCE: Pitting ≤5% nominal wall. Cross-section loss ≤10%.
REJECTION: Pit depth >15% nominal wall. Cross-section area loss >10%. Corrosion in primary members not quantified — must measure, not estimate.
---

[CLAUSE: 6.1 — Repair Welding Requirements]
All repair welding on drilling structures shall be performed:
1. Using a qualified WPS per AWS D1.1 (structural steel) or equivalent.
2. By a certified welder qualified in the welding position required.
3. With preheat per WPS (minimum 93°C/200°F for carbon steel members >25mm thick).
4. Followed by NDE (MT or PT minimum) of the completed repair weld.
5. Documented with a repair record showing: welder ID, WPS used, NDE results, inspector sign-off.
ACCEPTANCE: Repair weld meets AWS D1.1 visual and MT/PT criteria. Documentation complete.
REJECTION: Repair weld without qualified WPS. Repair by unqualified welder. No NDE after repair. No documentation.
---

[CLAUSE: 7.1 — Load Rating and Documentation]
Every drilling structure shall have a nameplate or documentation specifying:
- Manufacturer name
- Serial or unit number
- Maximum rated hook load (kN or tons)
- Wind load rating
- Date of manufacture
If the original nameplate is missing or illegible, the rated load shall be determined by engineering analysis and documented before the structure is placed in service.
ACCEPTANCE: Nameplate present and legible. Rated loads available. Operating loads remain within rated capacity.
REJECTION: Operating a structure without a nameplate or documented load rating. Exceeding rated hook load. No wind speed limitation documented.
