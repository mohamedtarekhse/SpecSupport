CLAUSE 1 — SCOPE AND PURPOSE OF AWS B1.11 VISUAL EXAMINATION
[Section: AWS B1.11 Clause 1.1 – 1.3]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

AWS B1.11 establishes the requirements, procedures, and guidance for performing
visual examination (VT) (الفحص البصري) of welds in metallic structures and
pressure-containing components. The standard applies to all weld types including
fillet welds (لحام الزاوية), groove welds (لحام الأخدود), and plug/slot welds
produced by any fusion welding process. Visual examination is the primary and
most widely used nondestructive examination (NDE) (الفحص غير التدميري) method in
welding quality assurance programs; it is the only method that can be applied at
all three stages — before, during, and after welding. AWS B1.11 does not
establish acceptance criteria independently; it is used in conjunction with the
applicable construction standard (e.g., AWS D1.1 for structural steel, API 1104
for pipelines) which governs the final acceptance/rejection decision.

ACCEPTANCE:
- VT program is documented and references both AWS B1.11 (procedure) and the
  governing construction standard (acceptance criteria) — both documents must
  be identified on the Inspection and Test Plan (ITP) (خطة الفحص والاختبار).
- VT is performed at minimum at three stages: pre-weld, in-process, and
  post-weld — all three stages must be recorded.

REJECTION:
- Any VT program that substitutes AWS B1.11 as the acceptance standard without
  referencing the applicable construction code is non-conforming.
- Post-weld VT performed without documented pre-weld and in-process records is
  incomplete and the weld status must be placed on hold (تعليق) pending
  review.

CLAUSE 2 — INSPECTOR QUALIFICATION REQUIREMENTS FOR VISUAL EXAMINATION
[Section: AWS B1.11 Clause 4.1 – 4.3]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

AWS B1.11 Clause 4.1 requires that visual examination be performed only by
qualified and certified personnel who have demonstrated competency in weld
evaluation. Accepted qualification credentials include: AWS Certified Welding
Inspector (CWI) (مفتش اللحام المعتمد من AWS), CSWIP 3.1 Welding Inspector
(TWI-UK certification), ASNT Level II VT (الفحص البصري – المستوى الثاني),
or personnel qualified to the requirements of the applicable construction
standard. In the MENA oilfield (عمليات النفط والغاز — منطقة الشرق الأوسط
وشمال أفريقيا), client companies (e.g., EGPC, ENI, BP Egypt) typically require
CWI or CSWIP 3.1 as the minimum for structural and pressure piping VT. Senior
inspectors may hold CWI Senior Welding Inspector (SCWI) for supervisory roles.
All certifications must be current and valid at the time of inspection — an
expired certificate is not acceptable under any circumstances.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Inspector holds valid (unexpired) CWI, CSWIP 3.1, or ASNT Level II VT
  certification — certificate expiry date must be ≥ the date of inspection.
- CWI certification cycle: 9-year recertification; CSWIP 3.1: 5-year renewal.
- Inspector experience: minimum 2 years working experience in welding inspection
  for pressure or structural applications, documented in resume/CV on file.

REJECTION:
- Inspector certification expired prior to date of weld inspection — all VT
  results are invalid and inspection must be repeated by a qualified inspector.
- Inspector holding only ASNT Level I VT performing final acceptance VT without
  Level II supervision — non-conformance (عدم المطابقة).
- Inspector with no documented formal qualification performing VT on
  safety-critical welds — automatic stop-work, escalate to QA Manager.

CLAUSE 3 — EYE VISION REQUIREMENTS FOR VT INSPECTORS
[Section: AWS B1.11 Clause 4.2]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

AWS B1.11 Clause 4.2 mandates that all inspectors performing visual examination
demonstrate adequate near visual acuity (حدة الرؤية القريبة) before conducting
weld inspections. The standard minimum requirement is the ability to read Jaeger
Number 2 (J2) type or equivalent at a distance of not less than 12 inches
(300 mm) with or without corrective lenses (نظارات طبية). Color perception
(تمييز الألوان) testing is required when the inspector must distinguish color
contrast during VT — a standard Ishihara color plate test or equivalent must be
passed. Vision testing must be performed annually (كل سنة) and documented; test
records must be retained in the inspector's personnel qualification file. An
inspector who cannot demonstrate J2 near vision at 12 inches must not perform
visual examination of welds, regardless of other qualifications held.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Near vision: reads Jaeger J2 or equivalent at ≥ 12 inches (300 mm) — with
  or without corrective lenses, annually verified and documented.
- Color vision: passes Ishihara 14-plate minimum test OR equivalent color
  perception test — no more than 2 plate misidentifications permitted.
- Vision test record on file and current (test date within 12 months of
  inspection date).

REJECTION:
- Inspector cannot read Jaeger J2 at 12 inches — disqualified from VT until
  vision is corrected and re-tested.
- No annual vision test record available — inspector is not cleared for duty;
  inspection results are suspect and must be verified.
- Inspector with documented color blindness (عمى الألوان) performing unaided
  color-contrast dependent VT — non-conforming; must be paired with a
  color-sighted supervisor or use alternative contrast methods.

CLAUSE 4 — LIGHTING REQUIREMENTS FOR VISUAL EXAMINATION
[Section: AWS B1.11 Clause 5.1 – 5.2]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

AWS B1.11 Clause 5.1 specifies that the examination surface (سطح الفحص) must
be adequately illuminated to permit detection of discontinuities. The minimum
required illuminance (شدة الإضاءة) on the examination surface is 1,000 lux
(100 foot-candles) for direct VT as recommended by AWS B1.11 and affirmed by
ASME V Article 9; a practical minimum of 500 lux (50 foot-candles) is the
absolute floor below which VT must not proceed. In oilfield field conditions
(conditions الحقل), natural daylight supplemented by portable LED inspection
lamps (مصباح فحص LED محمول) is acceptable provided illuminance is verified with
a calibrated lux meter (جهاز قياس الإضاءة) before starting work. Reflective
glare and shadowing must be controlled — the light source must be positioned to
maximize surface relief and discontinuity detection, not to create blind spots.
Lux meter calibration must be current (within 12 months) and recorded on the
inspection report.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Illuminance at examination surface ≥ 1,000 lux (100 fc) — preferred and
  target value for all VT.
- Minimum permissible illuminance: 500 lux (50 fc) — only acceptable in
  areas where achieving 1,000 lux is physically impractical (e.g., confined
  space, deep pipe ID access).
- Lux meter reading recorded at start and end of each inspection shift on the
  VT report.

REJECTION:
- Illuminance measured < 500 lux (< 50 fc) — VT must stop immediately;
  additional lighting must be provided before inspection continues.
- Lux meter used is uncalibrated or calibration expired — readings are invalid;
  inspection is on hold until a calibrated instrument is used.
- Inspection performed in direct sunlight causing specular glare with no
  shielding — light conditions are non-compliant; reposition or shield the
  light source.

CLAUSE 5 — VIEWING ANGLE AND DISTANCE REQUIREMENTS
[Section: AWS B1.11 Clause 5.3 – 5.4]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

AWS B1.11 Clause 5.3 defines the geometric conditions under which direct visual
examination is valid. The inspector's eye must be positioned within 24 inches
(610 mm) of the examination surface (مسافة الفحص) and at an angle of not less
than 30 degrees from the surface plane (زاوية الفحص). Angles below 30 degrees
create severe foreshortening (تشوه المنظور) which prevents reliable detection of
surface discontinuities, particularly planar flaws such as cracks and lack of
fusion. Where direct access is not possible (e.g., pipe ID root surface, back
side of weld), remote visual examination using mirrors (مرايا الفحص), borescopes
(منظار الثقوب), or video-assisted equipment may be used, provided the resolution
is equivalent to direct VT at the specified distance and angle. The use of
optical aids (magnifying glass 2×–4×, borescope) is permitted and encouraged
when finer evaluation of detected indications is required.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Viewing distance: inspector's eye ≤ 24 inches (610 mm) from the examination
  surface.
- Viewing angle: ≥ 30 degrees from the surface plane (i.e., ≥ 30° from
  tangent to the surface at the point of examination).
- Remote VT optical aids: image resolution equivalent to or better than 20/20
  vision at 24 inches — verified by standard resolution test chart.

REJECTION:
- Inspector viewing weld surface at < 30 degrees from the surface plane — the
  examination is invalid for that surface area; must be repeated at correct
  angle.
- Viewing distance > 24 inches without supplemental optical aids — examination
  is non-compliant; inspector must reposition.
- Remote VT equipment used without documented resolution qualification —
  examination results cannot be accepted.

CLAUSE 6 — SURFACE CLEANING REQUIREMENTS BEFORE VT
[Section: AWS B1.11 Clause 6.1 – 6.2]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

AWS B1.11 Clause 6.1 requires that the weld and adjacent base metal surface be
cleaned of all materials that could mask or obscure surface discontinuities prior
to visual examination. Slag (خبث اللحام), weld spatter (رذاذ اللحام), heavy
scale (قشرة الأكسيد), paint, grease, and dirt must be removed from the weld bead
and a minimum of 1 inch (25 mm) on each side of the weld toes (أطراف اللحام).
Cleaning methods include wire brushing (فرشاة سلكية), chipping (إزالة الخبث),
grinding (جلخ), and solvent wiping — but must not mask underlying defects or
damage the weld surface. Flame cleaning is prohibited where it could alter the
heat input to the heat-affected zone (منطقة التأثر الحراري). After cleaning,
the surface must be visually dry and free of cleaning agent residues before VT
commences. In the field on pipelines and structural steel, the inspection team
must verify cleaning quality before the inspector is called to the weld.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Weld surface and ≥ 1 inch (25 mm) each side of weld toes: free of slag,
  spatter, heavy scale, grease, paint, and debris.
- Surface is dry and free of cleaning agent residue.
- Weld bead color: light gray or metallic — indicating proper interpass
  cleaning; no heavy black oxide layer remaining.

REJECTION:
- Any slag islands (جزر الخبث) remaining on weld surface — cleaning is
  incomplete; VT must not proceed until slag is fully removed.
- Spatter accumulation within 1 inch of weld toes not removed — rejects
  cleaning QC; spatter must be removed.
- Surface wet with water, solvent, or coating material — VT on hold; surface
  must dry completely before examination begins.

CLAUSE 7 — PRE-WELD VISUAL EXAMINATION — FIT-UP INSPECTION
[Section: AWS B1.11 Clause 7.1 – 7.5]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

AWS B1.11 Clause 7.1 establishes that pre-weld visual examination is mandatory
and must be completed and documented before any welding commences. The pre-weld
VT (الفحص البصري قبل اللحام) covers joint fit-up geometry including: root
opening / root gap (فتحة الجذر), root face / land (وجه الجذر), groove angle /
bevel angle (زاوية الشطب), and high-low misalignment (سوء المحاذاة — هاي-لو).
Additionally, base metal condition must be checked for laminations, gouges, and
pre-existing cracks at the bevel faces. Tack welds (لحامات التثبيت) must be
inspected for cracks, porosity, and adequate size; defective tack welds must be
removed entirely, not just ground flush. Measuring tools for pre-weld VT include:
root gap gauge (مقياس فتحة الجذر), hi-lo gauge (مقياس الهاي-لو), protractor
(منقلة الزاوية), and straight edge.

ACCEPTANCE (per AWS D1.1 Table 6.1 — referenced by B1.11 for structural):
- Root gap (butt weld, SMAW/FCAW): 1/16 inch to 3/16 inch (1.6 – 4.8 mm).
- Root face (land): 0 – 1/8 inch (0 – 3.2 mm) per WPS (وثيقة مواصفة إجراء
  اللحام).
- Groove angle: per WPS ± 5 degrees — typical 60° ± 5°.
- Hi-lo (linear misalignment): ≤ 1/16 inch (1.6 mm) for t ≤ 3/4 inch;
  ≤ 10% of t or 1/8 inch (3.2 mm) max for t > 3/4 inch.
- Tack welds: no cracks, no visible porosity — cracked tacks must be removed.

REJECTION:
- Root gap < 1/16 inch (1.6 mm) — risk of incomplete root fusion; must be
  re-fit.
- Root gap > 3/16 inch (4.8 mm) without engineer-approved backing or weld
  procedure revision — reject fit-up.
- Hi-lo > 1/8 inch (3.2 mm) on t > 3/4 inch or > 1/16 inch on t ≤ 3/4 inch
  — reject fit-up; realign before welding.
- Any crack detected on tack weld or bevel face — tack must be removed; base
  metal crack must be reported and evaluated per engineering hold (توقف
  هندسي).

CLAUSE 8 — IN-PROCESS VISUAL EXAMINATION — ROOT PASS AND INTERPASS
[Section: AWS B1.11 Clause 7.6 – 7.8]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

AWS B1.11 Clause 7.6 mandates in-process visual examination at defined
inspection hold points (نقاط التوقف للفحص) during welding. The most critical
in-process VT is inspection of the root pass (تمريرة الجذر) before any
subsequent fill passes are deposited; once the root is buried, surface VT of the
root is impossible and RT/UT would be required to detect root defects. Each
interpass (التمريرة البينية) must be visually checked and cleaned before the next
pass is deposited — slag inclusions trapped between passes will create embedded
defects. The inspector must verify that the welder is following the qualified WPS
for preheat temperature (درجة حرارة ما قبل اللحام), interpass temperature
(درجة حرارة ما بين التمريرات), and electrode/wire specification. Any cold lap
(تداخل بارد), undercut (قطع أسفل اللحام), or cracks found in-process must be
corrected before the next pass is deposited.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Root pass: full penetration on pipe OD side — visible root bead with no
  burn-through > 1/4 inch (6 mm) in any direction; root concavity ≤ 1/32
  inch (0.8 mm) for pipe welds.
- Each interpass: free of slag, cracks, and cold lap before next pass; surface
  is bright metallic after brushing.
- Interpass temperature: ≤ maximum specified in WPS (typically 300°C / 572°F
  for carbon steel); measured with contact pyrometer (ميزان الحرارة التلامسي)
  or temp-stick.

REJECTION:
- Root pass with burn-through > 1/4 inch (6 mm) long — stop welding; repair
  required before fill passes.
- Cold lap (تداخل بارد) visible on any interpass surface — no fill pass until
  cold lap is ground out and re-inspected.
- Crack detected on any interpass — stop welding immediately; evaluate extent
  and perform weld repair (إصلاح اللحام); re-inspect repaired area.
- Interpass temperature exceeding WPS maximum — stop welding; allow cooling;
  document deviation; evaluate for heat input effect.

CLAUSE 9 — POST-WELD VISUAL EXAMINATION — FINAL WELD PROFILE INSPECTION
[Section: AWS B1.11 Clause 7.9 – 7.11]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

AWS B1.11 Clause 7.9 defines the requirements for post-weld visual examination
(الفحص البصري بعد اللحام) which is the final gate before a weld is released for
service, painting, or further NDE (PT, MT, UT, RT). Post-weld VT must be
performed after all welding, including repairs, is complete and after the weld
has cooled to ambient temperature (unless the construction code permits hot
inspection, which must be documented). The inspector examines the full weld
length for profile conformance, surface discontinuities, dimensional compliance,
and weld identification marks. Post-weld VT must cover both weld faces and both
toe lines (حد الاندماج مع المعدن الأساسي) — inspecting only the weld crown
face and ignoring the weld toes is a common field error. All final VT results
must be recorded on a formal VT examination report with weld ID, inspector name,
date, and disposition (قبول / رفض).

ACCEPTANCE:
- Weld completely cooled to ambient before VT commences (unless hot VT is WPS/
  code-approved and documented).
- Full weld length examined on all accessible surfaces — 100% length coverage
  documented on report.
- All weld identification (joint number, weld number, welder ID stamp) legible
  on the joint.

REJECTION:
- VT performed on hot weld (> 50°C above ambient) without code authorization —
  results invalid; must be repeated after cooling.
- Less than 100% weld length coverage documented — incomplete VT; extend
  examination to full length.
- Missing welder identification stamp — non-conformance; weld traceability is
  compromised; NCR (تقرير عدم المطابقة) to be raised.

CLAUSE 10 — WELD DIMENSIONS: SIZE, WIDTH, AND REINFORCEMENT LIMITS
[Section: AWS B1.11 Clause 8.1 – 8.4]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

AWS B1.11 Clause 8.1 requires that weld dimensions be verified as conforming to
the design drawing (رسم التصميم) and WPS requirements. For fillet welds
(لحام الزاوية), the leg size (طول الساق) and effective throat (الحلق الفعلي)
must meet minimum drawing requirements. For groove welds (لحام الأخدود),
reinforcement height (ارتفاع التدعيم) on both crown and root sides must be
within permitted limits — excessive reinforcement creates a stress concentration
that reduces fatigue life. Weld width (عرض اللحام) must be sufficient to achieve
full penetration without excessive heat input; narrow welds in wide grooves
indicate insufficient weave or travel speed errors. Dimensional measurement tools:
fillet weld gauge (مقياس لحام الزاوية / Hi-Lo gauge), weld reinforcement gauge
(مقياس التدعيم), and vernier caliper (فرجار ورنييه).

ACCEPTANCE (per AWS D1.1 Clause 5.24):
- Groove weld crown reinforcement: ≤ 1/8 inch (3.2 mm) for t ≤ 1/4 inch;
  ≤ 3/16 inch (4.8 mm) for t > 1/4 inch.
- Fillet weld leg: ≥ required leg size per drawing; undersize ≤ 1/16 inch
  (1.6 mm) under specified size permitted for ≤ 10% of weld length.
- Weld width: consistent along length — no abrupt width variations > 25% of
  nominal width.

REJECTION:
- Crown reinforcement > 3/16 inch (4.8 mm) — excess reinforcement; grind flush
  or to code limit; re-inspect.
- Fillet weld leg undersize > 1/16 inch (1.6 mm) for > 10% of weld length —
  reject; add weld pass to restore size.
- Root reinforcement (pipe internal bead) < 0 mm (flush or concave) or
  > 3.2 mm (1/8 inch) — reject; evaluate for repair.

CLAUSE 11 — SURFACE CRACKS — ZERO TOLERANCE RULE
[Section: AWS B1.11 Clause 8.5; AWS D1.1 Clause 6.9.1]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

AWS B1.11 Clause 8.5, in conjunction with all referenced construction standards,
establishes absolute zero tolerance for surface cracks (شقوق السطح) in structural
and pressure-retaining welds. A crack (شق / صدع) is a planar discontinuity
characterized by a sharp tip and high aspect ratio (length >> width) — this
geometry creates severe stress intensification that can cause brittle fracture
without warning. Cracks may appear as: weld metal cracks (hot cracks/
solidification cracks), heat-affected zone (HAZ) cracks (cold cracks /
hydrogen-induced cracking — تشقق بسبب الهيدروجين), underbead cracks, crater
cracks (شقوق الحفرة الختامية), and toe cracks. Any linear indication detected
during VT that cannot be proven to be benign (e.g., a scrape mark or tool
mark) must be treated as a crack until proven otherwise by PT or MT. There is
no minimum crack length for rejection — even a hairline crack (0.1 mm length)
is cause for rejection.

ACCEPTANCE:
- No cracks of any type, orientation, or length are acceptable — zero
  tolerance applies to all weld zones: weld metal, fusion line, HAZ, and
  adjacent base metal within 1 inch (25 mm) of weld toe.
- Visual indication that appears crack-like but is confirmed as a tool mark /
  scratch by PT or MT with no bleed-out — may be accepted with documentation.

REJECTION:
- Any crack detected on weld surface, at weld toe, at crater, or in HAZ —
  immediate rejection; stop further fabrication on that joint; raise NCR.
- Crater crack (شق حفرة الختام) at weld stop position — reject; grind out
  crater and re-weld with proper run-out technique.
- Any linear indication > 1 mm length that cannot be confirmed as a non-crack
  by supplemental NDE (PT/MT) — must be treated as crack; reject pending NDE
  disposition.

CLAUSE 12 — POROSITY — VISUAL LIMITS (SIZE, FREQUENCY, GROUPING)
[Section: AWS B1.11 Clause 8.6; AWS D1.1 Table 6.1]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

AWS B1.11 Clause 8.6 addresses surface-breaking porosity (المسامية السطحية) —
gas pockets that are visible on the weld surface as round or elongated pits.
Surface porosity is the visible portion of what may be a more extensive internal
porosity network; therefore, its discovery during VT must trigger a root-cause
analysis of shielding gas, electrode condition, and joint cleanliness. For
structural steel welds per AWS D1.1, visible surface porosity is limited by
diameter and cluster rules. In oilfield pipeline applications per API 1104
Section 9.3.2, the rules are even stricter due to the cyclic loading and
internal pressure service. Porosity in the crater (مسامية حفرة الختام) is
particularly dangerous as it often indicates gas entrapment at the weld stop —
always inspect weld starts and stops carefully.

ACCEPTANCE (per AWS D1.1 Clause 6.9.3 for structural):
- Individual pore diameter: ≤ 3/32 inch (2.4 mm).
- Sum of visible pore diameters in any linear inch of weld: ≤ 3/8 inch
  (9.5 mm).
- Cluster porosity: no cluster larger than 1 inch (25 mm) diameter; clusters
  separated by ≥ 6 inches (150 mm).
- Frequency: ≤ 6 individual pores per linear foot (300 mm) of weld.

REJECTION:
- Any individual pore > 3/32 inch (2.4 mm) diameter — reject; grind and
  repair.
- Sum of pore diameters > 3/8 inch (9.5 mm) in any 1-inch weld length — reject.
- Crater pipe (أنبوب الحفرة): elongated shrinkage cavity at weld stop — reject;
  grind out and re-weld.
- Any surface porosity on pipe butt welds per API 1104: individual pore >
  1/8 inch (3.2 mm) OR more than 5 pores in any 12-inch (300 mm) weld
  length — reject.

CLAUSE 13 — UNDERCUT — DEPTH LIMITS PER STRUCTURAL APPLICATION
[Section: AWS B1.11 Clause 8.7; AWS D1.1 Clause 6.9.4]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Undercut (القطع الأسفلي / القطع تحت اللحام) is a groove melted into the base
metal adjacent to the weld toe (حافة اللحام) that is not refilled by weld metal,
leaving a sharp notch at the toe. Undercut is caused by excessive welding current,
incorrect electrode angle, excessive travel speed, or poor weaving technique. It
is one of the most dangerous surface discontinuities because the sharp notch at
the toe acts as a stress concentration site for fatigue cracking — particularly
critical in cyclic loading environments such as offshore structures, rotating
equipment skids, and pressure vessels in pulsating service. Undercut depth must
be measured with a calibrated depth gauge (مقياس العمق المعايَر) — visual
estimation alone is not acceptable for borderline cases. Both weld toes (inner
toe and outer toe on fillet welds; crown toe and root toe on groove welds) must
be checked.

ACCEPTANCE (per AWS D1.1 Clause 6.9.4):
- Primary structural members in tension (under tensile load): undercut depth
  ≤ 0.010 inch (0.25 mm) maximum.
- Secondary structural members and compression members: undercut depth ≤ 1/32
  inch (0.8 mm) maximum, provided length ≤ 2 inches (50 mm) in any 12-inch
  (300 mm) weld length.
- Pipeline welds per API 1104 Clause 9.3.9: undercut depth ≤ 1/32 inch
  (0.8 mm) and length ≤ 1 inch (25 mm) total in any 12-inch (300 mm).

REJECTION:
- Undercut depth > 0.010 inch (0.25 mm) on tension members — reject; repair
  by welding (deposit additional weld metal to fill the undercut groove).
- Undercut depth > 1/32 inch (0.8 mm) on any member — reject.
- Undercut running continuously along full weld toe for > 10% of weld length
  on any member — reject regardless of depth; indicates systemic welding
  technique problem; WPS or welder technique review required.

CLAUSE 14 — OVERLAP (COLD LAP) — REJECTION CRITERIA
[Section: AWS B1.11 Clause 8.8]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Overlap, also called cold lap (تداخل بارد) or rollover, is a protrusion of weld
metal beyond the weld toe that has not fused with the base metal — the molten
weld pool has rolled over without creating metallurgical bond to the adjacent
surface. Overlap creates a mechanical notch identical in stress-concentration
effect to undercut; additionally, the gap beneath the overlapping metal traps
moisture and corrosive media in oilfield service, creating a crevice corrosion
(تآكل الشقوق) site. Overlap is common in fillet welds made with high heat input
and slow travel speed — the pool "laps" over onto the base metal plate. On
horizontal fillet welds, the lower toe is particularly prone to overlap.
AWS B1.11 Clause 8.8 specifies that overlap is unacceptable in any amount —
zero tolerance — because the unfused interface beneath the rolled metal
constitutes a planar crack-like defect.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Zero overlap (تداخل صفري) on all weld toes — no protrusion of weld metal
  over the base metal surface without fusion.
- Weld toe must blend smoothly into the base metal with a concave or flush
  profile — no distinct "lip" or roll visible.

REJECTION:
- Any visible overlap at any weld toe, regardless of length or amount —
  immediate rejection; zero tolerance.
- Method of repair: grind out the overlap and re-inspect by VT; if underlying
  lack of fusion (قصور في الاندماج) is suspected, supplement with PT or MT.
- Repeated overlap on same welder's joints — welder technique review required;
  consider requalification test.

CLAUSE 15 — ARC STRIKES — IDENTIFICATION AND DISPOSITION
[Section: AWS B1.11 Clause 8.9; AWS D1.1 Clause 5.29]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Arc strikes (ضربات القوس الكهربائي) are localized areas of base metal that have
been melted or thermally affected by inadvertent arc contact outside the intended
weld joint. They are caused by accidental touching of the live electrode to the
base metal surface, by careless electrode positioning, or by poor work clamp
placement causing stray arcing. Arc strikes create small hardened zones
(مناطق مُقسّاة صغيرة) through rapid heating and quenching, which are
susceptible to hydrogen cracking (تشقق هيدروجيني) in hardenable steels and
fatigue cracking in all steels. In oilfield and pressure equipment fabrication,
arc strikes on pressure-retaining components (مكونات تحتفظ بالضغط) are
especially critical and may be mandatory rejection with engineering review.
AWS D1.1 Clause 5.29 prohibits arc strikes outside the weld area.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Zero arc strikes acceptable on pressure-retaining components, structural
  tension members, and any high-strength steel (SMYS > 65 ksi / 448 MPa).
- On non-critical secondary members only: engineer may accept a ground-flush
  arc strike with subsequent MT/PT to confirm no cracking — must be
  documented with NCR and engineering sign-off.

REJECTION:
- Any arc strike detected on pressure piping, vessels, or structural tension
  members — reject; NCR required; engineering disposition mandatory.
- Arc strike on high-strength steel or CRA (Corrosion Resistant Alloy) —
  reject; risk of HAZ cracking; grind to clean metal and perform MT or PT;
  if crack confirmed, full excavation and repair.
- Multiple arc strikes indicating poor workmanship control — stop fabrication;
  worker counseling and Foreman notification required.

CLAUSE 16 — WELD SPATTER — WHEN IS IT REJECTABLE
[Section: AWS B1.11 Clause 8.10]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Weld spatter (رذاذ اللحام) consists of metal droplets expelled from the weld
pool during welding that adhere to the base metal or weld surface outside the
weld bead. Spatter is inherent to certain processes (SMAW, FCAW, MIG short-
circuit) and is controlled — not eliminated — by proper parameter selection,
electrode condition, and shielding gas mixture. Spatter is generally considered
a cosmetic defect; however, in oilfield service it becomes rejectable in specific
circumstances: where it prevents proper NDE (covering areas to be PT/MT tested),
where it constitutes an arc strike risk (unfused spatter balls with HAZ beneath),
where painting systems require a clean surface (NACE/SSPC standards), or where
spatter accumulation could trap corrosion media in service. Per AWS B1.11
Clause 8.10, spatter must be removed before VT, PT, and MT are performed.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Isolated spatter particles that are easily brushed off and leave no arc-
  strike HAZ beneath — acceptable on non-critical secondary members if cleaned
  before NDE.
- Spatter density: ≤ 2 particles per cm² on any surface requiring paint
  coating per NACE/SSPC SP-6 (commercial blast) standards.
- No spatter within the weld bead area (weld crown surface) — spatter on crown
  may mask surface cracks.

REJECTION:
- Spatter that constitutes a de facto arc strike (ball > 2 mm diameter with
  visible HAZ discoloration beneath) — reject; treat as arc strike per
  Clause 15.
- Spatter on surfaces to be PT/MT tested that cannot be removed without
  damaging the substrate — surface preparation is non-compliant; clean before
  NDE proceeds.
- Heavy spatter fields (> 10 particles per cm²) on pressure equipment
  external surfaces — reject surface condition; power tool clean per SSPC-SP3
  minimum before painting.

CLAUSE 17 — ROOT CONCAVITY vs. BURN-THROUGH CRITERIA
[Section: AWS B1.11 Clause 8.11; API 1104 Clause 9.3.6 / 9.3.7]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Root concavity (تقعر الجذر) — also called suck-back or internal undercut — is a
condition where the root bead surface is recessed below the adjacent inside pipe
or plate surface, forming a concave profile. Burn-through (الاحتراق الكامل) is a
more severe condition where the root bead has collapsed entirely, leaving a hole
or a very thin, porous membrane with no structural integrity. Root concavity is
evaluated by examining the pipe internal root bead (تمريرة الجذر الداخلية)
directly (for bore access possible) or indirectly by RT film density, or UT
TOFD. Burn-through is visible as a dark spot on RT or as a physical hole.
AWS B1.11 requires that root conditions be assessed by the most reliable
available method — for pipeline field welds where bore access is impractical,
RT is the primary method; VT is used on accessible root surfaces of vessels and
large-bore fittings.

ACCEPTANCE (per API 1104 Clause 9.3.6):
- Root concavity: permitted if the resulting weld thickness (net section) is
  ≥ nominal pipe wall thickness — i.e., concavity depth ≤ weld reinforcement
  height on OD side (net: no thinner than base metal).
- Root concavity maximum depth (accessible surface, AWS D1.1 groove welds):
  ≤ 1/32 inch (0.8 mm).
- Root bead width visible on ID: minimum equal to root opening + 2 × 1/16
  inch (1.6 mm) each side — confirming side-wall fusion.

REJECTION:
- Root concavity that reduces net weld section below base metal wall thickness
  — reject; repair by back-weld from ID or temper-bead from OD per approved
  WPS.
- Any burn-through (visible hole or membrane < 0.5 mm thick) — reject; full
  excavation and repair required; do not fill over burn-through without
  proper ground-out.
- Root concavity > 1/32 inch (0.8 mm) depth on groove welds where ID surface
  is accessible — reject; back-weld repair.

CLAUSE 18 — FILLET WELD THROAT vs. LEG INSPECTION — MEASURING TOOLS
[Section: AWS B1.11 Clause 8.12 – 8.13]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

For fillet welds (لحام الزاوية), AWS B1.11 Clause 8.12 requires dimensional
verification of both the leg size (طول الساق) and — for design purposes — the
effective throat (الحلق الفعلي). The leg size is the distance from the root of
the joint to the weld toe, measured along each fusion face; both legs (vertical
and horizontal legs on a T-joint) must be checked. The theoretical throat is
0.707 × leg size for an equal-leg fillet weld; the effective (actual) throat is
less if the weld is concave. Convex fillet welds may appear oversized but if
their concavity is excessive (i.e., the throat is reduced despite larger legs),
they are deficient in structural strength. The standard tool for fillet weld
measurement is the fillet weld gauge set (طقم مقياس لحام الزاوية) — typically
the bridge cam gauge or the AWS fillet gauge; vernier calipers are used for
precise measurement in borderline cases.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Fillet leg size: ≥ required size per drawing (e.g., 8 mm, 10 mm, 12 mm).
- Undersize tolerance: ≤ 1/16 inch (1.6 mm) under specified leg for ≤ 10%
  of weld length (AWS D1.1 Clause 6.9.2.1).
- Effective throat: ≥ 0.707 × minimum specified leg size — confirmed by
  fillet gauge measurement on the flat face of the weld.
- Fillet gauge reading: use bridge cam gauge in "minimum" position — if gauge
  rides on the crown of a convex fillet, the actual leg may be acceptable;
  verify by measuring each leg independently with the straight-edge arm.

REJECTION:
- Leg size < specified – 1/16 inch (1.6 mm) for > 10% of weld length —
  reject; add weld pass; re-inspect.
- Effective throat < 0.707 × specified minimum leg — reject; excessive
  concavity reduces throat below structural requirement.
- Unequal legs where the smaller leg is undersized beyond tolerance — reject;
  applies to T-joints where the vertical member leg must meet minimum
  independently.

CLAUSE 19 — WELD PROFILE — CONVEXITY AND CONCAVITY LIMITS
[Section: AWS B1.11 Clause 8.14; AWS D1.1 Clause 5.24]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

AWS B1.11 Clause 8.14 addresses the overall weld bead profile (مظهر ومقطع
اللحام) requirements for both fillet and groove welds. Excessive convexity
(تحدب مفرط) of fillet welds stores residual stress at the toes and creates a
stress-riser geometry that promotes toe cracking under fatigue loading. Excessive
concavity (تقعر مفرط) of fillet welds reduces the effective throat below the
required value, directly compromising joint strength. For groove welds, the
crown profile must be slightly convex (reinforcement) but not excessively so —
a high crown acts as a notch at the weld toes. The profile must be smooth and
consistent along the weld length — abrupt humps (تقوسات مفاجئة), valleys, and
irregular surface patterns indicate parameter inconsistency and must be
investigated. Profile is assessed visually with the naked eye first; borderline
cases are measured with a weld reinforcement gauge or bridge cam gauge.

ACCEPTANCE (per AWS D1.1 Clause 5.24):
- Fillet weld convexity: ≤ 1/16 inch + 0.10 × weld size (leg) or maximum
  1/8 inch (3.2 mm), whichever is less.
  Example: 10 mm leg → max convexity = 1 + 1.0 = 2.0 mm (use 3.2 mm cap).
- Groove weld crown reinforcement (convexity): ≤ 3/16 inch (4.8 mm) for any
  joint thickness; must not be abrupt or peaked.
- Fillet weld concavity: weld toes must be at or above the theoretical throat
  plane; no negative throat permitted.
- Groove weld surface: smooth and regular; no abrupt surface irregularities
  > 1/16 inch (1.6 mm) in 1 inch (25 mm) measured length.

REJECTION:
- Fillet convexity > 1/8 inch (3.2 mm) — reject; grind to profile within
  limits; re-inspect.
- Fillet concavity resulting in throat < 0.707 × minimum leg — reject; add
  weld metal; re-inspect.
- Groove weld crown reinforcement > 3/16 inch (4.8 mm) or peaked / sharp
  crown profile — reject; grind to profile; re-inspect.
- Irregular profile with surface valleys (dips) exposing possible incomplete
  fill — reject; additional fill pass required; re-inspect.

CLAUSE 20 — VT OF HEAT-AFFECTED ZONE (HAZ) — WHAT TO LOOK FOR
[Section: AWS B1.11 Clause 8.15]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The heat-affected zone (منطقة التأثر الحراري — HAZ) is the base metal region
immediately adjacent to the weld fusion boundary that has been thermally altered
by the welding heat without melting. AWS B1.11 Clause 8.15 requires that VT
extend beyond the weld bead to include HAZ inspection — minimum 1 inch (25 mm)
each side of the weld toes. Critical HAZ discontinuities detectable by VT
include: HAZ cracks (especially cold / hydrogen cracks in high-strength or
high-carbon equivalent steels), lamellar tearing (تشقق الصفائح) on thick plates
with through-thickness stress, base metal laminations exposed at the HAZ, and
HAZ corrosion pitting (تآكل HAZ) in completed welds being inspected during
in-service inspection. The HAZ on carbon steel is visually identifiable by a
distinct blue/golden heat tint band (discoloration) adjacent to the weld bead
— inspect this entire band systematically. In sour service (H₂S environment —
بيئة كبريتيد الهيدروجين), HAZ cracking (Sulfide Stress Cracking — SSC) must be
specifically investigated.

ACCEPTANCE:
- HAZ surface: free of cracks, tears, pitting, and linear indications within
  1 inch (25 mm) of each weld toe.
- No lamellar tearing visible as stepped, staircase-pattern separation in
  plate through-thickness direction — particularly check corner joints and
  T-joints on thick plate (t > 25 mm).
- HAZ discoloration (heat tint): acceptable if confined to within 1 inch of
  weld toe; extensive discoloration > 3 inches suggests excessive heat input —
  document and review against WPS.

REJECTION:
- Any crack in HAZ — zero tolerance; immediate rejection; MT or PT required
  to define extent; NCR and engineering hold.
- Lamellar tearing detected in HAZ or base metal within 3 inches of weld —
  reject; engineering evaluation required; UT through-thickness testing
  (lamination scan) must be performed.
- HAZ pitting or crevice corrosion detected on in-service weld inspection —
  reject for continued service; engineering fitness-for-service (FFS)
  assessment required per API 579.

CLAUSE 21 — DISTORTION AND WARPING ASSESSMENT — DIMENSIONAL DEVIATION
[Section: AWS B1.11 Clause 9.1 – 9.3]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

AWS B1.11 Clause 9.1 requires that post-weld dimensional conformance
(المطابقة الأبعادية بعد اللحام) be verified as part of the VT scope, because
welding-induced distortion (تشوه اللحام) and warping (الالتواء) can render a
structurally sound weld unacceptable for its intended fit and function. Distortion
types include: angular distortion (تشوه زاوي) of T-joints and butt welds,
longitudinal shrinkage (الانكماش الطولي) causing bowing, transverse shrinkage
(الانكماش العرضي) reducing joint width, and rotational distortion. The inspector
uses straight edges (مسطرة مستقيمة), levels (ميزان الاستواء), feeler gauges,
and dimensional survey measurements to quantify distortion. Acceptance limits
for distortion are set by the engineering drawing dimensional tolerances, the
applicable construction code, or the fabrication specification — AWS B1.11
does not itself set universal distortion limits; the drawing governs.

ACCEPTANCE:
- All fabricated dimensions within drawing tolerances — typically ±3 mm for
  structural steel fabrication per AWS D1.1 and ±1.5 mm for pressure piping
  spools per ASME B31.3.
- Angular distortion of T-joints: ≤ 1 degree from perpendicular (90°) unless
  drawing specifies tighter tolerance.
- Straightness of beams/girders: ≤ L/1000 (L = member length) bow or sweep
  — verify with taut string line or laser level.
- Pipe spool alignment (flange face squareness): ≤ 1.5 mm across flange
  diameter for all spool sizes.

REJECTION:
- Any dimension outside drawing tolerance — reject fabrication; issue NCR;
  evaluate for cold correction (ضبط بارد), heat straightening (تقويم حراري),
  or rework.
- Angular distortion > 1 degree on structural T-joints without drawing
  allowance — reject; heat straightening or cut-and-refit required.
- Pipe spool flange face squareness > 1.5 mm — reject; spool is
  non-conforming; rework required; retest after rework.
- Distortion that prevents mating of adjacent components at next assembly
  stage — immediate hold; engineering disposition.

CLAUSE 22 — DOCUMENTATION — MINIMUM CONTENT OF VT REPORT
[Section: AWS B1.11 Clause 10.1 – 10.5]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

AWS B1.11 Clause 10.1 through 10.5 establishes the minimum documentation
requirements for a visual examination report (تقرير الفحص البصري). A VT report
is the legal record of the examination and must be sufficient to: identify the
weld uniquely, establish the qualifications of the examiner, record the
conditions of examination, document all findings (accept and reject), and provide
traceability for quality system audits. Incomplete VT reports are a major
nonconformance finding in ISO 3834, API Q1, and ASME Section III / Section VIII
quality audits. In the Egypt/MENA oilfield environment, VT reports must be
retained for the duration of the facility design life plus 7 years (per Egyptian
Oil Ministry quality regulations for safety-critical pressure equipment), or as
specified by the COMPANY quality plan. Electronic records are acceptable if
tamper-proof and backed up.

ACCEPTANCE — Minimum required data fields on every VT report:
1. Project name, contract number, and work order number
2. Weld identification number (رقم وصلة اللحام) — unique per joint
3. Weld joint type (butt, fillet, T-joint, corner) and process (SMAW, FCAW, TIG)
4. Base material specification and thickness
5. Inspector name, certification type, certificate number, and expiry date
6. Vision test date and result (J2 at 12 inches — Pass)
7. Date and time of examination
8. Stage of VT (pre-weld / in-process / post-weld)
9. Lighting conditions (lux meter reading and meter serial number / cal date)
10. Viewing distance and angle used
11. Surface condition at time of examination (cleaned / as-welded)
12. Each discontinuity found: type, location (from datum), length/depth
13. Disposition: ACCEPT (مقبول) / REJECT (مرفوض) / REPAIR REQUIRED (يحتاج إصلاح)
14. Reference code and clause for each acceptance/rejection decision
15. Inspector signature and date
16. QA/QC supervisor countersignature for rejected welds

REJECTION — Report is non-conforming (يُعاد تحريره) if:
- Inspector certification number or expiry date is missing — report invalid.
- Weld ID is not unique or is missing — report is untraceably vague; NCR.
- Disposition (Accept/Reject) is not stated — incomplete; report on hold.
- Inspector signature is absent — report has no legal standing; invalid.
- Lighting or vision test data absent — examination conditions unverified;
  inspection results suspect; must repeat or obtain documented justification.
