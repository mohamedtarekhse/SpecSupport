CLAUSE T-210 — SCOPE AND GENERAL REQUIREMENTS
(نطاق الفحص بالأشعة ومتطلباته العامة)

T-210.1 SCOPE
Radiographic Examination (RT — الفحص بالأشعة السينية أو أشعة جاما) per ASME Section V
Article 2 covers the radiographic examination of metallic and nonmetallic materials
using X-ray or gamma-ray sources. The article establishes minimum requirements for
technique, equipment, film, processing, image quality, and record retention. It applies
wherever referenced by a Construction Code (كود الإنشاء) such as ASME Section VIII,
ASME B31.3, or API 1104. The inspector must confirm which Construction Code is
invoked before commencing examination, because acceptance criteria are always taken
from the Construction Code — not from Article 2 itself.

T-210.2 WRITTEN PROCEDURE REQUIREMENT
A written RT procedure (إجراء مكتوب) shall be established, qualified, and followed for
all radiographic examinations. The procedure must be demonstrated to produce acceptable
radiographs on a calibration or mockup assembly if required by the referencing Code.
Essential variables, when changed beyond the documented limits, require re-qualification
of the procedure. Non-essential variables may be changed without re-qualification
provided the change is documented. The procedure document number, revision, and date
shall be recorded on the examination report.

T-210.3 PERSONNEL QUALIFICATION
All personnel performing and interpreting radiographic examinations shall be qualified
and certified in accordance with their employer's written practice, which shall conform
to SNT-TC-1A (latest edition referenced by the Construction Code) or ASNT CP-189.
Interpretation of radiographs (قراءة وتفسير الأفلام الإشعاعية) shall be performed by
Level II or Level III certified RT personnel. Level I personnel may operate equipment
under the direct supervision of a Level II or Level III. Certifications shall be
current and on file, and copies shall be available to the Authorized Inspector (AI)
upon request.

T-210.4 APPLICABLE MATERIAL FORMS
Article 2 applies to welds (لحامات), castings (مسبوكات), forgings (مطروقات), and
plate material. Pipe and tube welds are covered by specific technique paragraphs
(T-271 through T-274). The article does not cover real-time radiography (RTR) or
computed radiography (CR) and digital detector array (DDA) systems — those are
covered by Articles 2 and Mandatory Appendices II, III, and IV respectively, and
require additional procedure requirements if applied.

T-210.5 SURFACE PREPARATION
Surfaces to be radiographed (السطح المراد فحصه بالأشعة) shall be free of irregularities
that could mask or be confused with discontinuities. Weld reinforcement and backing
rings, if present, shall be documented. Surface preparation (تجهيز السطح) may be by
mechanical grinding or other suitable means. No filler compound or masking material
shall be applied to the weld surface unless specifically permitted by the Construction
Code.

ACCEPTANCE: Written procedure exists, is Rev-controlled, and personnel hold current
Level II or III certification. Surface condition suitable for interpretation.
REJECTION: No written procedure; personnel uncertified or expired certification;
surface irregularities that prevent reliable interpretation of the radiograph.

CLAUSE T-221 — FILM TYPE SELECTION (CLASS I vs CLASS II)
(اختيار نوع الفيلم الإشعاعي — الفئة الأولى مقابل الفئة الثانية)

T-221.1 FILM CLASSIFICATION
ASME Section V recognizes two classes of industrial radiographic film:
  • Class I (الفئة الأولى) — Fine-grain, high-contrast film. Examples: Kodak T-MAT D,
    Agfa D4, FUJI IX80. Provides the highest image quality and is required for Code
    work on pressure-containing welds unless the referencing Code specifically permits
    Class II.
  • Class II (الفئة الثانية) — Coarser grain, lower contrast, but higher speed. Examples:
    Kodak T-MAT G, Agfa D7, FUJI IX100. Permitted only when specifically allowed by
    the applicable Construction Code and engineering authority.

T-221.2 SELECTION CRITERIA
The film class shall be selected based on:
  (a) Required image quality (IQI sensitivity — حساسية مؤشر جودة الصورة).
  (b) Radiation source energy and exposure geometry.
  (c) Requirements of the Construction Code.
Film class shall be documented in the written RT procedure. Mixing film classes
within a single exposure is not permitted.

T-221.3 FILM STORAGE AND HANDLING
Unexposed film (الفيلم غير المعرض) shall be stored in a cool, dry location away from
radiation sources, chemical fumes, and direct sunlight. Storage temperature shall not
exceed 24°C (75°F) and relative humidity shall be 30–60%. Film shall be used prior
to its expiration date. Outdated film shall not be used for Code examination.
Film boxes shall not be stored horizontally under heavy loads to prevent pressure fog.

T-221.4 FILM CASSETTES AND HOLDERS
Film cassettes (كاسيتات الفيلم) shall be light-tight and shall hold the film flat
against the intensifying screens with intimate contact. Poor screen-to-film contact
causes geometric unsharpness and image blur — which is a cause for rejection.
Lead cassettes or flexible film holders may be used, provided contact is verified.

ACCEPTANCE: Class I film used for all Code pressure-containing weld RT unless
Construction Code explicitly permits Class II. Film within expiration date. Storage
conditions documented and within limits.
REJECTION: Class II film used where Class I is required. Film past expiration date.
Film fogged from improper storage (background density > 0.05 H&D units above base
plus fog). Evidence of pressure marks, scratches, or light leaks on processed film.

CLAUSE T-222 — INTENSIFYING SCREENS
(شاشات التكثيف / شاشات الرصاص)

T-222.1 TYPES PERMITTED
Two types of intensifying screens (شاشات التكثيف) are used in industrial RT:
  (a) Lead screens (شاشات الرصاص) — Most common in industrial work. Front and back
      lead screens reduce scatter radiation and improve image contrast. Lead screen
      thickness for X-ray up to 150 kV: front screen 0.010 in. (0.25 mm) min;
      back screen 0.010 in. (0.25 mm) min. For higher energies and Ir-192/Co-60:
      front screen 0.010–0.020 in., back screen 0.020 in. min.
  (b) Fluorescent screens — NOT permitted under ASME Section V Article 2 for Code
      radiography because they reduce geometric sharpness (unsharpness — ضبابية هندسية).

T-222.2 SCREEN CONDITION
Intensifying screens shall be free of:
  - Scratches (خدوش)
  - Dents (حفر أو تشوهات)
  - Oxidation or tarnish (أكسدة)
  - Foreign matter between screen and film
Damaged screens shall be removed from service. Screen condition shall be verified
prior to each use and shall be documented on the examination record.

T-222.3 SCATTER RADIATION CONTROL
A lead letter "B" (2 in. high, 1/4 in. thick minimum) shall be attached to the back
of each film cassette to check for back-scatter radiation. If the letter "B" appears
as a lighter image (light "B") on the processed film, it indicates excessive back
scatter — the exposure is rejected and must be repeated with additional shielding.
If the "B" appears darker or is not visible, back-scatter is acceptable.

T-222.4 SCREEN-TO-FILM CONTACT
Uniform, intimate contact between lead screens and film shall be maintained throughout
the exposure. Air gaps caused by warped cassettes or debris cause localized blur.
Contact shall be verified by bending test or vacuum-cassette pressure test prior to
use on Code work.

ACCEPTANCE: Lead screens used; screen thickness meets minimum per source energy;
screens free of damage; "B" not visible or appears dark on processed film; intimate
screen-film contact confirmed.
REJECTION: Fluorescent screens used. Damaged screens in service. Light "B" (back
scatter) appears on processed film — exposure void, must repeat. Evidence of poor
contact causing unacceptable blur in the region of interest.

CLAUSE T-223 — RADIATION SOURCES (X-RAY vs GAMMA-RAY)
(مصادر الإشعاع — الأشعة السينية مقابل أشعة جاما)

T-223.1 APPROVED RADIATION SOURCES
ASME Section V Article 2 permits the following radiation sources:
  (a) X-ray machines (أجهزة الأشعة السينية) — Energies up to 1,000 kV (1 MeV) for
      conventional RT; higher energies (linac/betatron) for thick materials.
  (b) Iridium-192 (Ir-192 — إيريديوم 192) — Gamma energy ~0.31–0.61 MeV; suitable
      for steel thickness 20–90 mm (3/4 in. to 3-1/2 in.); most common gamma source
      in Egyptian and MENA oilfield practice.
  (c) Cobalt-60 (Co-60 — كوبالت 60) — Gamma energy ~1.17–1.33 MeV; suitable for
      steel thickness 50–175 mm (2 in. to 7 in.); used for heavy-wall vessels and
      reactors.
  (d) Selenium-75 (Se-75) — Gamma energy ~0.27 MeV; suitable for thin-wall pipe
      10–40 mm; produces finer-grain images than Ir-192 on thin material.
  (e) Ytterbium-169 (Yb-169) — Very low energy; for very thin sections 2–15 mm.

T-223.2 SOURCE SIZE AND ACTIVITY
Source size (حجم المصدر / البقعة المضيئة) — also called focal spot size for X-ray —
directly affects geometric unsharpness (Ug). Smaller source = less Ug = sharper image.
Source activity (نشاط المصدر — measured in TBq or Ci) determines exposure time.
Source dimensions (effective focal spot — البقعة الفعلية) shall be as specified in
the RT procedure and shall be confirmed from source manufacturer's certificate or
X-ray machine data plate.

T-223.3 TECHNIQUE SELECTION BY MATERIAL THICKNESS
Recommended source selection by nominal material thickness (steel):
  • Up to 3 mm (1/8 in.):    X-ray preferred; Yb-169
  • 3–20 mm:                  X-ray (100–200 kV); Se-75
  • 20–90 mm:                 X-ray (200–400 kV); Ir-192
  • 50–175 mm:                X-ray (MeV); Co-60
  • >175 mm:                  Linac / Betatron
Using a source with energy too high for the material thickness reduces image
contrast and may result in failure to meet IQI sensitivity requirements.

T-223.4 SOURCE CERTIFICATION
Each radioactive source (مصدر إشعاعي) shall have a current source certificate (شهادة
المصدر) traceable to the manufacturer, showing:
  - Isotope identification
  - Source size (nominal and effective focal spot dimensions, both directions)
  - Activity at reference date
  - Wipe test results (leak test — اختبار التسرب) current within 6 months
Sources with expired leak tests shall not be used until re-tested and passed.

ACCEPTANCE: Source type appropriate for material thickness; source size documented
in procedure; leak test current (within 6 months for gamma sources); focal spot
size confirmed from manufacturer's data.
REJECTION: Source type not appropriate for thickness range (e.g., Co-60 on thin
wall ≤ 10 mm causing excessive scatter and loss of sensitivity); leak test expired;
source size not documented; X-ray machine voltage exceeding equipment rated capacity.

CLAUSE T-224 — GEOMETRIC UNSHARPNESS (Ug) — CALCULATION AND LIMITS
(الضبابية الهندسية — الحساب والحدود المسموح بها)

T-224.1 DEFINITION
Geometric unsharpness (Ug — الضبابية الهندسية) is the penumbra (zone of partial shadow)
created at the edge of a discontinuity image due to the finite size of the radiation
source. Smaller source size and longer source-to-object distance reduce Ug and improve
image sharpness. Ug is one of the most critical parameters in RT quality control.

T-224.2 FORMULA
Ug = (F × t) / D

Where:
  F = Source size (effective focal spot dimension, mm or in.)
  t = Object-to-film distance (OFD — المسافة من الجسم إلى الفيلم), mm or in.
      (distance from source-side surface of the object to the film)
  D = Source-to-object distance (SOD — المسافة من المصدر إلى الجسم), mm or in.
      (distance from source to source-side surface of the object)

Units: F, t, and D must be in the same units (all mm or all inches).
Ug result is in the same units as F and t.

T-224.3 MAXIMUM PERMITTED Ug VALUES (ASME V T-274 Table T-274)
Material thickness (t_weld — سُمك المادة عند موضع اللحام):

  t_weld ≤ 3/4 in. (19 mm):       Ug max = 0.020 in. (0.51 mm)
  t_weld > 3/4 in. to 2 in.       Ug max = 0.030 in. (0.76 mm)
    (19 mm to 50 mm):
  t_weld > 2 in. to 3-1/2 in.     Ug max = 0.040 in. (1.02 mm)
    (50 mm to 88.9 mm):
  t_weld > 3-1/2 in. (88.9 mm):   Ug max = 0.070 in. (1.78 mm)

NOTE: For nozzle welds and other complex geometries, the controlling thickness is
the nominal thickness of the thicker member at the weld.

T-224.4 VERIFICATION
Ug shall be calculated for each technique (each combination of source, geometry,
and material thickness) and the result shall be recorded in the RT procedure and
on the examination report. If geometry changes during a job (different pipe
schedules, different nozzle configurations), Ug shall be recalculated and
re-documented. The inspector shall verify that SFD and OFD measurements used in
the calculation are taken accurately in the field.

ACCEPTANCE: Calculated Ug ≤ maximum permitted value for the applicable thickness
range, as tabulated above. Ug calculation and inputs documented in procedure and
on each examination report.
REJECTION: Calculated Ug exceeds maximum permitted value for the applicable
thickness range. Ug not calculated or not documented. SFD/OFD measurements
inconsistent with as-built geometry.

CLAUSE T-225 — SOURCE-TO-FILM DISTANCE (SFD) / FOCUS-TO-FILM DISTANCE (FFD)
(مسافة المصدر إلى الفيلم / مسافة البؤرة إلى الفيلم)

T-225.1 DEFINITION
Source-to-Film Distance (SFD), also called Focus-to-Film Distance (FFD) for X-ray
(مسافة البؤرة إلى الفيلم), is the total distance from the radiation source (focal
spot or gamma source capsule) to the film plane. SFD is the sum of:
  SFD = SOD + OFD
Where SOD = source-to-object distance and OFD = object-to-film distance.
Increasing SFD improves geometric sharpness (reduces Ug) but requires longer
exposure times to maintain adequate film density.

T-225.2 MINIMUM SFD — FORMULA-BASED CONTROL
Article 2 does not mandate a single minimum SFD, but instead uses Ug limits
(T-224) to control geometry. The minimum SFD must be whatever distance satisfies:
  D_min = (F × t) / Ug_max

Where Ug_max is the maximum permitted geometric unsharpness from Table T-274 for
the applicable thickness.

Example: For F = 3 mm source, t (OFD) = 15 mm, material thickness ≤ 19 mm (Ug_max = 0.51 mm):
  D_min = (3 × 15) / 0.51 = 88 mm SOD minimum
  SFD_min = 88 + 15 = 103 mm (approximately 4 inches minimum)

T-225.3 FIELD MEASUREMENT
SFD shall be physically measured in the field using a calibrated tape measure or
fixture — not estimated. For circumferential pipe shots with the source inside
(panoramic exposure — التصوير البانورامي), SFD is measured from the source centerline
to the outer surface of the pipe wall (OFD = wall thickness). Variance in pipe
roundness shall be accounted for when calculating OFD at multiple film positions.

T-225.4 DOCUMENTATION
The SFD (or SOD and OFD individually) shall be recorded on the RT examination
report for every exposure. When the SFD is changed from the procedure value (e.g.,
because of access limitations), the inspector shall:
  (a) Recalculate Ug to confirm compliance.
  (b) Recalculate exposure time or mA-min to maintain density.
  (c) Document the change as a procedure variable change.

ACCEPTANCE: SFD results in Ug ≤ limit for applicable thickness. SFD measured and
documented for each exposure. Recalculation performed and documented when SFD
changes from procedure.
REJECTION: SFD results in Ug exceeding the permitted limit. SFD not measured but
estimated. No documentation of SFD in the examination record.

CLAUSE T-230 — FILM PROCESSING AND DARKROOM REQUIREMENTS
(معالجة الفيلم ومتطلبات غرفة التحميض)

T-230.1 PROCESSING METHOD
Film shall be processed (تحميض الفيلم) using either:
  (a) Manual (hand) processing (التحميض اليدوي) — Developer, stop bath, fixer, wash,
      and drying in a controlled darkroom (غرفة مظلمة).
  (b) Automatic processing (معالجة أوتوماتيكية) — Roller-transport processor with
      temperature-controlled chemical tanks; most common on modern site labs.
The processing method shall be documented in the RT procedure.

T-230.2 DEVELOPER TEMPERATURE AND TIME
Manual processing:
  - Developer temperature: 18–24°C (65–75°F). Optimal: 20°C (68°F).
  - Development time at 20°C: per film manufacturer's recommendation, typically
    5–8 minutes for most Class I industrial films.
  - Time-temperature relationship: Higher temperature = shorter time. Deviations
    from manufacturer's chart result in under- or over-development.
  - Under-development: Low density, low contrast — may cause rejection for density.
  - Over-development: High fog, high base density — may cause rejection for density.

T-230.3 CHEMICAL REPLENISHMENT AND REPLACEMENT
Developer and fixer chemicals (مواد التحميض) shall be replenished or replaced per
the chemical supplier's recommendations. Chemical exhaustion causes:
  - Reduced image density (under-developed appearance)
  - Loss of contrast
  - Increased development time requirements
Chemical activity shall be monitored using a step-wedge sensitometric strip
(شريط الحساسية) daily when the darkroom is in active use.

T-230.4 WASH AND DRYING
Films shall be washed in running water for a minimum of 20–30 minutes after fixing
(per manufacturer) to remove residual fixer (hypo). Residual fixer causes long-term
image deterioration (yellow-brown stain — بقع صفراء). Rapid fixers and wash aids
(hypo eliminators) may reduce wash time. Films shall be dried in a dust-free
environment and shall not be viewed until completely dry and cool.

T-230.5 DARKROOM SAFELIGHTS AND CLEANLINESS
The darkroom (غرفة التحميض المظلمة) shall be light-tight. Safelights shall use filters
appropriate for the film type (green or amber — as specified by film manufacturer).
Exposed film shall not be loaded or unloaded except under safelight conditions.
Darkroom surfaces, tanks, and hangers shall be cleaned regularly to prevent chemical
contamination of films.

T-230.6 SENSITOMETRIC CONTROL
A sensitometric quality control (QC) program shall be in place when automatic
processors are used. Daily step-wedge strips shall be processed and evaluated for:
  - Speed index (±0.15 OD from control)
  - Contrast index (±0.15 OD from control)
  - Base + fog (≤ 0.30 OD above unexposed base)
If QC strips fall outside control limits, the processor shall be taken out of
service and corrected before processing Code radiographs.

ACCEPTANCE: Processing by documented method; developer at 18–24°C; manual time
per manufacturer chart; QC sensitometric strips within ±0.15 OD of control;
base + fog ≤ 0.30 OD; films fully washed and dried.
REJECTION: Processing outside temperature/time limits; base + fog > 0.30 OD on
processed film; chemical exhaustion causing non-compliant density; light leaks in
darkroom causing film fog; films not fully dry when viewed.

CLAUSE T-241 — IMAGE QUALITY INDICATOR (IQI) — HOLE-TYPE AND WIRE-TYPE SELECTION
(مؤشر جودة الصورة — اختيار نوع الثقب أو السلك)

T-241.1 PURPOSE OF IQI
The Image Quality Indicator (IQI — مؤشر جودة الصورة), also called a penetrameter
(بينيترامتر), is placed on the object being radiographed to provide a direct visual
indication of the quality (sensitivity — الحساسية) of the radiographic technique.
The IQI does NOT measure the minimum detectable flaw size — it measures technique
quality. Its visibility on the radiograph confirms that the technique meets minimum
ASME sensitivity requirements.

T-241.2 HOLE-TYPE (ASTM E1025) — PLAQUE IQI
Hole-type IQIs (مؤشر الثقب / البلاك بينيترامتر) are flat metal plates with:
  - Three drilled holes: 1T, 2T, and 4T diameter
    (T = nominal plaque thickness in inches)
  - Made from the same material group as the object being radiographed
    (steel IQI for steel welds; aluminum IQI for aluminum welds, etc.)
IQI designation number = IQI thickness × 1000 (in inches). Example: IQI #10 = 0.010 in. thick.
Plaque IQI selection based on nominal single-wall thickness of weld:

  Material Thickness (in.)   |  IQI Designation Number
  Up to 0.25                 |  5
  0.26 – 0.375               |  7
  0.376 – 0.50               |  10
  0.51 – 0.75                |  12
  0.76 – 1.00                |  15
  1.01 – 1.50                |  17
  1.51 – 2.00                |  20
  2.01 – 2.50                |  25
  2.51 – 3.00                |  30
  3.01 – 4.00                |  35
  4.01 – 6.00                |  40
  6.01 – 8.00                |  50
(Per ASME Section V Table T-276)

T-241.3 WIRE-TYPE (ASTM E747) — WIRE IQI
Wire-type IQIs (مؤشر السلك) consist of 6 wires of increasing diameter encased in
a plastic envelope. There are four standard wire sets (A, B, C, D):
  Wire Set A: Wire diameters 0.0032–0.010 in. (0.08–0.25 mm)  — for thin materials
  Wire Set B: Wire diameters 0.010–0.032 in. (0.25–0.81 mm)   — most common
  Wire Set C: Wire diameters 0.032–0.10 in. (0.81–2.54 mm)    — for thick materials
  Wire Set D: Wire diameters 0.10–0.32 in. (2.54–8.13 mm)     — for very thick
Wire IQI material shall match the object material group. Wire IQIs are preferred
for curved surfaces (pipe welds — لحامات الأنابيب) where flat plaques cannot lie flat.

T-241.4 MATERIAL GROUPING FOR IQI
IQI material shall be selected from the same material group as the part:
  Group 1: Carbon and low-alloy steel (الفولاذ الكربوني)
  Group 2: Austenitic stainless steel (الفولاذ المقاوم للصدأ الأوستنيتي)
  Group 3: Aluminum alloys (سبائك الألومنيوم)
  Group 4: Titanium alloys
  Group 5: Nickel alloys
  Group 6: Copper and alloys
  Group 7: Zirconium alloys

ACCEPTANCE: IQI type (hole or wire) documented in procedure; IQI material matches
object material group; correct IQI designation selected from Table T-276 for the
applicable wall thickness; IQI within calibration/certification traceability.
REJECTION: Wrong IQI material group used (e.g., aluminum IQI on steel); wrong IQI
designation number (too thick or too thin for the material thickness range); IQI
without traceable manufacturer identification.

CLAUSE T-242 — IQI PLACEMENT (SOURCE-SIDE vs FILM-SIDE)
(وضع مؤشر جودة الصورة — جانب المصدر مقابل جانب الفيلم)

T-242.1 PREFERRED PLACEMENT — SOURCE SIDE
IQIs shall be placed on the source side (جانب المصدر) of the part being examined,
in contact with the weld or object surface. Source-side placement gives the most
conservative (most demanding) sensitivity result because the IQI image has
maximum penumbra. The IQI shall be placed on the weld or adjacent to the weld
(within 1/4 T of the weld edge or on the weld crown — on the source side).

T-242.2 ALTERNATIVE PLACEMENT — FILM SIDE
Film-side placement (جانب الفيلم) is permitted when source-side placement is
physically impossible (e.g., inside pipe welds where access is blocked, or
double-wall techniques). When film-side IQI placement is used:
  (a) A lead letter "F" shall be placed near the IQI on the film side.
  (b) The "F" shall appear on the processed radiograph.
  (c) The required IQI sensitivity shall be one IQI number or wire size finer
      (more sensitive) than the standard source-side requirement, unless a
      technique demonstration was performed showing equivalence.

T-242.3 NUMBER OF IQIs PER RADIOGRAPH
  - Single radiograph (single film placement): 1 IQI per image.
  - Panoramic (360° single exposure — التصوير البانورامي الكامل): At minimum, IQIs
    shall be placed at 120° intervals (3 IQIs minimum around the circumference)
    to verify sensitivity around the full exposure.
  - Film strips covering one weld in multiple shots: 1 IQI per film position.

T-242.4 IQI LOCATION ON THE IMAGE
The IQI image shall appear within the area of interest on the processed radiograph.
The IQI shall not be placed over the weld unless the weld crown has been flush-
ground, as the crown height will produce a false density differential.
For fillet welds and nozzle welds, the IQI shall be placed on a shim (رقاقة فاصلة)
of the same material and of thickness equal to the weld reinforcement, so that the
IQI sits at the same nominal elevation as the weld surface.

T-242.5 IQI ORIENTATION
Hole-type IQI: Placed with the largest hole (4T) nearest the weld, away from
the edge of the part.
Wire-type IQI: Wires oriented perpendicular to the weld axis (عمودي على محور اللحام)
for best sensitivity to longitudinal defects.

ACCEPTANCE: IQI on source side unless access impossible; "F" marker present and
visible on radiograph when film-side placement used; minimum number of IQIs present
per radiograph area; IQI within area of interest; correct orientation.
REJECTION: IQI missing from radiograph; "F" designation absent when film-side
placement was used; IQI image outside area of interest; IQI placed on opposite side
from required without documentation or "F" marking.

CLAUSE T-243 — IQI SENSITIVITY REQUIREMENTS (2-2T HOLE OR DESIGNATED WIRE)
(متطلبات حساسية مؤشر جودة الصورة — ثقب 2-2T أو السلك المحدد)

T-243.1 REQUIRED SENSITIVITY — HOLE-TYPE IQI
For hole-type (plaque) IQIs, the minimum required sensitivity is:
  Visibility of the 2T hole (ثقب 2T) in the correct IQI designation.
  This means: on the processed radiograph, the 2T hole (whose diameter = 2 × IQI
  thickness) must be visible as a distinct circular image within the IQI body.
  This is the "2-2T" requirement — IQI #X with the 2T hole visible.
  The 1T hole is NOT required to be visible (it is the "quality check" extra).

T-243.2 REQUIRED SENSITIVITY — WIRE-TYPE IQI
For wire-type IQIs, the essential wire (السلك الأساسي) must be visible on the
processed radiograph. The required essential wire number for each material
thickness range is as specified in ASME Section V Table T-276. Example (steel):
  t ≤ 0.25 in.:      Wire #5 (0.008 in. / 0.20 mm)
  0.26–0.375 in.:    Wire #6 (0.010 in. / 0.25 mm)
  0.376–0.50 in.:    Wire #7 (0.013 in. / 0.33 mm)
  0.51–0.75 in.:     Wire #8 (0.016 in. / 0.40 mm)
  0.76–1.00 in.:     Wire #9 (0.020 in. / 0.51 mm)
  1.01–1.50 in.:     Wire #10 (0.025 in. / 0.64 mm)
  1.51–2.00 in.:     Wire #11 (0.032 in. / 0.81 mm)
  2.01–2.50 in.:     Wire #12 (0.040 in. / 1.02 mm)
  2.51–4.00 in.:     Wire #13 (0.050 in. / 1.27 mm)
(Consult Table T-276 for exact wire requirements per material and thickness.)

T-243.3 VERIFICATION ON RADIOGRAPH
The interpreter (مفتش القراءة) shall confirm:
  (a) The correct IQI designation is used for the material thickness.
  (b) The required 2T hole or essential wire is visible on the radiograph image.
  (c) The IQI image is within the area of interest.
  (d) The image quality (density, contrast) is sufficient to see the IQI clearly.
If the required hole or wire is not visible, the radiograph fails sensitivity
requirements and the weld must be re-radiographed.

T-243.4 ADDITIONAL THICKNESS ALLOWANCE (SHIM)
When the IQI is placed on a shim adjacent to a weld with reinforcement, the shim
thickness shall equal the actual weld reinforcement (crown height — ارتفاع التدعيم).
The IQI designation shall be selected based on:
  Nominal single-wall thickness + allowable weld reinforcement height.
This ensures the IQI sensitivity reflects the actual radiographic thickness.

ACCEPTANCE: 2T hole visible (plaque) or essential wire visible (wire IQI) on
processed radiograph within the area of interest. Correct IQI designation for
applicable thickness. IQI shim used where required.
REJECTION: 2T hole NOT visible on processed radiograph. Essential wire NOT visible.
Wrong IQI designation used (too heavy — does not meet sensitivity). IQI image
outside area of interest or obscured by weld image.

CLAUSE T-260 — RADIOGRAPH DENSITY LIMITS (OPTICAL DENSITY)
(حدود كثافة التصوير الإشعاعي — الكثافة الضوئية)

T-260.1 DEFINITION OF OPTICAL DENSITY
Optical density (OD — الكثافة الضوئية) is the measure of the degree of blackening
of the processed radiographic film. It is measured using a calibrated densitometer
(جهاز قياس الكثافة). OD is defined as:
  OD = log10 (I0 / I)
Where I0 = incident light intensity and I = transmitted light intensity through film.
Higher OD = darker film = more radiation absorbed in that area.

T-260.2 MINIMUM AND MAXIMUM DENSITY REQUIREMENTS
Per ASME Section V T-282.1 and Table T-282.1:

  MINIMUM optical density (OD) in the area of interest:
    X-ray:     OD ≥ 1.8
    Gamma ray: OD ≥ 2.0

  MAXIMUM optical density (OD) in the area of interest:
    All sources: OD ≤ 4.0

  These limits apply throughout the area of interest of the weld being examined
  (المنطقة موضع الاهتمام — region of interest including weld and minimum 1/4 in.
  each side of weld).

T-260.3 ACCEPTABLE DENSITY RANGE SUMMARY
  Technique      |  Minimum OD  |  Maximum OD
  X-ray          |     1.8      |     4.0
  Gamma-ray      |     2.0      |     4.0

  NOTE: Many fabricators target 2.5–3.5 OD as the practical working range for
  best contrast and sensitivity.

T-260.4 BASE PLUS FOG DENSITY
The base + fog density (الكثافة الأساسية + الضباب) of the unexposed but processed
film shall not exceed 0.30 OD above the clear film base. If base + fog exceeds
0.30 OD, the film lot or processing is suspect and shall be investigated before
use on Code radiographs.

T-260.5 EFFECT OF DENSITY ON IMAGE QUALITY
Density below minimum (under-exposed or under-developed — كثافة أقل من المطلوب):
  - Reduces subject contrast, making thin laminations and porosity harder to see.
  - May cause failure to resolve IQI 2T hole or essential wire.
Density above maximum (over-exposed or over-developed — كثافة أعلى من المطلوب):
  - Film too dark to read on standard film viewer.
  - Requires high-intensity viewer (شاشة عرض عالية الكثافة) rated ≥ 300 cd/m².
  - Even with high-intensity viewer, OD > 4.0 is not acceptable under ASME V.

ACCEPTANCE: OD ≥ 1.8 (X-ray) or OD ≥ 2.0 (gamma) AND OD ≤ 4.0 throughout
the area of interest. Base + fog ≤ 0.30 OD.
REJECTION: OD < 1.8 (X-ray) or OD < 2.0 (gamma) anywhere in area of interest —
REJECT, re-expose. OD > 4.0 anywhere in area of interest — REJECT, re-expose.
Base + fog > 0.30 OD — investigate and reject the film lot or processing.

CLAUSE T-261 — DENSITY VARIATION LIMITS ACROSS THE RADIOGRAPH
(حدود تباين الكثافة عبر الصورة الإشعاعية)

T-261.1 PURPOSE
Even when each individual film density reading falls within the T-260 limits
(1.8–4.0 OD), extreme density variation within a single radiograph may indicate:
  - Non-uniform radiation beam
  - Geometric thickness changes not accounted for in technique
  - Processing irregularities (streaks — خطوط على الفيلم)
  - Film cassette air gaps (poor screen contact)
The density variation requirement protects against localized areas of the radiograph
that technically "pass" absolute limits but have insufficient contrast or sensitivity
relative to adjacent areas.

T-261.2 PERMITTED DENSITY VARIATION
Per ASME Section V T-261:
The density through the IQI image (or adjacent to it, in the area of interest) and
the density in the same area of interest shall not vary by more than MINUS 15% or
PLUS 30% from the density reading taken through the IQI.

  That is:
  D_min_permitted = D_IQI × (1 − 0.15) = D_IQI × 0.85
  D_max_permitted = D_IQI × (1 + 0.30) = D_IQI × 1.30

  Example: If density through IQI = 2.50 OD:
    D_min = 2.50 × 0.85 = 2.13 OD
    D_max = 2.50 × 1.30 = 3.25 OD

  All density readings within the area of interest must fall within this range AND
  also within the absolute limits of T-260 (min 1.8/2.0, max 4.0 OD).

T-261.3 MEASUREMENT POINTS
Density readings shall be taken at:
  (a) The IQI location (through the IQI body, not through a hole).
  (b) At the darkest area within the area of interest.
  (c) At the lightest area within the area of interest.
All three readings shall be documented in the examination record.

T-261.4 WHEN VARIATION IS EXCEEDED
If the density variation exceeds the ±15%/+30% limit, the radiograph (or the
affected portion) is rejected. Causes shall be investigated:
  - Multiple-film technique with mismatched exposures
  - Irregular surface geometry
  - Processing streaks or chemical contamination
  - Partial blockage of the beam by fixtures or clamps

ACCEPTANCE: All density readings within area of interest fall within ±15% (lower)
/ +30% (upper) of the IQI density reading, AND within absolute limits (1.8/2.0 to 4.0 OD).
Density readings documented at IQI, darkest point, and lightest point.
REJECTION: Any area-of-interest density reading deviates more than −15% below or
+30% above the IQI density, OR falls outside absolute OD limits. Density variation
not measured and documented.

CLAUSE T-262 — DENSITY MEASUREMENT — DENSITOMETER USE
(قياس الكثافة الضوئية — استخدام جهاز الدنسيتومتر)

T-262.1 DENSITOMETER REQUIREMENT
All film density measurements for Code radiography shall be performed using a
calibrated transmission densitometer (دنسيتومتر — جهاز قياس الكثافة الضوئية بالإرسال).
Visual estimation of film density is not acceptable for Code work. The densitometer
shall be the transmission type (not reflection type used for photographic prints).

T-262.2 CALIBRATION REQUIREMENTS
The densitometer shall be calibrated using a calibrated step-tablet (شريط درجات
الكثافة المعايَر — step-wedge film) traceable to the National Institute of Standards
and Technology (NIST) or equivalent national standards body. Calibration shall be
verified:
  (a) At the beginning of each period of use (each shift or each day of use).
  (b) After any repair or battery replacement.
  (c) At the end of each period of use to confirm no drift.

T-262.3 CALIBRATION TOLERANCE
The densitometer reading on each step of the calibration step-tablet shall be
within ±0.05 OD of the certified value stamped on the step-tablet. If any step
reads outside ±0.05 OD, the instrument is out of calibration and shall not be
used until calibrated.

T-262.4 STEP-TABLET CALIBRATION
Step-tablets shall have a current calibration certificate (شهادة معايرة) with
certified OD values. Step-tablets shall be replaced or recertified if:
  - The certification is expired (typically annual or per laboratory schedule).
  - The tablet is scratched or damaged.
  - Any certified step reads visually different from adjacent steps.

T-262.5 MEASUREMENT PROCEDURE
When measuring film density:
  (a) Clean the densitometer aperture and the film surface.
  (b) Allow the densitometer to warm up per manufacturer requirements (typically
      5–10 minutes).
  (c) Zero the instrument (or verify zero on clear area of film or air).
  (d) Place the film on the aperture and read the density.
  (e) Record each reading to at least two decimal places (e.g., 2.35 OD).
  (f) Densitometer aperture size shall be ≤ 1/16 in. (1.6 mm) for most measurements
      to avoid averaging over too large an area.

ACCEPTANCE: Densitometer calibrated with NIST-traceable step-tablet within ±0.05 OD.
Calibration verified at start and end of each use period. Step-tablet current
certification. Readings documented to ≥ 2 decimal places.
REJECTION: Densitometer not calibrated or calibration expired. Densitometer reads
outside ±0.05 OD on calibration step-tablet. Density estimated visually without
densitometer. Step-tablet uncertified or expired calibration.

CLAUSE T-270 — EXAMINATION COVERAGE AND OVERLAP REQUIREMENTS
(تغطية الفحص ومتطلبات التداخل بين اللقطات)

T-270.1 GENERAL COVERAGE REQUIREMENT
All required weld length or area shall be covered by radiography with no un-examined
gaps. The extent of examination (نطاق الفحص) is defined by the Construction Code
(e.g., 100% RT, spot RT, or random RT). The RT procedure shall define the film
placement map (خريطة مواضع الفيلم) showing coverage of the entire required weld length.

T-270.2 FILM OVERLAP REQUIREMENT
Adjacent film placements (لقطات متجاورة) shall overlap each other sufficiently to
ensure full coverage with no gaps in the area of interest. Minimum overlap:
  - Minimum 1/2 in. (13 mm) between adjacent film exposures.
  - For circumferential pipe welds shot with multiple longitudinal film placements
    (external source — multiple shots): films shall overlap by ≥ 1/2 in. at each
    joint between adjacent film positions.
  - For panoramic (single-shot 360°) exposures: full circumference is covered in
    one exposure — overlap N/A for the weld, but IQI placement at 120° intervals
    verifies full circumference coverage.

T-270.3 WELD LENGTH COVERAGE VERIFICATION
The examination map shall be reviewed by the examiner and signed off. Coverage
shall account for:
  - Weld start and stop points (beginning and end of each weld pass)
  - Any areas where access is limited
  - Heat-affected zones (HAZ — منطقة التأثير الحراري) in cases where HAZ examination
    is required by the Construction Code

T-270.4 DOUBLE-FILM TECHNIQUE COVERAGE
When a double-film technique (طريقة الفيلم المزدوج) is used (two films in one cassette,
processed separately), the coverage requirements apply to both films. If the two
films show different areas in focus due to radiation geometry, the film providing
coverage of the area of interest for interpretation purposes shall be clearly
identified in the examination record.

ACCEPTANCE: Film placement map showing 100% coverage of required weld (or spot
percentage as required by Construction Code). Minimum 1/2 in. overlap between
adjacent film positions confirmed on examination map and radiographic images.
REJECTION: Gaps in coverage — any required weld length with no valid radiograph.
Overlap less than 1/2 in. between adjacent shots. No film placement map or coverage
documentation in examination record.

CLAUSE T-271 — TECHNIQUE FOR CYLINDRICAL WELDS (SINGLE-WALL, DOUBLE-WALL)
(تقنية التصوير لحامات الأسطوانية — جدار واحد، جدارين)

T-271.1 TECHNIQUE CLASSIFICATION
ASME Section V defines four primary radiographic techniques for pipe and vessel
cylindrical welds based on source position, film position, and number of walls:

  (a) Single-Wall Single-Image (SWSI — جدار واحد / صورة واحدة):
      Source inside pipe, film outside. Best technique for pipe with sufficient
      bore to allow internal source placement. For panoramic (360°) exposure:
      source at pipe centerline. For offset source: source inside near weld.

  (b) Double-Wall Single-Image (DWSI — جداران / صورة واحدة):
      Source outside, film outside, source ellipse technique. Pipe axis at ~3–7°
      to beam to separate near-wall and far-wall weld images. Only the far-wall
      image is interpreted. Used for pipe OD 3.5 in. (88.9 mm) and larger.
      Requires minimum 2 exposures 90° apart (or 3 exposures 60° apart).

  (c) Double-Wall Double-Image (DWDI — جداران / صورتان):
      Source outside, film outside, beam through both walls. Both welds superimposed
      on the same film and both are interpreted. Used for small-diameter pipe
      OD < 3.5 in. (88.9 mm). Minimum 3 exposures at 60° intervals OR 2 exposures
      at 90° intervals to give full coverage of weld circumference.

  (d) Single-Wall Single-Image (SWSI) — external source, internal film:
      Used for flat plates or vessels with film clamped inside. Less common.

T-271.2 PANORAMIC TECHNIQUE (SWSI — Source Inside at Centerline)
For pipe OD ≥ 3.5 in. and source placed at pipe centerline (تصوير بانورامي كامل):
  - Single exposure covers entire weld circumference.
  - SFD = pipe ID/2 (source to film = pipe inner radius to outer wall + film).
  - Three IQIs minimum at 0°, 120°, 240° positions.
  - Elliptical distortion less than ±1 in. (25 mm) from weld centerline is acceptable.

T-271.3 DOUBLE-WALL SINGLE-IMAGE TECHNIQUE (DWSI)
  - Source offset 5° to 7° from perpendicular to pipe axis (elliptical technique).
  - Minimum 2 shots at 90° apart, or 3 shots at 60° apart.
  - IQI placed on the source-side (outer) surface adjacent to the weld.
  - Only the far (film-side) weld image is interpreted for DWSI.
  - Geometric unsharpness calculation uses full OD of the pipe as OFD approximation.

T-271.4 DOUBLE-WALL DOUBLE-IMAGE TECHNIQUE (DWDI)
  - Source perpendicular to pipe axis.
  - Both near and far wall weld images are superimposed — both evaluated.
  - Minimum 3 exposures at 60° intervals required for full circumference coverage.
  - IQI on source side. Film-side "F" designation if film-side IQI required.
  - The elliptical image separation shall be between 1/4 OD and 3/4 OD of the pipe
    to ensure near-wall and far-wall images are distinguishable.

ACCEPTANCE: Technique (SWSI/DWSI/DWDI) selected per pipe OD and access as specified
in written procedure. Minimum number of exposures made for full coverage. IQIs in
correct positions. Image readable and meeting density/sensitivity requirements.
REJECTION: DWDI technique used on pipe OD ≥ 3.5 in. without documented justification.
Insufficient number of exposures leaving weld area un-covered. Images elliptically
distorted beyond limits. Coverage less than 100% of required weld circumference.

CLAUSE T-274 — TECHNIQUE FOR NOZZLE WELDS
(تقنية تصوير لحامات الوصلات / البوق النافذ)

T-274.1 APPLICABILITY
Nozzle welds (لحامات الوصلات / البوق النافذ) and set-in or set-on branch connections
present complex geometry for radiography. The examination technique shall be
specifically addressed in the written RT procedure. Common nozzle configurations:
  - Set-through (set-in) nozzle: nozzle bore penetrates the shell or head.
  - Set-on nozzle: nozzle butt-welded onto the shell exterior.
  - Elbolet, weldolet, sockolet connections.

T-274.2 SOURCE PLACEMENT OPTIONS FOR NOZZLES
  (a) Source inside nozzle bore, film on shell exterior: Best technique when
      nozzle ID is sufficient for source placement. Gives SWSI geometry.
  (b) Source on shell exterior, film on nozzle exterior: Used for small nozzle
      bores. Requires careful calculation of OFD across the weld.
  (c) Panoramic from vessel interior, film outside nozzle and shell: For large
      vessels with internal access.

T-274.3 IQI PLACEMENT ON NOZZLE WELDS
IQIs shall be placed on the source side. For nozzle welds with variable thickness
across the exposure:
  - Use the maximum single-wall thickness within the radiographic image to
    determine IQI designation.
  - Place IQI on a shim equal in thickness to the weld reinforcement if present.
  - Minimum 1 IQI per nozzle radiograph (more if coverage requires multiple shots).

T-274.4 GEOMETRIC UNSHARPNESS — NOZZLE SPECIAL CASE
For nozzle welds where the OFD is variable (e.g., oblique exposures), Ug shall be
calculated using the maximum OFD within the area of interest. The Ug shall not
exceed the limits in T-224.3 based on the nominal thickness of the thicker member
joined by the weld.

T-274.5 MINIMUM COVERAGE
For a full-circumference nozzle weld:
  - If source can be placed inside at nozzle centerline: 1 panoramic exposure covers
    full weld. IQI at 3 positions minimum.
  - If external source technique: minimum 3 exposures at 120° intervals (or more as
    required to eliminate unexamined areas).

ACCEPTANCE: Nozzle RT technique documented in procedure showing source location,
film location, and IQI positions. Ug calculated using maximum OFD. Full
circumference of nozzle weld covered by valid exposures. IQI sensitivity met.
REJECTION: Nozzle weld technique not documented in procedure. Ug exceeds limit
using maximum OFD for nozzle geometry. Gaps in nozzle weld coverage. IQI missing
or sensitivity not demonstrated.

CLAUSE T-280 — EVALUATION — READABLE vs UNREADABLE RADIOGRAPHS
(تقييم الصور الإشعاعية — قابلة للقراءة مقابل غير قابلة للقراءة)

T-280.1 DEFINITION OF A READABLE RADIOGRAPH
A radiograph is considered readable (قابل للقراءة) and acceptable for evaluation if:
  (a) It meets the minimum density requirements (OD ≥ 1.8 for X-ray; ≥ 2.0 for gamma).
  (b) Density does not exceed maximum (OD ≤ 4.0).
  (c) Required IQI sensitivity is demonstrated (2T hole or essential wire visible).
  (d) There are no processing artifacts or physical damage in the area of interest
      that could mask or simulate discontinuities.
  (e) The area of interest is correctly positioned within the film image.
  (f) Correct identification markers are present and legible.

T-280.2 UNREADABLE RADIOGRAPHS — CAUSES
A radiograph is declared unreadable (غير قابل للقراءة) and must be repeated if any
of the following artifacts or deficiencies occur in the area of interest:

  PROCESSING ARTIFACTS (عيوب المعالجة):
    - Chemical streaks (خطوط الكيماويات)
    - Pressure marks (علامات الضغط)
    - Light leaks / fog (تسرب الضوء)
    - Static marks (علامات الكهرباء الساكنة)
    - Scratches (خدوش على الفيلم)
    - Water spots or drying marks (بقع الماء)
    - Crimp marks (طيات الفيلم)
    - Dichroic fog (ضباب ثنائي اللون) from fixer contamination

  TECHNIQUE DEFICIENCIES (عيوب التقنية):
    - Density out of limits
    - IQI sensitivity not met
    - Geometric distortion (image blur, double image)
    - Incorrect film placement (weld not in image)
    - Back-scatter ("B" light image)

T-280.3 EVALUATION PROCEDURE
The certified Level II or Level III RT interpreter shall:
  (1) Examine radiographs on a calibrated film viewer (شاشة عرض معايَرة) in a
      darkened room, eliminating extraneous light at film edges with masking cards.
  (2) Verify density with densitometer before interpretation.
  (3) Verify IQI sensitivity.
  (4) Scan entire film systematically (weld and both heat-affected zones).
  (5) Document any observed indications with location, orientation, and size.
  (6) Make accept/reject determination per Construction Code acceptance criteria.
  (7) Sign and date the examination report.

T-280.4 FILM VIEWER (ILLUMINATOR) REQUIREMENTS
The film viewer (جهاز عرض الأفلام) shall:
  - Provide uniform diffuse light.
  - Have adjustable intensity (variable intensity type).
  - Be rated for high-density films when viewing OD > 3.0 (minimum 300 cd/m²
    transmitted luminance at OD 3.0).
  - Be calibrated for light output (verified annually or per employer procedure).

ACCEPTANCE: Radiograph free of artifacts in area of interest; density 1.8–4.0 OD
(X-ray) or 2.0–4.0 OD (gamma); IQI sensitivity demonstrated; identification
markers present; interpreted by Level II or III; report signed and dated.
REJECTION: Any processing artifact, physical damage, or density non-compliance in
the area of interest — REJECT, repeat exposure. IQI sensitivity not demonstrated.
Radiograph interpreted by Level I without direct Level II/III supervision.

CLAUSE T-282 — DISPOSITION OF INDICATIONS — LINEAR vs ROUNDED
(التصرف في المؤشرات — خطية مقابل مستديرة)

T-282.1 CLASSIFICATION OF RADIOGRAPHIC INDICATIONS
Radiographic indications (المؤشرات الإشعاعية) observed in the area of interest shall
be classified as:
  (a) LINEAR INDICATIONS (مؤشرات خطية):
      Length ≥ 3 × width in any orientation.
      Examples: cracks (تشققات), lack of fusion (عدم الانصهار), incomplete penetration
      (عدم الاختراق الكامل), elongated slag lines (خبث خطي), undercut (الحفر الجانبي).
  (b) ROUNDED INDICATIONS (مؤشرات مستديرة):
      Length < 3 × width. Circular or irregularly shaped but not linear.
      Examples: porosity (المسامية — isolated pores), inclusions (شوائب),
      clustered porosity (مجموعات مسامية).
  (c) GEOMETRIC INDICATIONS (مؤشرات هندسية):
      Indications caused by joint geometry, not actual discontinuities.
      Examples: weld root geometry, backing ring shadows.
      Geometric indications are NOT evaluated as weld defects.

T-282.2 SIZING OF INDICATIONS
Linear indication length and width shall be measured in the direction of maximum
extent on the radiograph image. Rounded indication maximum dimension shall be
measured as the maximum diameter of the indication image. Sizing shall be performed
using calibrated measuring tools on the film (loupe — عدسة مكبرة — or ruler).
Image scale correction shall be applied if magnification from geometry is significant.

T-282.3 DISPOSITION PROCEDURE
  Step 1: Classify the indication as linear or rounded.
  Step 2: Measure dimensions (length, width, maximum diameter).
  Step 3: Determine if the indication is a true discontinuity (not a processing
           artifact or geometric image).
  Step 4: Apply Construction Code acceptance criteria (see T-283).
  Step 5: If rejected, notify the responsible engineering or QC authority.
  Step 6: Document all indications — accept and reject — on the examination report
           with sketch or schematic showing indication location and orientation.

T-282.4 MULTIPLE INDICATIONS (CUMULATIVE LENGTH)
Some Construction Codes require evaluation of cumulative indication lengths within
a defined weld length (e.g., in any 6 in. of weld, maximum total indication length
is limited). The inspector shall evaluate both individual and cumulative indication
dimensions when required.

ACCEPTANCE: Indications classified as linear or rounded; sized accurately; evaluated
per applicable Construction Code acceptance criteria; all accept/reject decisions
documented with indication location, type, size, and disposition.
REJECTION: Any linear indication with length ≥ applicable Construction Code limit
(e.g., ≥ 3/4 in. for full penetration welds per ASME VIII). Indications not
classified or sized. Disposition not documented.

CLAUSE T-283 — ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA REFERENCE (B31.3 and ASME VIII)
(مرجع معايير القبول — B31.3 وASME القسم الثامن)

T-283.1 ARTICLE 2 DOES NOT SPECIFY ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA
ASME Section V Article 2 establishes examination TECHNIQUE requirements only.
Acceptance criteria (معايير القبول) for radiographic indications are ALWAYS taken
from the applicable Construction Code. The inspector must confirm which Construction
Code governs the examination before applying accept/reject limits.

T-283.2 ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA — ASME SECTION VIII DIV. 1 (UW-51 and UW-52)
For pressure vessels (أوعية الضغط) under ASME Section VIII Division 1:

  REJECTABLE INDICATIONS (100% RT — UW-51):
    - Any crack (تشقق) — any size — REJECT.
    - Any incomplete fusion (عدم الانصهار) — REJECT.
    - Any incomplete penetration (عدم اختراق كامل) — REJECT.
    - Any elongated slag inclusion with length > 1/3 T where T is the lesser of the
      two plate thicknesses joined (and not exceeding 3/4 in. absolute maximum).
    - Aligned slag in a line where aggregate length in 6T exceeds T and gaps < 3L
      (where L = longer slag inclusion length).
    - Rounded indication: charts in ASME VIII Appendix 4 define maximum allowed
      rounded indication size and count per 6 in. of weld.
    - Undercut > 1/32 in. (0.8 mm) depth if discernible by RT.

T-283.3 ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA — ASME B31.3 (PROCESS PIPING — أنابيب العمليات الصناعية)
Per ASME B31.3 Table 341.3.2 (for Normal Fluid Service, 100% RT):

  REJECTABLE INDICATIONS:
    - Any crack — REJECT.
    - Any lack of fusion — REJECT.
    - Any lack of penetration — REJECT (Normal Service); depth > 1 mm or 20% of
      thinner wall (whichever is less) for Category D normal fluid service.
    - Elongated indication (single): length > 1/3 T or 6 mm (0.25 in.) — REJECT.
      (T = weld thickness at indication location)
    - Rounded indication: maximum size 3 mm (1/8 in.) for T ≥ 3/4 in.; maximum
      size 2.5 mm for T < 3/4 in.; no more than 12 in any 6 in. of weld (consult
      B31.3 Appendix A Fig. A341.3.2 rounded indication acceptance charts).
    - Underfill (concavity) > 1 mm or 20% of wall thickness — REJECT.

T-283.4 ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA — API 1104 (PIPELINE WELDS — لحامات خطوط الأنابيب)
For pipeline welds under API 1104:
    - Cracks: REJECT — any length.
    - Incomplete fusion: individual length > 1 in. (25 mm), aggregate in 12 in.
      weld > 1 in. — REJECT.
    - Incomplete penetration (IP): individual IP > 1 in. (25 mm), aggregate in
      12 in. weld > 1 in., or aggregate in total weld > 8% of weld length — REJECT.
    - Burn-through: any burn-through exceeding applicable chart limits — REJECT.
    - Porosity: consult API 1104 Section 9.3 chart limits by pipe size and wall.

T-283.5 INSPECTOR DUTY
The inspector (مفتش الفحص) shall confirm which Code applies to each joint before
issuing a disposition. Mixing acceptance criteria from different Codes on the same
joint is NOT permitted. The applicable Code and edition shall be recorded on the
examination report.

ACCEPTANCE: Indications evaluated per applicable Construction Code; no linear
indications (crack, LF, LP) per ASME VIII UW-51 or B31.3; rounded indications
within chart limits; accept/reject decision documented per correct Code.
REJECTION: Any crack (any size, any Code). Any lack of fusion. Any incomplete
penetration beyond Code limits. Slag or rounded indications exceeding applicable
Code charts. Wrong Code acceptance criteria applied.

CLAUSE T-291 — RADIOGRAPH RETENTION REQUIREMENTS
(متطلبات الاحتفاظ بالأفلام الإشعاعية وسجلات الفحص)

T-291.1 RETENTION PERIOD
Per ASME Section V T-291, radiographic films and examination records shall be
retained for a minimum period as specified by the applicable Construction Code.
Typical requirements:
  - ASME Section VIII Div. 1: Films and records shall be retained for the life
    of the vessel or as agreed upon in the purchase specification (minimum 5 years
    in the absence of other requirements). Many jurisdictions require retention for
    the life of the pressure vessel.
  - ASME B31.3: Minimum 5 years from the date of examination.
  - API 1104: Minimum 3 years from the date of examination (some owner specifications
    require longer — always check owner's ITP or project specification).

T-291.2 RECORDS REQUIRED
The following records shall be maintained and retained:

  (a) Radiographic examination report (تقرير الفحص الإشعاعي) including:
      - Job/project identification
      - Component identification (weld number, spool number, joint ID)
      - Applicable code and edition
      - Radiographic procedure number and revision
      - Technique parameters (source type, SFD, OFD, exposure time, kV or Ci,
        film type, screen type, Ug calculation)
      - IQI type, designation, and placement (source or film side)
      - Film density readings (at IQI, darkest, lightest areas)
      - Description of all indications found (type, location, size, disposition)
      - Accept/reject disposition per Construction Code
      - Name, certification level, and signature of interpreter
      - Date of examination and date of report

  (b) Radiographic films themselves (الأفلام الإشعاعية نفسها) — properly identified
      with permanent film identification.

  (c) Densitometer calibration records.

  (d) RT personnel qualification and certification records.

T-291.3 FILM STORAGE FOR RETENTION
Films retained for the long term shall be stored:
  - In a cool, dry environment (temperature < 20°C / 68°F; RH < 50%).
  - Away from radiation sources, chemical fumes, and magnetic fields.
  - In acid-free envelopes or sleeves.
  - In labeled, indexed files traceable to the examination record.
Improper long-term storage (humidity, heat, chemical fumes) causes film deterioration
(yellow-brown staining — بقع صفراء وتدهور الفيلم) that can render films unreadable.

T-291.4 DIGITAL RECORDS
Where digital records (سجلات رقمية) are maintained as backup to or replacement for
paper records, the digital system shall:
  - Be backed up regularly (minimum daily).
  - Have access control to prevent unauthorized modification.
  - Provide traceability (revision history, date/time stamps).
  - Be maintained in a readable format for the full retention period.

ACCEPTANCE: Examination reports complete and signed by Level II/III; films
identified, indexed, and stored per T-291.3; retention period per applicable
Construction Code maintained; densitometer calibration records and personnel
certification records on file.
REJECTION: Examination reports incomplete, unsigned, or missing. Films not
retained for required period or lost. Film storage conditions causing film
deterioration. No traceability between film and examination report.

CLAUSE RT-SAFETY-01 — RADIATION SAFETY AND AREA CONTROL
(السلامة الإشعاعية والتحكم في المنطقة الإشعاعية)

NOTE: While ASME Section V Article 2 covers examination technique, radiation
safety requirements are governed by national nuclear regulatory authorities
and employer radiation safety programs. In Egypt: Egyptian Nuclear and Radiological
Regulatory Authority (ENRRA — هيئة الرقابة النووية والإشعاعية المصرية) and applicable
Ministry of Manpower regulations. The following requirements represent minimum
industry-standard radiation safety practices applicable in oilfield and fabrication
environments in Egypt and the MENA region.

RT-SAFETY-01.1 RADIATION EXCLUSION ZONE
A controlled exclusion zone (منطقة الإقصاء الإشعاعي) shall be established around the
radiation source during all exposures. Zone dimensions shall be calculated using
the source activity, beam direction, and dose rate survey. The exclusion zone shall
be established to maintain dose rates at the zone boundary ≤ 0.5 mSv/hr (2.5 mR/hr
or 50 μGy/hr) for controlled areas, and ≤ 0.02 mSv/hr (2 mR/hr) at uncontrolled
area boundaries (per IAEA and ENRRA guidelines). For Ir-192 at typical field
activity levels (5–75 Ci), controlled exclusion zones typically range from 30 to
150 meters in the unshielded beam direction.

RT-SAFETY-01.2 ZONE MARKING AND BARRICADING
The exclusion zone shall be physically barricaded and marked with:
  - Radiation warning rope or tape (شريط تحذير الإشعاع — yellow and magenta radiation
    hazard tape)
  - Standard radioactive/radiation hazard warning signs (ملصقات التحذير من الإشعاع)
  - Warning lights (red rotating or flashing light visible in daylight and dark)
  - Audible warning (horn or siren) activated during exposure
Guards (حراس المنطقة الإشعاعية) shall be posted at all access points to the exclusion
zone. No unauthorized personnel may enter the exclusion zone during exposure.

RT-SAFETY-01.3 PERSONNEL DOSIMETRY
All personnel working in or near the radiation zone shall wear:
  - Thermoluminescent dosimeters (TLDs — جرعات التيرمولومينسنت) — worn at chest or
    collar, processed and read monthly.
  - Direct-reading dosimeters (pocket dosimeters — جرعات جيب — or digital dosimeters)
    for real-time dose monitoring, read before and after each shift.
  - Film badges (شارات الفيلم) — acceptable alternative to TLD in some jurisdictions.
Dose records shall be maintained and reviewed against regulatory annual dose limits:
  - Occupational workers: 20 mSv/year (whole body) averaged over 5 years, max
    50 mSv in any single year (IAEA BSS / Egyptian regulations).
  - Lens of eye: 20 mSv/year.
  - Extremities and skin: 500 mSv/year.
Personnel exceeding dose limits shall be removed from radiation work until reviewed
by Radiation Protection Officer (RPO — مسؤول الحماية من الإشعاع).

RT-SAFETY-01.4 SURVEY METERS
Before commencing each RT exposure session:
  - Survey the area with a calibrated Geiger-Müller (GM) or ionization chamber
    survey meter (جهاز مسح الإشعاع المعايَر).
  - Verify source is fully retracted to safe position before personnel approach.
  - After exposure: Verify source retraction with survey meter before releasing
    the exclusion zone. Dose rate at source position (at 1 m from source, after
    retraction) shall be ≤ background + 0.5 μSv/hr before releasing.
Survey meters shall be calibrated annually by an accredited laboratory.

RT-SAFETY-01.5 SOURCE TRANSPORT AND STORAGE
Radioactive sources (المصادر المشعة) in the field shall be transported in Type B
transport containers (حاويات النقل من النوع ب) with:
  - Current transport certificate and package identification.
  - Surface dose rate ≤ 2 mSv/hr (200 mR/hr) at container surface.
  - Dose rate at 1 m from surface ≤ 0.1 mSv/hr (10 mR/hr).
Containers shall be secured during transport and stored in a locked, access-controlled
location when not in use. Inventory of all radioactive sources shall be maintained
by the RPO.

RT-SAFETY-01.6 EMERGENCY PROCEDURES
A source emergency procedure (إجراء طوارئ المصدر الإشعاعي) shall be available at the
work site and all RT personnel shall be trained in:
  - Response to source ejection (stuck-open source — مصدر متوقف في الفتح)
  - Evacuation of the area
  - Notification of RPO and site emergency response
  - First aid for radiation exposure victims
Emergency equipment (طوارئ الإشعاع) including long-handled tongs, lead containers,
and emergency survey meter shall be available at the RT work site.

ACCEPTANCE: Exclusion zone established, marked, and guarded. Personnel in radiation
zone wearing TLD and direct-reading dosimeter. Survey meter calibrated and used
before and after each exposure. Source retraction verified before zone release.
Source transport in Type B container with current documentation.
REJECTION: No exclusion zone or zone inadequately marked. Personnel in zone without
dosimetry. Survey meter not used or uncalibrated. Source not verified retracted
before zone release. Radioactive source unaccounted for or not in secure storage.
Any stuck-source incident without immediate emergency response activation.

CLAUSE RT-ID-01 — FILM IDENTIFICATION REQUIREMENTS (MARKING AND TRACEABILITY)
(متطلبات تعريف الفيلم — التحديد والتتبع)

RT-ID-01.1 MANDATORY FILM IDENTIFICATION
Every radiographic film shall carry identification information that is visible on the
processed radiograph (مرئي على الفيلم المعالج). Identification shall be applied using
lead letters and numerals (حروف وأرقام رصاص) placed on the film side of the object
before exposure, so the identification images are cast directly onto the film by the
radiation source. Do NOT write identification on the film after processing with pen
or marker — this does not satisfy the Article 2 requirement for permanent, radiograph-
integral identification.

RT-ID-01.2 REQUIRED IDENTIFICATION INFORMATION
Each radiograph shall show the following minimum identification on the film image:

  (a) MANUFACTURER / CONTRACTOR NAME or code (اسم الشركة المصنعة أو المقاول)
  (b) PART or WELD NUMBER (رقم الجزء أو رقم اللحام) — unique identifier traceable to
      the examination map and project documentation.
  (c) FILM LOCATION MARKER (مؤشر موضع الفيلم) — showing the exact position of the
      film along the weld, referenced to a fixed datum point on the component
      (e.g., weld start point 0°, or a stamped reference mark).
  (d) DATE OF EXAMINATION (تاريخ الفحص) — in lead numbers or coded notation traceable
      to the examination report date.
  (e) REVISION NUMBER if re-examination (رقم المراجعة) — e.g., "RT-2" for a second
      attempt at a previously examined joint.
  (f) WELDER / WELD IDENTIFICATION (رقم تعريف اللحام واللحام) — for production traceability.

RT-ID-01.3 LOCATION MARKERS
Film location markers (مؤشرات موقع الفيلم) shall be placed at each end of the film
and shall appear on the processed radiograph. For circumferential welds:
  - Markers shall show the distance from a fixed reference datum.
  - "0°" or "0 in." marker at the reference start point.
  - Each subsequent film position shows its distance from 0 (e.g., 6 in., 12 in.)
    or angular position (e.g., 120°, 240°).
For panoramic shots: markers at 0°, 90°, 180°, 270° around circumference, or as
required to uniquely identify every point on the weld.

RT-ID-01.4 ADDITIONAL MARKERS
  - IQI identification: IQI designation number shall be visible on the IQI body
    as cast on the film. Lead "F" marker if film-side IQI placement was used.
  - Lead "B" marker on back of cassette (for back-scatter verification — T-222.3).
  - "+" or arrow markers to indicate direction of examination (beam direction) when
    relevant for complex geometry.
  - Shipper or owner ID when required by purchase specification.

RT-ID-01.5 LEAD LETTER SIZE AND PLACEMENT
Lead identification letters/numbers shall be:
  - Minimum 3/8 in. (10 mm) height for legibility.
  - Placed outside the area of interest but within the film boundaries.
  - Not placed over the weld unless flush-ground and inspection of that area is
    not required at that location.
  - Fastened securely to prevent movement during exposure.

RT-ID-01.6 TRACEABILITY TO EXAMINATION RECORD
The film identification shall provide a direct, unambiguous link between the film
image and the examination report. Any film that cannot be positively identified
and traced to its examination record is not acceptable as a Code record. The
inspector shall verify identification completeness before removing films from
cassettes and before signing off the examination report.

ACCEPTANCE: All required identification (company/contractor, weld/part number,
film location marker, date, revision if applicable) visible on processed radiograph
in lead image form. Identification legible (≥ 3/8 in. character height). Positive
traceability to examination report and component.
REJECTION: Any required identification element missing from the film image.
Identification applied post-processing with ink/marker (not lead image on film).
Film position marker absent or ambiguous — film cannot be unambiguously placed on
the weld. Film not traceable to examination report. Identification obscures or
falls within the area of interest.

GEOMETRIC UNSHARPNESS:
  Ug = (F × t) / D
  F = source size (mm or in.)
  t = object-to-film distance (mm or in.)
  D = source-to-object distance (mm or in.)

MINIMUM SOURCE-TO-OBJECT DISTANCE:
  D_min = (F × t) / Ug_max

DENSITY VARIATION LIMITS:
  D_min_acceptable = D_IQI × 0.85
  D_max_acceptable = D_IQI × 1.30
