CLAUSE: T-410
TOPIC: Scope — Contact and Immersion Ultrasonic Examination Methods
REFERENCE: ASME BPVC Section V, Article 4, T-410

FIELD EXPLANATION:
Article 4 covers ultrasonic examination (الفحص بالموجات فوق الصوتية) of welds,
base materials, forgings, and castings used in pressure-retaining components.
Two primary coupling methods are recognized: contact testing (الفحص بالتلامس
المباشر) where the transducer touches the part surface directly through a
thin couplant film, and immersion testing (الفحص بالغمر) where both the
transducer and the part are submerged in a liquid column or a focused water
jet column. Contact testing is the dominant method used in field and fabrication
shop environments across oilfield operations in Egypt and the MENA region.
Immersion testing is primarily used in automated shop scanning of plates,
nozzles, and complex geometries. Article 4 applies when referenced by the
Construction Code (e.g., ASME VIII Div. 1, B31.3) or the Owner's specification;
it does not stand alone as an acceptance standard. The written procedure
(الإجراء الكتابي المعتمد) must reference the applicable Construction Code
for acceptance criteria. All examinations must be performed by personnel
certified to at least UT Level II (المستوى الثاني) in accordance with
ASNT SNT-TC-1A or ASME CP-189 as specified by the employer.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Examination method (contact or immersion) must be specified in the approved
  written procedure before examination begins.
- Personnel qualification: minimum UT Level II (المستوى الثاني للفحص).
- Written procedure must be demonstrated and qualified per T-420.

REJECTION:
- Examination performed without an approved written procedure: REJECT the
  entire examination — results are not valid and must be repeated.
- UT Level I performing final evaluation without Level II supervision: REJECT
  evaluation — re-evaluate under qualified Level II or Level III oversight.
- Contact method used on geometries requiring immersion (e.g., curved OD < 4 in.
  [100 mm] without contouring): REJECT scan coverage — procedure non-conformance.

CLAUSE: T-421
TOPIC: Equipment — Pulse-Echo Instrumentation and Transducer Frequency Range
REFERENCE: ASME BPVC Section V, Article 4, T-421

FIELD EXPLANATION:
The ultrasonic instrument (جهاز الموجات فوق الصوتية) shall be a pulse-echo
type capable of generating and receiving frequencies in the range typically
used for pressure equipment examination. The instrument must have a calibrated
gain (amplifier) control readable in 2 dB increments or less, allowing precise
sensitivity adjustment during calibration and scanning. The display (شاشة
العرض) must have a linear horizontal sweep (A-scan presentation) and an
amplitude response within ±5% linearity for screen height from 20% to 80% FSH
(Full Screen Height — الارتفاع الكامل للشاشة). Transducer frequencies for
weld and pressure-vessel examination typically fall in the 1 MHz to 6 MHz
range; 2 MHz and 2.25 MHz are common for thick welds and heavy forgings
(> 50 mm), while 4 MHz and 5 MHz are used for thinner materials and higher
resolution needs. The instrument and transducer combination must be verified
for linearity (خطية الجهاز) at the beginning of each examination shift. Screen
height linearity and amplitude control linearity checks must be documented.
Equipment used outdoors in desert environments (as common in Egyptian oilfields)
must be verified to operate within the manufacturer's stated temperature range;
typical instruments operate from -10°C to +55°C ambient.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Instrument frequency range: 1 MHz to 6 MHz (per T-421.1).
- Amplitude control linearity: within ±20% of the nominal dB value throughout
  the 20% to 80% FSH range (T-421.2).
- Screen height linearity: within ±5% of FSH when checked per Mandatory
  Appendix I of Article 4.
- Gain control: readable in steps of 2 dB or less.
- Operating temperature: within manufacturer's specified range, documented.

REJECTION:
- Instrument fails screen height linearity check (deviation > ±5% FSH): REJECT
  instrument — remove from service, repair or replace, re-calibrate before use.
- Amplitude control linearity outside ±20%: REJECT instrument — do not use for
  examination until repaired and verified.
- Gain control readable in steps > 2 dB: REJECT instrument — cannot ensure
  calibration accuracy; replace unit.
- No documentation of pre-shift linearity checks: REJECT all examination data
  from that shift — re-examine with verified equipment.

CLAUSE: T-422
TOPIC: Couplant (مادة الاقتران) — Requirements and Temperature Limits
REFERENCE: ASME BPVC Section V, Article 4, T-422

FIELD EXPLANATION:
Couplant (مادة الاقتران أو مادة الربط) is the medium placed between the
transducer face and the examination surface to eliminate air gaps and enable
efficient ultrasonic energy transfer. ASME V T-422 requires that the couplant
used during calibration be the same type (or demonstrated equivalent) as that
used during the actual examination; switching couplant types between calibration
and examination is not permitted without re-calibration. Common couplants used
in MENA oilfield operations include glycerin (جليسرين), water-based gels, and
light machine oil; for elevated-temperature surfaces, high-viscosity glycerin or
purpose-made high-temperature pastes are used. For austenitic stainless steel
and nickel alloy components, the couplant must be certified low-halide content
(chloride content ≤ 200 ppm) to prevent stress corrosion cracking; this
requirement applies universally in Egyptian petrochemical plants. On vertical or
overhead surfaces, gel-type couplant must be used to prevent running and
inconsistent coupling. The surface temperature at the point of examination must
be within the couplant manufacturer's rated range; for standard glycerin, this
is typically -20°C to +52°C (−4°F to +125°F). Above this range, a
high-temperature couplant rated for the actual surface temperature must be used.
After examination is complete, couplant must be removed from pressure boundary
surfaces to prevent corrosion or coating adhesion problems.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Couplant type used during examination matches type used during calibration,
  or acoustic equivalency is demonstrated and documented.
- For austenitic SS and nickel alloys: couplant chloride content ≤ 200 ppm,
  certified by supplier's SDS or lab test.
- Surface temperature within couplant manufacturer's stated range.
- Couplant removed from all examination surfaces after completion.

REJECTION:
- Couplant changed between calibration and examination without re-calibration:
  REJECT all examination data — re-calibrate with correct couplant and
  re-examine.
- Chloride content > 200 ppm on austenitic SS or nickel alloy components:
  REJECT couplant immediately — clean surface, replace with certified
  low-halide couplant, re-examine.
- Surface temperature exceeds standard couplant rating without using
  high-temperature couplant: REJECT examination — couplant coupling is
  unreliable; switch to approved HT couplant and re-examine.
- Couplant not removed from ferritic carbon steel surfaces within 24 hours of
  examination: FLAG for corrosion monitoring; clean immediately.

CLAUSE: T-423
TOPIC: Calibration Blocks — IIW Block and Distance/Amplitude Curves (DAC)
REFERENCE: ASME BPVC Section V, Article 4, T-423; Mandatory Appendix E

FIELD EXPLANATION:
Calibration blocks (كتل المعايرة) must be fabricated from material whose
acoustic velocity and attenuation are representative of the examination
material — same product form, material specification, and heat treatment
condition, or demonstrated acoustic equivalency. The IIW (International
Institute of Welding) Type 1 block (V1 block) or Type 2 (V2 block) is
the standard reference block used for angle-beam calibration: it provides
a 100 mm radius curved surface for distance calibration, a 1.5 mm diameter
side-drilled hole for DAC reference, and a Perspex insert for checking
the refracted beam angle (T-423.2). For straight-beam calibration, a
step wedge calibration block or a basic calibration block with flat-bottom
holes (FBHs) at specified depths is used to establish the Distance-Amplitude
Correction (DAC) curve. DAC calibration requires establishing amplitude
responses from reference reflectors (side-drilled holes — SDH, or
flat-bottom holes — FBH) at a minimum of 3 points spanning the examination
range (near field, mid-range, and far field). The calibration block thickness
must encompass the thickness range being examined; a calibration block of
thickness T can qualify examination of material from T/2 to 2T (per T-434.1).
Calibration block surface finish must be representative of the examination
surface condition. In field operations in Egypt, IIW V1 blocks are the most
common; they must be accompanied by a calibration certificate and dimensional
verification report traceable to national standards (EOS or equivalent).

ACCEPTANCE:
- Calibration block material: same P-Number and Group Number as examination
  material, or acoustic velocity within ±5% of examination material velocity
  (demonstrated by measurement).
- IIW block dimensional tolerance: per AWS/IIW dimensional standard
  (radius 100 ±0.5 mm; SDH 1.5 mm ±0.05 mm).
- DAC curve established from minimum 3 reference reflector responses at
  ≥ 3 depth positions spanning the full examination range (T-461.1).
- Block thickness: calibrates thickness range of T/2 to 2T.
- Calibration block surface finish: Ra ≤ 6.3 µm (250 µin) on scanning surface.

REJECTION:
- Calibration block material P-Number/Group does not match and acoustic
  equivalency not demonstrated: REJECT block — fabricate or obtain correct
  calibration block before examination.
- Fewer than 3 DAC reference points established: REJECT DAC curve — rebuild
  with minimum 3 data points.
- IIW block dimensions outside tolerance (e.g., radius deviation > 0.5 mm):
  REJECT block — recertify or replace.
- Calibration block used for examination beyond its qualified thickness range
  (outside T/2 to 2T): REJECT examination data — use correct thickness block
  and re-examine.

CLAUSE: T-431
TOPIC: Surface Preparation (تجهيز السطح) Requirements
REFERENCE: ASME BPVC Section V, Article 4, T-431

FIELD EXPLANATION:
The examination surface (سطح الفحص) and adjacent areas within the scan zone
must be free of weld spatter, scale, loose scale, paint of excessive
thickness, and surface irregularities that would interfere with free movement
of the search unit or impair the acoustic coupling. For weld examination,
the weld crown (وجه اللحام) must be ground flush or dressed to allow the
angle-beam transducer (البروب الزاوي) access to scan within 1/2 in. (13 mm)
of the weld centerline where the geometry requires it. In practice, in
Egyptian refinery and pressure vessel fabrication shops, the weld crown is
typically allowed to remain un-dressed provided the transducer can achieve
full code-required scan coverage from the adjacent base metal; if weld
geometry blocks required scan paths, grinding flush is mandatory.
Surface temperature at examination must be within the equipment's stated
operational range as noted in T-421. Coated surfaces may be examined through
coating provided the coating thickness is uniform and representative of the
calibration surface condition used during calibration; otherwise, the coating
must be removed in the scan zone before examination. Painted or coated welds
in offshore or coastal Egyptian facilities require coating removal in the scan
band (typically 50–75 mm either side of weld centerline) before UT scanning.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Surface cleanliness: free of weld spatter, loose mill scale, and paint
  exceeding 1 mm (40 mils) unless examined through identical coating
  thickness during calibration.
- Surface roughness (خشونة السطح): Ra ≤ 6.3 µm (250 µin) on the scanning
  surface contact zone.
- Weld crown: ground flush or within profile that allows transducer to scan
  within 13 mm of weld centerline as required by scan plan.
- Coated surface examination: coating thickness uniform within ±10% of
  calibration surface coating thickness.

REJECTION:
- Surface roughness Ra > 6.3 µm causing unreliable coupling: REJECT — dress
  surface to meet requirement and re-examine.
- Weld spatter in scan path preventing complete transducer contact: REJECT
  partial scan — remove spatter, clean surface, re-examine affected scan area.
- Coating thickness non-uniform (deviation > ±10% from calibration coating
  thickness): REJECT examination through coating — remove coating, re-calibrate
  on bare surface, re-examine.
- Surface temperature outside equipment operating range at time of examination:
  REJECT — adjust conditions, wait for cooling or heating as applicable.

CLAUSE: T-432
TOPIC: Contact Scan — Straight Beam (Compression Wave / الموجة الضاغطة) Technique
REFERENCE: ASME BPVC Section V, Article 4, T-432

FIELD EXPLANATION:
The straight-beam (compression wave — الموجة الضاغطة أو الموجة المحورية)
contact technique uses a 0° transducer generating longitudinal waves
perpendicular to the examination surface. This technique is the primary method
for detecting laminations (طبقات أو انفصالات) in base material, for
evaluating material thickness, and for verifying the acoustic path before
angle-beam scanning. In weld examination per ASME V Article 4, straight-beam
scanning of the base metal adjacent to the weld is performed prior to
angle-beam examination to detect laminations that would interfere with the
shear-wave beam path or produce false indications. The transducer is moved
systematically over the base metal on both sides of the weld at a minimum scan
width equal to 1.5× the maximum refracted beam path in the examination volume.
The instrument gain is set so the back-wall reflection (صدى الجدار الخلفي)
from the base metal reaches 75–80% FSH during calibration. Any area where the
back-wall echo drops below 50% FSH (without a corresponding surface echo
increase indicating thickness change) is flagged as a potential lamination and
must be mapped and reported. Frequency selection: 4 MHz to 5 MHz for wall
thicknesses up to 50 mm; 2 MHz to 2.25 MHz for thicknesses above 50 mm.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Back-wall echo (صدى الجدار الخلفي) during scanning: ≥ 75% FSH on sound
  base metal areas, confirming adequate coupling and calibration.
- Scan coverage of base metal adjacent to weld: 100% within the defined
  scan band (minimum 1.5× maximum refracted beam path width on each side).
- Frequency: 2 MHz to 5 MHz as specified in written procedure, appropriate
  to material thickness.

REJECTION:
- Back-wall echo drops below 50% FSH in base metal without geometric
  explanation: FLAG and MAP as suspected lamination — report, evaluate per
  construction code lamination acceptance criteria.
- Back-wall echo loss > 6 dB sustained over an area > 6.5 cm² (1 in.²)
  without geometry change: REJECT base metal area as potentially laminated —
  further evaluate before angle-beam examination.
- Scan coverage < 100% of required base metal scan band: REJECT — complete
  scanning before releasing weld for angle-beam UT.

CLAUSE: T-433
TOPIC: Angle-Beam (Shear Wave — الموجة المستعرضة) Technique — Index Point
         and Refracted Angle Verification
REFERENCE: ASME BPVC Section V, Article 4, T-433

FIELD EXPLANATION:
The angle-beam shear-wave technique (تقنية الحزمة الزاوية — الموجة المستعرضة)
is the primary method for detecting planar flaws (عيوب مستوية) such as cracks,
lack of fusion (عدم الاندماج), and incomplete penetration in fusion welds.
The transducer uses a plastic wedge to introduce the ultrasonic beam at a
refracted angle — typically 45°, 60°, or 70° — into the examination material.
Before each examination session and after any transducer change, the technician
(الفني) must verify: (1) the exit point / index point (نقطة الخروج أو نقطة
الصفر) of the beam using the IIW block radius, confirming it is within ±1 mm
of the marked index point on the wedge; and (2) the actual refracted angle
using the angle verification holes on the IIW block or V2 block, confirming
the angle is within ±2° of the nominal angle. Beam spread and near-field
calculations must be documented in the written procedure. Scanning is performed
by moving the transducer parallel to the weld (longitudinal scan — المسح
الطولي) and perpendicular to the weld (transverse scan — المسح العرضي) with
the beam directed both toward and away from the weld, to achieve full volumetric
coverage. The scan pattern must cover the entire weld cross-section including
root (جذر اللحام), fusion zone (منطقة الاندماج), and heat-affected zone (HAZ
— المنطقة المتأثرة بالحرارة).

ACCEPTANCE:
- Index point deviation: ≤ ±1 mm from marked reference on wedge (check on
  IIW block 100 mm radius).
- Refracted beam angle: within ±2° of nominal (45°, 60°, or 70°) — verified
  on IIW or V2 block before each examination shift and after transducer change.
- Full volumetric scan coverage of weld cross-section: 100% coverage including
  root, body, and cap pass.
- Scan performed in both transducer orientations (toward weld and away from
  weld) for each required scan angle.

REJECTION:
- Index point deviation > ±1 mm: REJECT transducer/wedge — recondition wedge
  face, re-verify before use. All data collected with out-of-tolerance wedge
  must be re-examined.
- Refracted angle deviation > ±2° from nominal: REJECT transducer setup —
  replace wedge or transducer, re-calibrate, and re-examine entire weld.
- Scan coverage < 100% of required weld volume: REJECT UT report — identify
  uncovered zones, re-examine with supplemental angles or transducer positions.

CLAUSE: T-434
TOPIC: Calibration Frequency — Recalibration Triggers (إعادة المعايرة)
REFERENCE: ASME BPVC Section V, Article 4, T-434

FIELD EXPLANATION:
Calibration (المعايرة) is the process of adjusting the instrument's sweep
range and gain settings using reference reflectors in the calibration block
to establish a known sensitivity baseline before examination commences.
ASME V T-434 defines the mandatory frequencies at which calibration must be
verified and repeated. At a minimum, calibration must be performed: at the
start of each examination, after any interruption of 30 minutes or more, after
equipment change (instrument, transducer, cable, couplant, or battery),
and at the end of each examination period to verify calibration has been
maintained. If the equipment is found to have drifted at any verification
check, all examination performed since the last valid calibration check must
be considered suspect and re-examined. A calibration verification check
(التحقق من المعايرة) consists of scanning the reference reflectors on the
calibration block and confirming amplitude responses are within ±2 dB and
distance positions are within ±1 mm or ±2% of sweep range (whichever is
greater) of the original calibration values. In field operations in Egyptian
desert conditions (high temperature variation between morning and afternoon),
calibration drift due to temperature change in cables and transducers is common
and must be checked after significant ambient temperature shifts (> 14°C /
25°F change).

ACCEPTANCE:
- Calibration performed at start of every examination.
- Calibration verification performed after any break ≥ 30 minutes, equipment
  change, or shift end.
- Calibration verification check amplitude: reference reflector response
  within ±2 dB of original calibration amplitude.
- Distance calibration verification: screen position within ±1 mm or ±2% of
  sweep range (whichever is greater).

REJECTION:
- Calibration drift found: amplitude deviation > 2 dB from original
  calibration: REJECT — determine last valid calibration time; all examination
  performed since last valid calibration must be re-examined.
- Distance drift > ±1 mm or ±2% of sweep at verification check: REJECT — same
  consequence; re-examine all data since last valid check.
- Calibration not performed at start of examination session: REJECT entire
  examination session — no valid data accepted; re-examine from beginning
  with verified calibration.
- End-of-shift calibration verification not documented: FLAG as procedure
  non-conformance — supervisor review required.

CLAUSE: T-441
TOPIC: Distance-Amplitude Correction (DAC) Curve Construction
         (منحنى تصحيح المسافة والسعة)
REFERENCE: ASME BPVC Section V, Article 4, T-441; Mandatory Appendix C

FIELD EXPLANATION:
The Distance-Amplitude Correction (DAC) curve (منحنى DAC) compensates for the
natural decay of ultrasonic beam amplitude with increasing sound path distance
through the material. As the beam travels further, beam spread and material
attenuation reduce the echo amplitude from identical reflectors at greater
depths — the DAC curve corrects for this so that all reflectors of equivalent
size produce equal amplitude readings regardless of depth. To construct the DAC
curve, the technician scans the calibration block reference reflectors
(side-drilled holes or flat-bottom holes) at a minimum of 3 separate depths
covering the full examination thickness range. The peak echo amplitude from
each reference hole is marked on the A-scan screen, and the resulting points
are connected to form the DAC curve. The primary DAC line is drawn through
these points and represents 100% DAC (reference level). Two additional curves
are then established: the 50% DAC line (−6 dB below primary DAC) and the 20%
DAC line (−14 dB below primary DAC). All indications during scanning are
evaluated relative to these DAC lines. On modern digital UT instruments, the
DAC curve is stored electronically and displayed dynamically as the sound path
changes. For PAUT instruments, the TCG (Time-Corrected Gain) function
automatically compensates amplitude — PAUT DAC/TCG setup is addressed
separately in T-470 supplementary guidance. Re-draw the DAC whenever the
transducer, couplant, instrument, or material changes.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Minimum 3 reference reflector data points required to construct the DAC
  curve, spanning the full examination depth/distance range.
- Each reference point amplitude recorded at peak response (maximize echo by
  rocking and rotating transducer).
- Primary DAC line plotted through all 3+ peak amplitude points.
- 50% DAC (−6 dB) and 20% DAC (−14 dB) lines established from primary DAC.
- DAC curves established with same transducer, wedge, instrument, cables, and
  couplant as used in examination.

REJECTION:
- Fewer than 3 reference points used to establish DAC: REJECT DAC — reconstruct
  with minimum 3 data points; all examination data collected with invalid DAC
  is invalid and must be re-examined.
- DAC established with different equipment than used in examination: REJECT
  DAC — re-establish with actual examination equipment and re-examine.
- Peak response not verified (transducer not maximized on each hole):
  REJECT DAC point — re-maximize, re-mark, reconstruct curve.
- DAC not redrawn after equipment or couplant change: REJECT all data collected
  after change — re-calibrate, rebuild DAC, re-examine.

CLAUSE: T-442
TOPIC: Reference Sensitivity — 6 dB Drop and 20 dB Drop Methods
         (طرق حساسية الإشارة المرجعية)
REFERENCE: ASME BPVC Section V, Article 4, T-442

FIELD EXPLANATION:
Reference sensitivity (الحساسية المرجعية) defines the gain level at which the
examination is conducted, established from the calibration block reference
reflectors. In ASME V Article 4, the primary sensitivity reference is the DAC
curve itself — scanning is performed with the instrument set so that the
reference reflector produces a response at the primary DAC (100% DAC / reference
level). The 6 dB drop method (طريقة الانخفاض بمقدار 6 ديسيبل) is used primarily
for reflector sizing: the transducer is moved until the peak echo from an
indication drops by 6 dB (half amplitude / 50% of peak height), and the
reflector boundary is marked at that point on both sides — the reflector length
equals the distance between the 6 dB drop points. This method is valid when
the reflector length is greater than the beam width at the examination depth.
The 20 dB drop method (طريقة الانخفاض بمقدار 20 ديسيبل) maps the outer
boundary of a reflector by finding positions where the echo drops to 10% of
peak (20 dB below peak), and is typically used for mapping larger volumetric
reflectors. The sensitivity setting used during scanning must be established
and documented before scanning begins; scanning sensitivity (مستوى الحساسية
أثناء المسح) is typically set at the primary DAC reference level, not at the
evaluation or recording threshold level.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Scanning sensitivity: set so reference reflector (SDH or FBH per procedure)
  produces response at primary DAC (100% DAC ≡ reference level ≡ 0 dB
  correction).
- 6 dB drop sizing: transducer positions where amplitude = 50% of peak are
  recorded; distance between positions = reported reflector length.
- 20 dB drop sizing: transducer positions where amplitude = 10% of peak
  (−20 dB from peak) used for bounding volumetric or large reflectors.
- All sizing performed with same calibration and gain settings as used during
  detection scan.

REJECTION:
- Scanning sensitivity set below reference sensitivity (less sensitive than
  DAC) without written justification: REJECT scan — scanning sensitivity must
  be at or above reference sensitivity. Re-scan at correct sensitivity.
- 6 dB drop sizing applied to reflectors smaller than beam width at that depth:
  REJECT sizing result — use alternative sizing method (tip diffraction or
  equivalent sizing technique per procedure).
- Gain changed during scanning without recalibration: REJECT all data collected
  at non-calibrated gain setting.

CLAUSE: T-450
TOPIC: Scan Coverage Requirements — Overlap ≥ 10% (تداخل المسح)
REFERENCE: ASME BPVC Section V, Article 4, T-450

FIELD EXPLANATION:
Complete volumetric coverage (تغطية حجمية كاملة) of the examination volume
is the fundamental requirement of ASME V Article 4. For weld examination, the
entire weld cross-section — including weld metal, heat-affected zone (HAZ —
المنطقة المتأثرة بالحرارة), and adjacent base metal — must be interrogated
by the ultrasonic beam at the required angle(s). T-450 requires that the
scanning index overlap (تداخل الأثر الفعال للمسح) between adjacent passes be
a minimum of 10% of the transducer element (crystal) size, ensuring that no
gap exists between adjacent scan strips. In practice, the effective beam width
at the examination depth governs the maximum scan index (scan pitch / خطوة
المسح), and operators must verify that adjacent scan passes overlap by at
least 10% of the near-surface beam diameter. For standard manual contact UT
in oilfield field welds, a scan index (lateral step between passes) of no more
than 1/2 the transducer active element dimension is typical. Examination
coverage must be documented in the UT report using a scan map or scan diagram
showing transducer positions, angles used, and zones covered. Areas of
limited access (e.g., near nozzles, flanges, or supports) must be documented
as scan limitations in the report, and alternative examination methods should
be proposed if critical areas cannot be covered.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Scan index overlap: ≥ 10% of transducer active element size between
  adjacent scan passes (T-450.2).
- 100% of the required examination volume covered as defined in the written
  procedure and scan plan.
- Scan diagram or coverage map included in examination report confirming
  full coverage.
- All required scan directions completed (minimum: parallel and perpendicular
  to weld axis for angle-beam; 0° for straight-beam base metal check).

REJECTION:
- Scan overlap < 10% of active element size between passes: REJECT scan
  coverage — re-scan with corrected index to achieve minimum 10% overlap.
- Any portion of required examination volume not covered (documented scan gap):
  REJECT weld examination as incomplete — identify uncovered zone, re-scan
  with additional transducer positions or angles.
- Scan map not completed or coverage not documentable: REJECT UT report as
  incomplete — document cannot be released without coverage verification.

CLAUSE: T-461
TOPIC: Recording Thresholds — 100% DAC and 20% DAC Rules
         (عتبات التسجيل والتوثيق)
REFERENCE: ASME BPVC Section V, Article 4, T-461

FIELD EXPLANATION:
Recording thresholds (عتبات التسجيل) define the minimum indication amplitude
above which the technician must record the indication in the UT examination
report for further evaluation. ASME V T-461.1 establishes that all indications
with amplitudes at or above 20% of the primary DAC level (20% DAC) must be
investigated to determine their nature and origin. All indications with
amplitudes at or above 100% DAC (primary reference level) must be recorded,
characterized, and evaluated for acceptance or rejection against the applicable
Construction Code. The three zones defined by the DAC curves create a practical
decision framework: indications below 20% DAC (non-recordable zone — المنطقة
غير القابلة للتسجيل) are typically not recorded unless their nature or
location makes them significant; indications in the 20% to 100% DAC range
(evaluation zone — منطقة التقييم) require investigation and characterization;
indications at or above 100% DAC (recordable and rejectable zone — منطقة
الرفض) must be recorded with all required data and evaluated. Note: the
recording threshold (20% DAC) is an examination/documentation requirement per
Article 4; the acceptance/rejection criteria (whether an indication > 100% DAC
is actually rejectable) come from the Construction Code (e.g., ASME VIII,
B31.3, API 1104). Distance, orientation, and classification of indications
at or above 20% DAC must be recorded including sound path, transducer angle,
and location coordinates.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Indications < 20% DAC: not required to be recorded (may be noted for
  information if deemed significant by Level II).
- Indications ≥ 20% DAC but < 100% DAC: must be investigated and documented
  (location, amplitude, direction, depth); evaluated per construction code.
- Indications ≥ 100% DAC: must be fully recorded and reported — amplitude,
  location (weld station, depth, lateral position), sound path length, transducer
  angle, and reflector length (by 6 dB drop or specified method).

REJECTION:
- Indication ≥ 100% DAC not recorded in the examination report: REJECT report —
  missing required data; examiner must provide supplemental data from saved
  A-scan or repeat examination.
- Indication ≥ 20% DAC not investigated for classification: REJECT UT report
  — incomplete evaluation; return to site for characterization of all
  unclassified indications.
- Recording threshold set above 20% DAC in procedure without Construction Code
  approval: REJECT procedure as non-conforming — revise and re-qualify.

CLAUSE: T-462
TOPIC: Characterization of Reflectors — Planar vs Volumetric
         (تصنيف العواكس: مستوية أم حجمية)
REFERENCE: ASME BPVC Section V, Article 4, T-462

FIELD EXPLANATION:
Characterization (تصنيف العاكس) determines the nature of a recorded indication:
whether it is a planar reflector (عاكس مستوٍ — a crack, lack of fusion, or
linear discontinuity lying in a plane essentially parallel to the beam) or a
volumetric reflector (عاكس حجمي — a porosity cluster, slag inclusion, or void
with extent in three dimensions). This distinction is critical because planar
reflectors are generally more detrimental than volumetric ones of the same
overall size. Characterization is performed using angle-beam scan data:
a planar reflector typically produces a sharp, high-amplitude echo that
maximizes strongly at a specific transducer position and angle, shows amplitude
response from both forward and backward scan directions, and often shows tip
diffraction signals from its upper and lower extremities. A volumetric reflector
produces a broader, lower-amplitude response that changes slowly with transducer
movement, typically with lower amplitude-to-length ratio. Scanning the indication
from multiple angles (e.g., 45° and 70°) and from both sides of the weld aids
characterization. The technician must document the characterization basis —
using transducer response pattern, amplitude variations, and reflector geometry
— in the examination report. ASME VIII Div. 1 and most Construction Codes apply
more stringent acceptance limits to planar reflectors than volumetric ones.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Volumetric indication: characterized when amplitude response is gradual with
  transducer movement, shows no sharp maximization, and is consistent from
  multiple angles.
- Planar indication: characterized when sharp amplitude peak is found at
  specific transducer angle and position, often with tip diffraction signals.
- Characterization must be documented with the scan angles used, amplitude
  behavior description, and examiner's conclusion in the UT report.

REJECTION:
- Indication ≥ 100% DAC not characterized (not classified as planar or
  volumetric): REJECT UT report — characterization is required for all
  recordable indications; return for additional scanning or assessment.
- Planar indication classified as volumetric without documented justification
  from multi-angle scanning: REJECT classification — re-examine from additional
  angles; apply planar reflector acceptance criteria as conservative default
  until proper characterization is completed.

CLAUSE: T-463
TOPIC: Sizing Methods — 6 dB Drop and Tip Diffraction
         (طرق قياس الأبعاد: الانخفاض 6 ديسيبل وانعراج الطرف)
REFERENCE: ASME BPVC Section V, Article 4, T-463; Mandatory Appendix I

FIELD EXPLANATION:
Sizing (قياس أبعاد العيب) determines the through-wall height and length of a
detected reflector to enable engineering fitness-for-service assessment or
Construction Code evaluation. Two primary sizing methods are recognized in
ASME V Article 4 practice: (1) The 6 dB drop method (طريقة الانخفاض بـ 6
ديسيبل) — the transducer is moved until the peak echo amplitude from the
indication drops to 50% of its maximum (a 6 dB reduction), and the transducer
cursor positions at each drop point define the reflector's edges. Length is
the distance between two lateral 6 dB drop positions. This method is applicable
when the reflector's extent is larger than the beam width. (2) Tip diffraction
method (طريقة انعراج الطرف أو TOFD — Time of Flight Diffraction) identifies
discrete diffraction signals from the upper and lower tips of a crack or
lack-of-fusion defect; the time-of-flight difference between upper and lower
tip signals directly converts to the through-wall height of the defect. TOFD
is substantially more accurate for through-wall height sizing than amplitude
methods and is preferred for fitness-for-service (FFS) assessments. When using
the 6 dB drop for length sizing, the reported length is the distance between
6 dB drop points on the scanning surface. Through-wall height by 6 dB drop
requires precise depth coordinate calculations from sound path and refracted
angle geometry.

ACCEPTANCE:
- 6 dB drop method applicable when reflector length > beam width at examination
  depth (verify beam width from transducer specification or measurement).
- Reported reflector length = distance between 6 dB drop positions (lateral
  scan) on the scanning surface.
- TOFD tip diffraction: height = (c × Δt) / 2, where c = shear wave velocity
  and Δt = time difference between upper and lower tip diffraction signals.
- Sizing results must be referenced to the weld coordinate system and recorded
  in the UT report.

REJECTION:
- 6 dB drop sizing applied to reflectors smaller than beam width: REJECT length
  measurement — beam width overestimates small reflectors; use alternative
  method (TOFD or documented AWS Table 6.3 equivalent correction factor).
- Tip diffraction sizing performed without documented velocity verification:
  REJECT height measurement — verify shear wave velocity in material, recalculate.
- Through-wall height sizing not performed on indications ≥ 100% DAC where
  acceptance criterion is height-based: REJECT evaluation — perform required
  sizing before accepting or rejecting the indication.

CLAUSE: T-470
TOPIC: Evaluation of Indications — Amplitude Evaluation and Disposition
         (تقييم الإشارات: تقييم السعة والقرار النهائي)
REFERENCE: ASME BPVC Section V, Article 4, T-470; T-471

FIELD EXPLANATION:
Evaluation (التقييم) is the step following detection and characterization where
the technician and/or Engineer applies the Construction Code acceptance criteria
to determine whether an indication is acceptable or rejectable. ASME V Article 4
itself does not contain acceptance criteria — it specifies only the examination
method and recording requirements. Acceptance criteria are provided by the
applicable Construction Code: for pressure vessels, ASME VIII Div. 1 Appendix 12;
for piping, B31.3 Chapter VI; for pressure piping in refineries, API 570;
for pipelines, API 1104; and for structural welds, AWS D1.1. The evaluation
process in the field involves: (1) confirming the indication is real and not
a geometric echo (false indication from weld crown, root geometry, or backing
strip); (2) recording the amplitude as a percentage of DAC; (3) sizing the
reflector; (4) classifying as planar or volumetric; and (5) comparing all
measured parameters to the applicable code acceptance table. Amplitude alone
is not sufficient for evaluation in all codes — AWS D1.1 and ASME VIII use
amplitude combined with reflector length and distance from the weld surface.
The Level II examiner makes the recording; the acceptance/rejection evaluation
for Construction Code compliance may require Level III or engineer review
depending on the employer's written practice.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Indication characterized and evaluated per all applicable Construction Code
  criteria (amplitude, length, depth, and classification).
- Indication evaluated using correct acceptance table for the applicable code
  (e.g., ASME VIII Div. 1 App. 12 Table UW-52 or equivalent).
- Geometric echoes (false indications from root, crown, or backing) confirmed
  as non-relevant and documented with evidence (geometry sketch, supporting
  scan data, or reference reflector comparison).

REJECTION:
- Any indication ≥ 100% DAC not evaluated against applicable Construction Code
  acceptance criteria: REJECT weld — evaluation is incomplete; Level III review
  required before acceptance.
- Planar indication exceeding construction code length or amplitude acceptance
  limit: REJECT weld — require repair (إعادة اللحام أو الإصلاح) and re-examination.
- Volumetric indication exceeding applicable code limits for size or density:
  REJECT weld — require repair and re-examination per repair procedure.
- Indication classified as geometric (non-relevant) without documented
  supporting evidence: REJECT classification — must provide documented basis;
  re-examine with additional techniques to confirm.

CLAUSE: T-471
TOPIC: Acceptance Criteria Reference — Construction Code Interface
         (مرجع معايير القبول: الارتباط بكود البناء)
REFERENCE: ASME BPVC Section V, Article 4, T-471; ASME VIII App. 12;
            B31.3 Para. 344.6; AWS D1.1 Table 6.2 / 6.3

FIELD EXPLANATION:
As stated in T-471, the acceptance criteria to be applied in evaluating UT
indications must come from the Construction Code under which the component
is designed and fabricated — not from ASME V itself. This clause functions
as a mandatory cross-reference requirement. The examiner must have the
applicable acceptance table available at the examination site and must record
which code and edition is being applied in the UT examination report. Common
acceptance criteria references encountered in Egyptian petrochemical and oil
and gas projects include: ASME VIII Div. 1 Mandatory Appendix 12 (UT of welds
in pressure vessels), which uses a combination of amplitude above reference
and reflector length; B31.3 para. 344.6 referring to Appendix 12 of ASME V
for technique, with acceptance from ASME VIII or a project-specific standard;
AWS D1.1 Annex S (UT of structural welds) which uses a dB rating system;
and API 1104 Section 9 for pipeline girth weld UT. Examiners must verify that
the acceptance table is from the correct edition of the Construction Code
referenced by the project specification. In Egyptian projects governed by
Egyptian Standard ES or ISO 17640, additional criteria from those standards
may supplement the above. All acceptance decisions must be signed and dated
by the responsible Level II or Level III.

ACCEPTANCE:
- ASME VIII Div. 1 App. 12: indications accepted when amplitude < 100% DAC
  AND classified as non-planar; planar indications ≥ 100% DAC are rejectable.
- B31.3 (per App. 12 reference): same amplitude criteria as ASME VIII App. 12.
- AWS D1.1 (UT Class B welds): indication accepted when dB rating ≤ acceptance
  value from Table 6.2 (combination of amplitude + weld thickness correction).
- API 1104 Section 9: indications accepted when amplitude < 100% DAC AND
  length < code-specified limits per discontinuity type and pipe wall thickness.

REJECTION:
- Any planar indication ≥ 100% DAC in ASME VIII or B31.3 pressure boundary
  welds: REJECT — repair required regardless of length.
- AWS D1.1: dB rating > acceptance value from Table 6.2 for the applicable
  weld category: REJECT weld — repair and re-test.
- API 1104: amplitude ≥ 100% DAC AND length exceeding code-permitted limits
  for that indication type: REJECT weld — cut out and re-weld or engineer-
  approved repair.
- Acceptance criteria not identified in UT report (no code reference stated):
  REJECT report as incomplete — add code reference and re-issue.

CLAUSE: T-491
TOPIC: Report Requirements — Minimum Content of UT Examination Report
         (متطلبات التقرير: الحد الأدنى لمحتوى تقرير الفحص)
REFERENCE: ASME BPVC Section V, Article 4, T-491

FIELD EXPLANATION:
T-491 requires a written examination report (تقرير الفحص الكتابي) for every
ultrasonic examination performed under Article 4. The report is a quality
record that must be retained per the Construction Code and Owner's requirement
(typically minimum 5 years for pressure equipment in Egypt per EPC/EGPC
specifications, or the design life of the equipment). The minimum required
content of the UT report per T-491 includes: equipment identification
(manufacturer, model, serial number), transducer identification (frequency,
size, type, serial number), calibration block identification and reference
reflector data, couplant used, calibration date and personnel identification,
component identification (drawing number, weld number, material, thickness),
scan coverage diagram, all recorded indications with amplitude, location,
depth, sound path, transducer angle, and classification, disposition (accept
or reject with code reference), and the examiner's name, certification level,
and signature. Digital UT instruments with data logging must have the scan file
archived with the report. In Egyptian oil company (EGPC, EGAS, ENI Egypt,
Eni/IEOC) projects, the UT report must typically also carry the third-party
inspection company stamp and the Owner's inspector signature before the weld
is released. Any open indications requiring disposition by Level III or
engineering must be noted as "PENDING" until resolved.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Report must contain all mandatory elements per T-491 (see field explanation
  above) — all 14 minimum data fields must be present.
- All recorded indications (≥ 20% DAC) documented with amplitude, location,
  depth, sound path, angle, and classification.
- Disposition recorded for every indication: "Acceptable" or "Rejectable"
  with specific code paragraph reference.
- Examiner signature, certification level, and date on every report page.
- Calibration data appended or referenced by unique calibration record number.

REJECTION:
- Report missing any mandatory T-491 data field: REJECT report — return to
  examiner for completion; weld release is on hold pending complete report.
- Indication recorded without disposition (blank disposition field): REJECT
  report — all recorded indications must have documented disposition.
- Report signed by Level I examiner without Level II countersignature: REJECT
  report — Level I cannot independently release UT results; Level II or Level
  III countersignature required.
- No scan coverage diagram or coverage description: REJECT report — add
  coverage diagram and re-issue.

CLAUSE: T-434.2 (Supplemental)
TOPIC: Equipment Calibration Drift — When to Re-Examine
         (انجراف معايرة الجهاز: متى يجب إعادة الفحص)
REFERENCE: ASME BPVC Section V, Article 4, T-434.2; T-434.3

FIELD EXPLANATION:
Calibration drift (انجراف المعايرة) occurs when the instrument's calibration
shifts from its established baseline during an examination session due to
temperature change, battery depletion, component aging, or physical shock
to the equipment. ASME V T-434.2 specifies that if, at any calibration
verification check, the equipment is found to have drifted beyond permitted
limits (> 2 dB amplitude change or > ±1 mm / ±2% sweep position change),
all examination data collected since the last valid calibration verification
is suspect and must be voided. A formal "drift event" (حدث الانجراف) must
be documented in the examination record, identifying the time of last valid
calibration, the time the drift was discovered, and the identity of all
welds or areas examined during the suspect period. All welds in the suspect
period must be re-examined after equipment is repaired or replaced and
re-calibrated. In summer field conditions in Egypt (ambient temperatures
reaching 45–50°C), battery-powered UT instruments frequently experience
voltage sag that causes gain drift; calibration verification every 30
minutes is strongly recommended under these conditions rather than waiting
for the 30-minute mandatory minimum. Equipment that experiences physical
shock (dropped transducer, cable pulled sharply) must be immediately
re-calibrated before any further examination is recorded.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Calibration drift discovered at verification check within allowed limits:
  ± 2 dB amplitude AND distance within ±1 mm or ±2% sweep — calibration
  remains valid; document verification result and continue.
- All calibration verification results documented chronologically in the
  calibration log with time, result, and examiner initials.

REJECTION:
- Amplitude drift > 2 dB at verification check: REJECT all examination data
  since last valid calibration — document drift event; re-calibrate; re-examine
  all suspect welds.
- Distance drift > ±1 mm or > ±2% sweep at verification check: REJECT all data
  since last valid calibration — same consequence as amplitude drift.
- Physical shock to equipment (dropped transducer, cable jerk) without immediate
  re-calibration: REJECT all data collected after shock event — re-calibrate and
  re-examine.
- No calibration log entries for a shift: REJECT entire shift's examination data
  — calibration history is unverifiable; re-examine all welds from that shift.

CLAUSE: T-420 (Supplemental)
TOPIC: Phased Array Ultrasonic Testing (PAUT — الفحص الطوري متعدد العناصر)
         — When Applicable and Basic Setup Requirements
REFERENCE: ASME BPVC Section V, Article 4, T-420; Mandatory Appendix D;
            ASME V Article 4 Nonmandatory Appendix N (PAUT)

FIELD EXPLANATION:
Phased Array Ultrasonic Testing (PAUT — الفحص بالموجات فوق الصوتية متعدد
العناصر والتحكم في الطور) uses multi-element transducer arrays (typically 16,
32, or 64 elements) with electronically controlled time delays to steer and
focus the ultrasonic beam at multiple angles and depths simultaneously — without
physically moving a single-element transducer wedge. PAUT is recognized within
the ASME V Article 4 framework when implemented per a qualified written procedure,
with the technique demonstrated to meet or exceed the detection capability of
conventional UT. Under ASME V 2023, PAUT procedures must be formally qualified,
either by demonstration or by use of a procedure qualification record (PQR)
showing equivalent performance on representative specimens. PAUT basic setup
elements required by the written procedure include: focal law file definition
(element selection, aperture, delays), angular range and step (e.g., 40°–70°
in 1° increments), focus depth or focal law optimization depth, index point
verification for each active focal law, TCG (Time-Corrected Gain) calibration
replacing conventional DAC for each focal law, reference sensitivity set from
calibration block SDHs, and minimum required S-scan angular coverage of the
weld cross-section. PAUT is increasingly mandatory in Egyptian refinery turnaround
inspection (TAR) for code-compliant weld examination of high-value equipment
where conventional UT coverage is limited by geometry.

ACCEPTANCE:
- PAUT procedure qualified by performance demonstration on a qualification test
  block containing representative flaws of the minimum detectable size for the
  applicable construction code.
- Focal law file verified: index point per focal law within ±1 mm; refracted
  angle per law within ±2° of nominal.
- TCG calibration established from reference SDH at minimum 3 depths per focal
  law, all reference amplitudes within ±2 dB after TCG application.
- Full weld cross-section coverage demonstrated on procedure qualification block
  before production examination.
- S-scan angular range covers full required weld bevel geometry with ≥ 2°
  overlap between adjacent angular positions.

REJECTION:
- PAUT procedure not formally qualified per T-420 or equivalent qualification
  evidence: REJECT — PAUT results not acceptable; supplement with conventional
  UT from a qualified procedure or formally qualify the PAUT procedure.
- Index point deviation per focal law > ±1 mm: REJECT focal law file — recalculate
  delays, re-verify, and re-calibrate before scanning.
- TCG calibration deviation > ±2 dB from reference on any focal law: REJECT
  TCG setup — rebuild focal law file and re-calibrate.
- S-scan angular coverage gap leaving any portion of weld cross-section
  unexamined: REJECT scan coverage — add supplemental focal laws or conventional
  UT to cover gap.

CLAUSE: T-432 / T-433 (Supplemental)
TOPIC: Weld Geometry Limitations — Crown, Root, Backing Strip, and Access
         Restrictions Affecting Scan Coverage
         (قيود هندسة اللحام: تأثير الوجه والجذر وشريط الدعم على المسح)
REFERENCE: ASME BPVC Section V, Article 4, T-432; T-433; T-450;
            ASME V Article 4 Nonmandatory Appendix B

FIELD EXPLANATION:
Weld geometry limitations (قيود هندسة اللحام) represent the most frequent
source of incomplete scan coverage in field UT operations. Three primary
geometric features cause scan limitations: (1) The weld crown (وجه اللحام
المرتفع / reinforcement) — a raised weld crown prevents the angle-beam
transducer from being positioned directly adjacent to the weld centerline,
creating a dead zone (منطقة عمياء) near the weld root when scanning from
one side. The crown height must be measured and documented; if crown height
> 3 mm (1/8 in.) interferes with required beam path, the crown must be
dressed flush. (2) The weld root with a backing strip (شريط الدعم / backing
bar) — a permanent backing strip creates a geometric reflector at the weld
root that can mask real root discontinuities (incomplete fusion to backing,
root cracks). This is the most problematic condition in weld UT; the examiner
must document the backing strip presence and the Construction Code's position
on whether root zone UT is considered meaningful in this condition. ASME VIII
App. 12 acknowledges that root zone detection is compromised by backing strips.
(3) Access restrictions from nozzle, flange, or structural clamp proximity —
when the scan cannot reach within the required minimum distance of the weld
centerline, the uncovered zone must be documented in the scan limitation table
of the UT report. Alternative methods (TOFD, PAUT, or RT) must be considered
for areas with geometric scan limitation. Crown height ≤ 3 mm and smooth
transition to base metal are targets for acceptable geometry.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Weld crown height: ≤ 3 mm (1/8 in.) with smooth blend to base metal — allows
  transducer positioning within code-required distance of weld centerline.
- Backing strip (permanent): document its presence in UT report; root zone UT
  is performed but results are qualified with notation that geometric masking
  may affect detectability of root discontinuities.
- Access restriction: document the unexamined volume precisely (station, depth
  range, and reason). Propose alternative examination method for unexamined zone.
- Scan limitation noted and signed by Level II: acceptable as documented
  limitation provided alternative method is applied or Owner accepts the risk
  in writing.

REJECTION:
- Weld crown height > 3 mm (1/8 in.) blocking required transducer access to
  weld centerline: REJECT — crown must be dressed flush before UT is performed;
  document in NCR (Non-Conformance Report — تقرير عدم المطابقة).
- Backing strip present and root zone UT reported as "complete and valid" without
  acknowledging geometric masking: REJECT characterization — UT report must note
  the limitation; root zone results qualified accordingly.
- Unexamined volume > 5% of required examination volume with no alternative
  examination method proposed or applied: REJECT weld as insufficiently examined
  — apply supplemental method (TOFD, RT, or PAUT) to cover the gap.
- Crown geometry causing scan gap > 3 mm through-wall at the root zone without
  documentation: REJECT report — undocumented geometric limitation is a
  reportable procedure non-conformance.
