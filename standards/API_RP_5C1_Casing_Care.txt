CLAUSE 01 — RECEIVING INSPECTION AT WELLSITE (VISUAL CHECK AND STENCIL VERIFICATION)
[REF: API RP 5C1, Section 5 — Receiving, Unloading, and Handling; Subsection 5.1]

Inspect every joint of casing or tubing (غلاف / أنابيب إنتاج) immediately upon
arrival at the wellsite before unloading commences. Verify that the stencil
markings (الطباعة على الأنبوب) on each pipe joint match the mill certificate
and delivery ticket item-by-item; minimum stencil data to verify includes:
API size (outside diameter), nominal weight (lb/ft), grade, connection type,
and heat number (رقم الصهر). Reject any joint whose stencil is missing,
illegible, or does not match the delivery documentation; do not downgrade or
accept on verbal assurance alone. Perform an overall visual survey (الفحص
البصري) of each joint for gross damage — dents, bends, crushed ends, missing
thread protectors — before the pipe leaves the transport vehicle. Any joint
showing visible buckling (تشوه / انحناء) or more than one (1) missing thread
protector from a bundle must be segregated and individually re-inspected before
acceptance. Record the joint count against the packing list; a discrepancy of
more than 1% of the tally count shall trigger a full physical recount before
offloading continues.

ACCEPTANCE:
- All stencil data matches delivery ticket and mill cert; OD size ±1.0%, grade
  matches, heat number present and legible.
- Thread protectors (واقيات الخيوط) installed on both pin and box ends of
  every joint.
- Visual condition: no visible bends, no dents deeper than 1/4 in (6.35 mm),
  no crushed pipe body.
- Joint count within ±1% of packing list quantity.

REJECTION:
- Any joint with stencil missing, illegible, or mismatched to delivery
  documents — reject entire joint; do not use.
- Any joint showing visible bow or curvature that, when rolled on a flat rack,
  shows gap > 1/4 in per 10 ft (6.35 mm per 3 m) of pipe length — reject.
- Any joint with both thread protectors missing; inspect threads immediately;
  reject if thread damage is confirmed.
- Count discrepancy > 1% with packing list — halt offloading, perform recount.

CLAUSE 02 — UNLOADING AND HANDLING — SLING REQUIREMENTS, NO HOOKS ON THREADS
[REF: API RP 5C1, Section 5.2 — Unloading]

Pipe (أنابيب) shall be unloaded from transport vehicles using wide-body fabric
slings (حبال قماشية / شناكل قماشية), minimum 6 in (150 mm) wide, or purpose-built
pipe cradles; sling material must be rated for the calculated load with a safety
factor of not less than 5:1 on the working load. Wire rope slings are NOT
permitted to contact the pipe body directly without protective lagging because
steel wire will indent the pipe surface and initiate corrosion pits. Under no
circumstances shall pipe handling hooks (شناكل معدنية / خطاف) be inserted into
the threaded box or pin end for lifting purposes; hook-on-thread lifting crushes
thread form and renders the connection unacceptable. When a crane or forklift is
used, the lift points must be positioned to prevent overhang of more than 10 ft
(3 m) beyond the support point so that the pipe does not deflect and take a
permanent set. Tag lines shall be used to control swing during all crane
lifts; pipe bundles shall not be allowed to impact rig structures, crown block
substructure, or other pipe joints during the swing arc. Bundles shall be broken
apart on the ground before individual joints are handled by personnel; do not
allow workers to stand directly under suspended loads at any time.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Fabric sling minimum 6 in (150 mm) wide, rated ≥ 5:1 safety factor, no cuts
  or fraying present.
- Wire rope slings used only with full-length rubber or canvas lagging covering
  the pipe-contact zone.
- Overhang not exceeding 10 ft (3 m) per support point.
- Zero instances of hook insertion into threaded ends; any attempt is an
  immediate stop-work condition.

REJECTION:
- Any unpadded wire rope sling contacting bare pipe body — stop lift, replace
  with padded sling before continuing.
- Any pipe joint dropped or swung into contact with another pipe or structure —
  reject that joint, inspect for dents and ovality before use.
- Any joint picked by inserting a hook or casing spider into the thread box
  without a proper thread-saver sub — reject, perform thread inspection.
- Fabric sling showing cut, burn, or load-rating tag missing — remove from
  service immediately.

CLAUSE 03 — STORAGE YARD INSPECTION — STACKING HEIGHT AND PIPE RACK REQUIREMENTS
[REF: API RP 5C1, Section 6 — Storage]

Pipe stored in the yard (مستودع الأنابيب / الساحة) must be laid on wooden or
rubber-topped pipe racks with a minimum of two support points per joint; skid
spacing shall not exceed 20 ft (6.1 m) for casing and 15 ft (4.6 m) for tubing
to prevent sag and permanent bow. Maximum stacking height (ارتفاع التكديس) for
casing shall not exceed 5 ft (1.5 m) above the bottom pipe, and shall not
exceed 3 ft (0.9 m) for tubing, regardless of pipe weight; exceeding these
heights risks crushing lower joints and deforming thread-protector fit. The
bottom layer of pipe must be chocked (كتل خشبية) front and rear on every skid
to prevent rolling; wooden chocks must contact the pipe body and be spiked or
wired to the skid so they cannot kick out. Pipe shall be stored with the box-end
(الصندوق / الكوبلينج) facing in a consistent direction and elevated slightly to
allow rainwater drainage away from threads; standing water inside box ends
initiates corrosion under thread protectors within 24–48 hours in humid or
saline environments. Inspect the yard storage area after any high-wind event,
rain, or sand storm (عاصفة رملية) for displaced chocks, rolled pipe, and
protector condition before the next picking cycle begins.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Rack skid spacing ≤ 20 ft (6.1 m) for casing, ≤ 15 ft (4.6 m) for tubing.
- Stack height ≤ 5 ft (1.5 m) for casing; ≤ 3 ft (0.9 m) for tubing.
- Chocks present, secured, and in contact on both sides of every bottom-tier
  joint at every skid.
- Thread protectors intact on all stored joints; no standing water visible
  inside box ends.

REJECTION:
- Any stack exceeding maximum height limits — immediately de-stack to
  acceptable height before pulling further joints.
- Bottom-layer joints with missing chocks on any skid — stop picking, re-chock
  before proceeding.
- Any joint discovered to have rolled off the rack — segregate, inspect for
  dents, ovality, and thread damage before allowing to be run.
- Thread protectors missing or displaced and threads showing corrosion or
  debris accumulation — tag joint for cleaning and thread inspection before
  acceptance.

CLAUSE 04 — THREAD PROTECTOR INSPECTION — WHEN TO REMOVE AND INSPECT
[REF: API RP 5C1, Section 7.1 — Thread Protectors]

Thread protectors (واقيات الخيوط / بلاستيك الخيوط) shall remain installed on
all pipe joints until the joint is positioned on the V-door or catwalk
immediately prior to picking to the rig floor. Do not remove protectors in the
yard simply to inspect threads unless the joint is being prepared for a specific
inspection programme; premature removal exposes threads to sand, moisture, and
mechanical damage with no benefit. When protectors are removed for inspection,
do so by hand or with a protector wrench; never use pipe tongs or strap wrenches
directly on the protector body because overtorquing can crack plastic protectors
and drive fragments into threads. Examine the interior of each removed protector
for debris, rust staining, moisture, or damaged plastic that could indicate the
thread beneath has been compromised; a protector that contains red or orange
rust water must be treated as a warning sign and the thread inspected immediately.
Thread protectors that have been removed must be replaced with new or undamaged
protectors if the joint is being returned to storage; do not re-install a
cracked, stripped, or broken protector as it offers no protection. When
protectors are removed at the wellsite for running, lay them out of the work
area to prevent tripping hazards on the rig floor.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Protectors intact, no cracks, no stripped threads on the protector itself,
  and no rust water visible inside the protector cavity.
- Protector removed by hand torque or protector wrench only; no tong marks
  on the protector body.
- Threads beneath protector: free of foreign material, no rust, no mechanical
  damage; dope film (if factory-applied) intact.

REJECTION:
- Protector containing rust-colored water or visible corrosion product —
  remove and inspect underlying thread immediately; reject joint if thread
  corrosion pitting depth exceeds 1/32 in (0.8 mm).
- Cracked or stripped protector — replace before returning to storage; do not
  re-install damaged protector.
- Thread protector showing tong jaw marks on its body — inspect protector
  engagement thread for deformation; if deformed, replace protector and inspect
  pipe thread.

CLAUSE 05 — DRIFTING REQUIREMENTS — API DRIFT MANDREL SIZE PER GRADE AND WEIGHT
[REF: API RP 5C1, Section 7.2 — Drifting; API Spec 5CT, Table C.41 (Drift Dimensions)]

Every joint of casing and tubing (غلاف / أنبوب إنتاج) shall be drifted at the
wellsite with the correct API drift mandrel (مندريل القياس / مقياس الانزلاق)
immediately before running, regardless of whether the joint was drifted at the
mill; yard handling, transport, and storage can introduce deformation that was
not present at the time of mill inspection. The drift mandrel shall conform to
API Spec 5CT Table C.41 for the specific size, weight, and grade combination;
using an undersized or oversized mandrel is not acceptable and will give false
results. The mandrel must pass through the full joint length by gravity or by
hand push only; no hammering, jarring, or forcing of the drift mandrel is
permitted because this can temporarily spring a deformed pipe and give a false
pass. If the mandrel hangs up at any point, rotate it 90° and attempt once more;
if it still hangs, reject the joint — do not attempt to force through. Both the
drift mandrel and the pipe bore must be clean and free of dope, debris, and
scale before drifting; contamination can cause a false failure on a good joint.
Record the drift result (pass/fail) against the joint serial or tally number on
the running tally sheet.

ACCEPTANCE (example values — confirm per API Spec 5CT Table C.41 for actual size/weight):
- 9-5/8 in, 47.0 lb/ft — drift OD: 8.525 in (216.5 mm); mandrel passes full
  length by gravity or light hand push.
- 7 in, 26.0 lb/ft — drift OD: 6.151 in (156.2 mm); mandrel passes freely.
- 4-1/2 in tubing, 12.60 lb/ft — drift OD: 3.795 in (96.4 mm); mandrel
  passes freely.
- In all cases: full unobstructed passage of the correct API drift mandrel.

REJECTION:
- Any joint where the API-size drift mandrel does not pass freely through the
  full joint length — reject immediately; do not run; tag and set aside.
- Any joint where the mandrel was forced, hammered, or jarred through — result
  is invalid; re-drift with correct procedure; if unable to drift cleanly, reject.
- Drifting performed with wrong mandrel size — results are invalid; re-drift
  with correct mandrel.
- Any joint not drifted at wellsite before running — stop, pull back, drift
  before continuing.

CLAUSE 06 — THREAD CLEANING PROCEDURE — CLEAN, DRY, INSPECT BEFORE CONNECTION
[REF: API RP 5C1, Section 7.3 — Cleaning and Inspection of Threads]

All thread forms (خيوط) on both pin (ذكر) and box (أنثى / صندوق) ends must be
cleaned to bare metal before application of any makeup compound; residual old
dope, rust, scale, sand, or elastomeric debris from protectors will cause
false torque readings and incomplete thread engagement. Approved cleaning method:
wire brush (فرشاة سلكية) for base cleaning, followed by solvent wipe (قطعة نظيفة
مع مذيب) to remove hydrocarbon residue; compressed air at a maximum of 30 psi
(207 kPa) directed along the thread helix to remove brush fragments and solvent.
Do not use chlorinated solvents that leave a residue harmful to thread compound
chemistry. After cleaning, inspect threads in good lighting — daylight or a
minimum 50 foot-candle (540 lux) artificial light — for mechanical damage, tears,
galling, or erosion. The thread must be completely dry before applying makeup
compound; moisture trapped under dope during makeup can cause hydrogen-assisted
stress corrosion under operating conditions. Any thread that cannot be
cleaned to bare metal because of severe corrosion bonding shall be condemned
and the joint rejected.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Thread surfaces cleaned to bare, bright metal; no residual dope, grease,
  scale, or debris visible.
- Thread surface dry; no moisture film or water droplets present before doping.
- Light level at inspection point ≥ 50 foot-candles (540 lux).
- Wire brush and solvent method used; no chemical etch products unless
  specifically approved by the connection OEM.

REJECTION:
- Threads that cannot be cleaned to bare metal after two wire-brush passes and
  solvent wipe — reject joint; do not apply dope over contamination.
- Threads still wet with solvent or moisture when dope application begins —
  stop, re-dry, re-inspect.
- Any use of chlorinated solvent not approved by connection OEM for premium
  connections — stop cleaning, switch to approved solvent, re-clean.

CLAUSE 07 — THREAD INSPECTION — VISUAL AND GAUGE (THREAD GAUGE REQUIREMENTS)
[REF: API RP 5C1, Section 7.4; API Spec 5B — Threading, Gauging, and Thread
Inspection; Sections 6, 8]

After cleaning, perform a systematic visual inspection of every thread using
a thread pick (مسبار الخيط) and adequate lighting to identify cuts, tears,
galling, compression damage, or deformed crests and roots; these defects are
the primary cause of makeup failure and connection leak paths (تسرب). Round
thread (خيط دائري) and Buttress thread (خيط مسطح الأسنان) connections shall be
gauged using calibrated API Spec 5B thread gauges — ring gauge on the pin,
plug gauge on the box — at a minimum frequency of 1 in 50 joints (2%) for
new pipe from the mill and 100% frequency for all recovered or re-use pipe.
Thread gauge calibration must be verified against certified master gauges at
the start of each inspection campaign and at intervals not exceeding 4 hours
of continuous use. For premium connections, thread profile gauging (hand-tight
standoff measurement) must conform to the OEM's published gauge specification;
API Spec 5B gauges alone are not sufficient for premium connection acceptance.
Document every gauge result on the tally sheet; if a pin passes visual but
fails gauge, the joint is rejected for running and tagged for mill repair.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Visual: no cuts, tears, or galling visible; thread crests and roots conform
  to API thread profile; no compressed or flattened threads.
- Gauge result (API Round Thread): pin hand-tight standoff within ±1 turn of
  the plug gauge reference face (API Spec 5B, Section 8.4).
- Gauge result (Buttress Thread): pin hand-tight standoff within ±1.5 turns
  of the reference face (API Spec 5B, Section 8.7).
- Gauge calibration verified against master within the last 4 hours of use.

REJECTION:
- Any thread with visible cut, tear, or galling over more than 1 continuous
  thread turn — reject joint.
- Pin gauge standoff outside ±1 turn (Round) or ±1.5 turns (Buttress) of
  reference — reject joint; tag for mill return.
- Gauge not calibrated within 4-hour period — all results since last
  calibration are invalid; re-gauge all joints inspected since last valid
  calibration.
- Any joint not gauged when 100% gauging is required (recovered pipe) —
  remove from running string until gauged.

CLAUSE 08 — MAKEUP COMPOUND APPLICATION — API MODIFIED THREAD COMPOUND (DOPE)
[REF: API RP 5C1, Section 7.5 — Thread Compound]

Thread compound (دهان الخيط / دوب) applied to casing and tubing connections shall
meet API modified thread compound specification as defined in API RP 5A3;
compounds not meeting this specification (e.g., general-purpose pipe dope, grease,
or petroleum jelly) shall not be used because they alter the torque-turn
relationship and risk either overtorque-induced yield or undertorque-related
leakage. Apply compound to the full length of the pin thread from root to last
thread, and on the inner 2–3 threads of the box; do not apply compound to the
thread protector; do not apply compound to the coupling shoulder face of
Buttress connections unless explicitly specified by the connection OEM. The
correct application is a continuous, even coat approximately 1/32 in (0.8 mm)
thick covering all thread roots and flanks; bare metal must not be visible
through the compound film after application. In cold temperatures (below 5°C /
41°F), warm the compound container to room temperature before application to
restore correct viscosity; cold compound applied too thick will squeeze out
during makeup and produce incorrect torque readings. Dispose of excess compound
and used brushes per the site environmental management plan; do not allow
compound to drain into the wellbore or open ground.

ACCEPTANCE:
- API RP 5A3 compliant modified thread compound, within manufacturer's stated
  shelf life, stored at manufacturer-recommended temperature range.
- Even coat applied to 100% of pin thread surface; box first 2–3 threads coated.
- Film thickness approximately 1/32 in (0.8 mm); no bare metal spots visible.
- Compound temperature ≥ 5°C (41°F) at time of application.

REJECTION:
- Any non-API compound or unapproved substitute used — stop makeup, clean
  threads, re-apply correct compound.
- Compound past shelf-life date on container — discard, obtain fresh compound.
- Uneven or bare-metal-visible application — re-apply before proceeding.
- Compound applied over uncleaned thread (dirt or residual old dope visible
  beneath) — clean threads completely, re-apply.

CLAUSE 09 — API ROUND THREAD MAKEUP — HAND-TIGHT POSITION PLUS WRENCH TURNS
[REF: API RP 5C1, Section 8.3 — Round Thread Makeup; Table 5 — API Round Thread
Makeup Turns]

API 8-round thread connections (خيط دائري) are made up by first establishing the
hand-tight position (وضع اليد) with the coupling flush on the pin by manual
rotation, then applying the specified number of additional wrench turns (دورات
المفتاح) with power tongs (كماشة الشد) to reach final makeup. Hand-tight position
is defined as the point where the coupling shoulder is flush with the last thread
on the pin — this is the baseline; do not use torque as the reference for
hand-tight position, use visual thread engagement. Additional wrench turns from
hand-tight to final makeup vary by size; standard values per API RP 5C1 Table 5
range from 3 to 7 turns depending on nominal size — field personnel must consult
the table for the exact size being run. Final position (الوضع النهائي) is
confirmed when the correct number of wrench turns has been completed AND the
coupling end face is within ±1 thread pitch of the optimum makeup position
shown in API RP 5C1 Table 5; torque monitoring is secondary but must be within
the range stated in API RP 5C1 Tables for the specific size and weight. Never
back off (فك) a made-up round thread connection and re-make it in the same
session without re-cleaning and re-doping the threads.

ACCEPTANCE (example — verify per API RP 5C1 Table 5 for actual size):
- 9-5/8 in Round Thread: hand-tight + 3 turns wrench turns; coupling end face
  flush to +3 thread pitch of pin end face.
- 7 in Round Thread: hand-tight + 3 turns wrench turns.
- 4-1/2 in Round Thread: hand-tight + 3.5 turns wrench turns.
- Thread compound applied per Clause 08 before makeup commences.

REJECTION:
- Final coupling position more than ±2 thread pitches outside the optimum
  window after correct wrench turns applied — reject makeup; inspect threads for
  damage; clean, re-dope, re-make.
- Final torque reading lower than the minimum tabulated value for the size and
  weight — investigate; joint may be under-engaged; inspect.
- Final torque reading exceeding the maximum tabulated value for the size and
  weight — inspect for possible overtorque; check for galling.
- Makeup achieved with fewer than specified wrench turns — back off, clean,
  re-dope, re-make to correct turn count.

CLAUSE 10 — BUTTRESS THREAD MAKEUP — TORQUE RANGE PER SIZE AND WEIGHT
[REF: API RP 5C1, Section 8.4 — Buttress Thread Makeup; Tables 6 and 7]

Buttress thread connections (خيط مسطح) use torque as the primary makeup control
parameter, unlike Round Thread which uses turn count; therefore, a calibrated
torque monitoring system on the power tongs is mandatory for all Buttress
makeup operations. API RP 5C1 Tables 6 and 7 provide the optimum, minimum, and
maximum torque values (ft-lb) for each size and weight combination; field
personnel must use the correct table row and must not extrapolate between rows.
Hand-tight position for Buttress is identified when the coupling face is flush
with the last thread on the pin; from hand-tight, apply tong torque continuously
and monitor the torque chart or display in real time — the torque vs. turn
signature should rise smoothly without spikes or flat spots, which would indicate
thread cross-threading (عبور الخيوط) or jump-out. Final makeup is reached when
the torque reading falls within the optimum range specified in the table for the
applicable size and weight; if optimum cannot be reached without exceeding
the maximum, the connection must be backed off and inspected for thread damage.
Document the final makeup torque value and turn count on the running tally for
every joint made up in the string.

ACCEPTANCE (example values — confirm per API RP 5C1 Table 6/7 for actual size/weight):
- 9-5/8 in, 47.0 lb/ft, K55 Buttress: Optimum 13,200 ft-lb; Min 9,900 ft-lb;
  Max 16,500 ft-lb.
- 7 in, 26.0 lb/ft, N80 Buttress: Optimum 7,200 ft-lb; Min 5,400 ft-lb;
  Max 9,000 ft-lb.
- 5-1/2 in, 20.0 lb/ft, L80 Buttress: Optimum 5,000 ft-lb; Min 3,750 ft-lb;
  Max 6,250 ft-lb.
- Torque chart: smooth monotonic rise; no spikes > 20% above prevailing torque.

REJECTION:
- Final torque below minimum table value — back off, inspect, re-dope, re-make.
- Final torque above maximum table value — back off immediately; inspect for
  galling; if galled, reject joint.
- Torque-turn chart showing spike > 20% above prevailing torque at any point
  during makeup — indicates possible cross-threading; back off, clean, inspect,
  re-make.
- Torque monitoring equipment not calibrated within the shift — all makeup
  results are suspect; verify calibration before continuing.

CLAUSE 11 — PREMIUM CONNECTION MAKEUP — PROPRIETARY TORQUE WINDOWS (OEM REQUIREMENT)
[REF: API RP 5C1, Section 8.5 — Premium Connection Makeup; OEM Technical
Bulletins (applicable per connection type)]

Premium connections (وصلات متميزة / وصلات بريميوم) — including but not limited to
Vallourec VAM, Tenaris TenarisHydril, NOV Grant Prideco, and other proprietary
designs — shall be made up strictly in accordance with the OEM's published
running procedure for that specific connection, size, weight, and grade
combination; API RP 5C1 standard torque tables do NOT apply to premium
connections. The OEM running procedure must be on the rig floor in hard copy
or approved electronic format before running commences; no OEM document means
no premium connection makeup shall proceed. Minimum torque (دوزة دنيا), optimum
torque (دوزة مثلى), and maximum torque (دوزة قصوى) windows must be loaded into
the tong control unit before makeup begins; the control unit must be capable
of recording torque vs. turn trace for every joint. A qualified premium
connection make-up supervisor or OEM-certified technician shall be present
during makeup of the first 10 joints of any new premium connection run, and
upon any change of crew running the connection. Torque-turn trace for every
joint must be archived and made available for post-run quality review.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Final torque falls within OEM-specified optimum window (min to max) for the
  specific connection, size, weight, and grade.
- Torque-turn trace shows smooth curve with delta-torque per turn within OEM
  acceptance band; no spikes or flat spots beyond OEM tolerance.
- Shoulder torque (for shouldered designs) confirmed on trace; OEM-defined
  "shoulder" event visible on chart.
- All makeup performed with OEM-approved compound only.

REJECTION:
- Final torque outside OEM minimum-to-maximum window — back off immediately;
  inspect per OEM protocol; re-make or reject per OEM guidance.
- Torque-turn trace shows spike > OEM-specified limit at any point — back off,
  inspect; if galling found on metal-to-metal seal zone, reject joint.
- OEM running procedure not available on rig floor — suspend premium makeup
  until document is available and verified.
- Non-OEM thread compound used on premium connection — back off, clean,
  obtain OEM-approved compound, re-make.

CLAUSE 12 — POWER TONG USE — TONG PLACEMENT, BACKUP, AND TORQUE MONITORING
[REF: API RP 5C1, Section 8.6 — Power Tong Operation]

Power tongs (كماشة الشد الآلية) shall be positioned so that the tong jaw grips
the pipe body in the tong area between the upset and coupling; the jaw must
never grip on the upset taper zone, the thread, or the coupling itself. The
backup tong (كماشة التثبيت) must be engaged on the coupling or on the joint
below the connection being made up, gripping the body — not the threads —
so that torque reaction is absorbed by the tong and not by the rig floor
or rotary table components. Tong jaw dies (أسنان الكماشة) shall be inspected
every shift for worn or broken teeth; a worn die transfers point loads that
produce tong marks (نقوش الكماشة) exceeding API RP 5C1 maximum allowable
depth; replace dies before they are worn to 50% of original tooth height.
The torque measuring system — mechanical dial gauge or electronic load-cell
system — shall be calibrated with a certified deadweight or load-cell reference
at the beginning of every running job; calibration records must be retained on
site. Hydraulic supply pressure must be verified against the tong manufacturer's
torque-pressure chart before makeup begins to confirm the gauge can reach
the required torque range; if the hydraulic system cannot reach required pressure,
do not attempt makeup and resolve the hydraulic issue first.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Tong jaw positioned on pipe body in the tong grip area only; minimum 6 in
  (150 mm) from the coupling face.
- Backup tong engaged on pipe body of joint below; no jaw contact with
  any threaded area.
- Tong dies: tooth height ≥ 50% of original; no broken die segments.
- Torque measuring system calibrated at start of job; calibration certificate
  available on site.

REJECTION:
- Tong jaw mark found on coupling, thread taper, or last-engaged thread zone
  — stop makeup; inspect connection for damage; reject if deformation present.
- Torque gauge reading higher than calibration chart value for the applied
  hydraulic pressure by > 5% — recalibrate before continuing.
- Backup tong slipping or absent — halt makeup; re-position backup before
  resuming.
- Tong dies with > 50% tooth wear or broken segments — replace dies before
  next joint.

CLAUSE 13 — TONG MARK ACCEPTANCE — MAXIMUM DEPTH AND LOCATION
[REF: API RP 5C1, Section 7.8 — Pipe Body Damage; Section 8.6.4 — Tong Mark
Acceptance Criteria]

Tong marks (نقوش الكماشة / علامات الكماشة) are the most common form of handling
damage on casing and tubing and must be assessed quantitatively, not subjectively.
Measure the tong mark depth with a calibrated pit gauge (مقياس العمق) or depth
micrometer; visual assessment alone is not acceptable for borderline cases.
A tong mark located in the pipe body (not in the coupling zone) with a depth
that does not reduce the remaining wall thickness below the minimum wall
thickness per API Spec 5CT (87.5% of nominal wall) is potentially acceptable
if no other damage is present. However, any tong mark located within 12 in
(305 mm) of the coupling face or within the tong-area upset is subject to closer
scrutiny because stress concentration at the coupling proximity is higher in
service. Tong marks with sharp, tool-mark profiles must be dressed with a fine
file to remove the stress-riser cusp before measurement; the dressed mark depth
is then used for the assessment. Marks that form a continuous groove around
more than 40% of the pipe circumference at any single cross-section shall be
rejected regardless of depth.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Tong mark depth ≤ 12.5% of nominal wall thickness (leaving ≥ 87.5% wall)
  when measured with calibrated depth gauge.
- Mark located > 12 in (305 mm) from coupling face; not in upset zone.
- Circumferential extent of mark at any cross-section ≤ 40% of pipe
  circumference.
- Sharp cusp dressed smooth before acceptance measurement.

REJECTION:
- Tong mark depth > 12.5% of nominal wall (i.e., remaining wall < 87.5% of
  nominal) — reject joint or cut and re-thread per mill if feasible.
- Tong mark located within 12 in (305 mm) of coupling face and depth > 10%
  of nominal wall — reject joint from running string.
- Circumferential groove mark at any cross-section exceeding 40% of
  circumference — reject regardless of depth.
- Any tong mark in the threaded area or on the coupling — reject connection;
  do not make up.

CLAUSE 14 — PICKING AND STABBING — STABBING GUIDE USE AND THREAD DAMAGE PREVENTION
[REF: API RP 5C1, Section 8.2 — Stabbing]

When stabbing (تثبيت / إدخال) a joint of casing or tubing into the joint below
in the rotary, always use a correctly sized stabbing guide (دليل الإدخال) on the
box end of the joint being picked up; a stabbing guide centers the pin and
prevents the thread face from contacting the coupling shoulder obliquely, which
is the primary cause of first-thread damage during running. Stabbing guides
shall be sized to fit snugly inside the coupling bore and must be made of brass,
aluminum, or reinforced plastic — materials that will not score the thread form
on contact; steel stabbing guides are prohibited on API round and Buttress
threads and on all premium connections. The joint being stabbed must be held
vertically plumb within ±3° of vertical before the stabbing guide enters the
coupling; oblique stabbing beyond 3° forces the pin thread into the coupling
shoulder and causes jump-in damage. After stab, slowly lower the joint under
control and rotate slowly (maximum 10 RPM) to start thread engagement; do not
spin a joint freely without thread engagement confirmed. The pick-up elevator
must be equipped with slips or a safety latch; do not depend on friction alone
to retain the joint during the stab.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Correct-size brass or aluminum stabbing guide fitted on box before stab.
- Joint plumb within ±3° of vertical at point of stab.
- Thread engagement confirmed before power tong makeup begins; no grinding or
  resistance at stab indicating oblique contact.
- Maximum stabbing speed: joint lowered under operator control; no free drops.

REJECTION:
- Stabbing performed without stabbing guide — stop; retrieve joint; inspect
  thread for damage before proceeding.
- Joint stabbed at > 3° oblique — retrieve, inspect thread; if first thread
  torn, reject joint.
- Steel stabbing guide used on any connection — prohibited; replace with
  brass/aluminum immediately.
- Any joint free-dropped onto the joint below without controlled stab —
  inspect both pin and box for thread damage; reject if damaged.

CLAUSE 15 — RUNNING SPEEDS — MAXIMUM JOINT RUNNING SPEED THROUGH ROTARY
[REF: API RP 5C1, Section 9 — Running Casing and Tubing; Section 9.5 — Running
Speed]

Running speed (سرعة الإدخال) for casing and tubing shall be controlled to
prevent formation damage from surge pressure (ضغط الموجة) generated by the
annular piston effect as the pipe displaces fluid below it; excessive running
speed is a primary cause of lost circulation (فقدان الدوران) and formation
breakdown in tight or depleted zones. API RP 5C1 does not specify a single
universal running speed but requires the operator to calculate the maximum
permissible running speed based on the equivalent circulating density (ECD)
at the formation of interest and the fracture gradient; the wellsite engineer
is responsible for providing the permitted running speed in the running
programme. As a field guidance value, a maximum running speed of 150 ft/min
(45.7 m/min) shall not be exceeded for casing and 100 ft/min (30.5 m/min) for
liner or tight-clearance situations unless surge calculations confirm a higher
speed is safe. When running through troublesome zones (lost circulation zones,
weak formations, or open hole), speed shall be reduced to 50 ft/min (15.2 m/min)
or as directed by the wellsite engineer. Never reciprocate the string at
running speed; reciprocation and rotation (if used) must be done at controlled
speed with the driller monitoring hook load for overpull or set-down weight
indicating stuck pipe.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Maximum running speed ≤ 150 ft/min (45.7 m/min) in open hole for standard
  casing; ≤ 100 ft/min (30.5 m/min) for tight clearance liner.
- In weak or depleted zones: ≤ 50 ft/min (15.2 m/min) or as specified in
  approved running programme.
- Surge pressure calculation performed and approved by wellsite engineer before
  running commences.
- Driller monitoring hook load continuously; weight indicator visible from
  driller's position.

REJECTION:
- Running speed exceeding approved maximum for any zone — immediately slow down;
  document exceedance in the morning report.
- No surge calculation performed and no running speed limit defined in running
  programme — suspend running until calculation is provided.
- Hook load indicates set-down weight exceeding 10% of string weight in air
  while running — stop, assess for tight hole or bridge before continuing.

CLAUSE 16 — CENTRALIZER AND FLOAT EQUIPMENT INSTALLATION — INSPECTION CRITERIA
[REF: API RP 5C1, Section 9.3 — Float Equipment and Centralizers; API RP 10D-2
(Centralizer Placement)]

Centralizers (ملمِّعات / مركِّزات) and float equipment (معدات التعويم — float
collar, float shoe) shall be inspected at the wellsite before installation; each
unit shall be free of cracks, visible deformation, and missing or damaged
elastomeric elements. Float shoes and float collars shall be pressure-tested at
the surface using water or drilling fluid before running; the test pressure shall
be not less than the maximum anticipated wellbore pressure at the float depth,
typically 1.5× the hydrostatic head at shoe depth, but not exceeding the
equipment manufacturer's rated working pressure. Bow-spring centralizers shall
be checked for broken or permanently deformed bows; any centralizer with more
than one broken bow or with spring bows that do not return to within 90% of the
rated stand-off after manual compression must be discarded and replaced. Slip-on
centralizers must be secured with properly sized stop collars; stop collars shall
be set at the manufacturer's specified torque to prevent migration during
running. Centralizer placement shall conform to the centralizing programme in
the casing design; field changes to placement spacing require written approval
from the wellsite engineer.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Float shoe and collar: no cracks, no deformation; float valve seats and
  discs intact; elastomers free of cuts or swelling incompatible with mud type.
- Surface pressure test of float valve: holds test pressure for minimum 10
  minutes with zero pressure drop.
- Test pressure = 1.5 × hydrostatic head at shoe depth, not exceeding OEM
  rated WP.
- Bow-spring centralizer: all bows intact; restoring force ≥ 90% of rated
  at maximum expected wellbore OD.
- Stop collar torque set per manufacturer specification.

REJECTION:
- Float valve leaks at any pressure during 10-minute test — reject; replace
  float shoe or collar; re-test before running.
- Centralizer with > 1 broken bow — reject; replace.
- Centralizer with stand-off restoring force < 90% of rated — reject; replace.
- Stop collar not torqued to spec or found loose on inspection — re-torque
  or replace; do not run loose stop collars.
- Float equipment OEM rated pressure less than calculated test pressure —
  do not test to that level; consult OEM for alternative; if no resolution,
  replace equipment with higher-rated unit.

CLAUSE 17 — DAMAGE ASSESSMENT — WHEN TO REJECT PIPE FROM THE HOLE
(BUCKLING AND OVALITY)
[REF: API RP 5C1, Section 7.6 — Pipe Body Damage; API Spec 5CT — OD Tolerance
and Ovality Limits]

A joint of casing or tubing that has been subjected to sinusoidal or helical
buckling (انحناء لولبي / انبعاج) in the hole may exhibit permanent plastic
deformation that cannot be detected by visual inspection of the pipe body alone;
the only reliable field assessment is an ovality measurement (قياس الاستدارة)
using an external diameter gauge or a calliper at multiple cross-sections.
Ovality is defined as (OD_max − OD_min) / OD_nominal × 100%; API Spec 5CT
permits a maximum OD tolerance of +0.75% / −0.75% on individual measurements,
but post-buckling ovality is assessed against a tighter field criterion.
Pipe with measured ovality exceeding 1.5% of nominal OD at any cross-section
shall be rejected from the running string until further assessment (wall
thickness at the ovalised section, yield verification) confirms it is fit for
service. Any joint with visible permanent bow (sagitta > 1/4 in per 10 ft or
6.35 mm per 3 m) shall be rejected outright without ovality measurement; it
cannot be straightened and re-used in a high-pressure string. Joints with
dents (انبعاج موضعي) exceeding 2% of OD in depth — measured as the perpendicular
depth from the original pipe surface contour to the dent bottom — shall be
rejected.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Ovality at any cross-section ≤ 1.5% of nominal OD.
- No visible permanent bow; sagitta ≤ 1/4 in per 10 ft (6.35 mm per 3 m)
  over full joint length.
- Dent depth ≤ 2% of nominal OD at any location on the pipe body.

REJECTION:
- Ovality > 1.5% of nominal OD at any measured cross-section — reject joint
  from running string.
- Visible permanent bow with sagitta > 1/4 in per 10 ft — reject joint;
  do not attempt to straighten.
- Dent depth > 2% of nominal OD — reject joint from running string.
- Any joint with deformation in the coupling zone or within 6 in (150 mm)
  of pin end — reject; connection integrity cannot be assured.

CLAUSE 18 — PIPE WITH MASHED OR DAMAGED CONNECTION — RETURN TO YARD VS. DISCARD
[REF: API RP 5C1, Section 7.9 — Handling Damage to Ends; Section 10 —
Reconditioning]

When a connection (وصلة) is found to be mashed, galled, or damaged beyond the
acceptance criteria in Clause 07 and Clause 13, it must be assessed against two
disposition options: return to mill for reconditioning, or discard. A connection
may be returned to an API-licensed mill or thread shop for reconditioning if the
pipe body is within all acceptance criteria, the connection damage is confined to
the last 1–3 threads and is not in the body of the coupling, and the overall pipe
length after re-cutting will remain within API Spec 5CT minimum joint-length
requirements (L-80: minimum 28 ft / 8.5 m for casing; minimum 28 ft for long
threads). A joint shall be discarded (disposed of as scrap) if the damage extends
into the pipe body beyond the thread makeup length, the pipe body has already
been cut to minimum acceptable joint length, or the grade or wall thickness of
the remaining body is insufficient to justify the cost of reconditioning.
Document the disposition decision on a Non-Conformance Report (NCR); verbal
disposition without documentation is not acceptable.

ACCEPTANCE FOR RETURN TO MILL:
- Pipe body within all dimensional and condition acceptance criteria per
  Clauses 07, 13, 17.
- Connection damage confined to last 1–3 threads only; coupling body undamaged.
- After re-cutting, joint length will be ≥ 28 ft (8.5 m) for casing or ≥ 25 ft
  (7.6 m) for tubing (verify per API Spec 5CT Table A.24 for actual size).
- NCR raised and disposition signed off by wellsite engineer.

REJECTION / DISCARD:
- Pipe body damaged, ovalised, or buckled in addition to connection damage —
  discard.
- After re-cutting, joint length would fall below API Spec 5CT minimum — discard.
- Connection damage extending into pipe body beyond thread makeup length —
  discard.
- Any grade H40, J55, or K55 casing with severe corrosion in body — assess
  wall; if remaining wall < 87.5% of nominal anywhere in body — discard.

CLAUSE 19 — PIPE INSPECTION IN HOLE — CALLIPER LOGS AND ACCEPTABLE WALL LOSS
[REF: API RP 5C1, Section 11 — Inspection of Casing in Service; API RP 5C2 —
Maintenance of Casing and Tubing (cross-reference)]

Casing (غلاف) remaining in a well after initial placement is subject to ongoing
corrosion from formation fluids, CO₂ (ثاني أكسيد الكربون), H₂S (كبريتيد
الهيدروجين), and mechanical wear from drill string rotation. Periodic calliper
surveys (مسح الكاليبر / مسح سُمك الجدار) using multi-finger callipers or
electromagnetic inspection tools shall be run at intervals specified in the well
integrity programme, or triggered by any indication of sustained casing pressure,
increased hook load on wireline, or anomalous fluid flow. The calliper log
shall be interpreted to determine the minimum remaining wall thickness (t_min)
at each depth; a wall loss calculation shall be performed as:
    % Wall Loss = [(t_nominal − t_min) / t_nominal] × 100%
where t_nominal is the nominal wall thickness per API Spec 5CT for the applicable
casing size and weight. Any zone with wall loss exceeding 20% of nominal wall
shall be assessed for remedial action — squeeze, patch, or liner — before the
casing is returned to service at its rated burst/collapse pressure. The calliper
log interpretation report shall be peer-reviewed by a qualified casing design
engineer before a remedial decision is made.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Measured minimum remaining wall (t_min) ≥ 80% of nominal wall thickness
  for the applicable size and weight per API Spec 5CT.
- Calliper log resolution: tool vertical resolution ≤ 6 in (150 mm); radial
  resolution ≤ 0.02 in (0.5 mm).
- % Wall Loss ≤ 20% at any logged depth.

REJECTION:
- % Wall Loss > 20% at any depth — flag zone; casing rated capacity at that
  depth must be derated; initiate remedial programme.
- % Wall Loss > 37.5% at any depth — equivalent to remaining wall < 62.5%
  of nominal; casing at that depth is to be treated as requiring immediate
  remedial action; do not pressure-test above derated rating.
- Calliper log tool quality insufficient for reliable interpretation (poor
  centralization, tool malfunction > 20% of fingers stuck) — re-log before
  any remedial decision.

CLAUSE 20 — RECOVERED PIPE INSPECTION — MILL SCALE, CORROSION, PITS, AND
ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA
[REF: API RP 5C1, Section 10.2 — Inspection of Recovered Pipe; API Spec 5CT,
Section 10]

Casing or tubing recovered (مسترجع) from a well must be treated as used pipe
and subjected to a full inspection campaign before any decision is made to
re-run it in the same or a different well; it shall never be mixed into a
new-pipe running string without passing this inspection. Visual inspection of
the external pipe body must identify and quantify: mill scale (قشر المطحنة)
remaining from original manufacture, active or arrested corrosion (تآكل),
and pitting (حُفر / نقوش تآكل). For pitting, measure the maximum pit depth
using a calibrated pit gauge; a corrosion pit does not fail solely on the basis
of area or cosmetic appearance but on the basis of remaining wall thickness.
Internal visual inspection using a bore scope or camera shall be performed on
all recovered tubing and on any casing joint where internal fluid-flow evidence
suggests internal corrosion; scale and wax deposits must be cleaned before
inspection. All joints must pass the full drift and thread inspection per
Clauses 05 and 07 before re-run acceptance; recovered pipe requires 100%
thread gauging, not the 2% sampling rate applicable to new mill pipe.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Mill scale: no active corrosion beneath; scale is tight and not
  underlift (no loose flakes); OD after scale is within nominal OD tolerance
  per API Spec 5CT (±1.0%).
- Corrosion pits: maximum pit depth ≤ 12.5% of nominal wall thickness
  (remaining wall ≥ 87.5% of nominal) at the deepest pit.
- Internal inspection: no active pitting exceeding 20% of nominal wall
  (see Clause 19 criterion); bore free of scale deposits after cleaning.
- 100% drift pass; 100% thread gauge pass.

REJECTION:
- Any corrosion pit where remaining wall at the pit bottom is < 87.5% of
  nominal wall — reject joint.
- Active (wet) corrosion present — clean, re-inspect; if corrosion extends
  to > 12.5% of nominal wall depth, reject.
- Mill scale that is loose, underlift, or blistered — remove; measure OD
  beneath; if OD < minimum API Spec 5CT tolerance, reject.
- Any recovered joint that fails drift — reject immediately; do not re-run.
- Any recovered joint that fails thread gauge — reject; tag for mill
  reconditioning assessment per Clause 18.

CLAUSE 21 — INTERNAL CORROSION ASSESSMENT — MINIMUM WALL THICKNESS FORMULA
[REF: API RP 5C1, Section 11.3 — Remaining Strength Assessment; API RP 579-1
(fitness-for-service basis); Barlow Formula for Burst Pressure]

When a calliper log or bore scope inspection identifies internal corrosion
(تآكل داخلي), the remaining burst pressure capacity of the affected zone must
be recalculated before the casing or tubing is accepted for continued or future
service. Use the Barlow formula (or API RP 5C3 burst formula) as the basis for
remaining burst capacity:
    P_burst(remaining) = [2 × SMYS × t_min] / OD
where:
    P_burst(remaining) = derated burst pressure (psi)
    SMYS = specified minimum yield strength for the grade (psi):
           J55: 55,000 psi; N80: 80,000 psi; L80: 80,000 psi;
           P110: 110,000 psi; Q125: 125,000 psi
    t_min = minimum measured remaining wall thickness (in)
    OD = nominal outside diameter (in)
This derated burst pressure must be compared to the maximum anticipated surface
treating pressure (MASP) or maximum wellhead pressure for the string; if
P_burst(remaining) < 1.1 × MASP, the affected joint does not have adequate
safety margin and must be remediated or replaced. Document the calculation on
a dedicated well integrity assessment form signed by the casing design engineer.

ACCEPTANCE:
- P_burst(remaining) ≥ 1.1 × MASP (10% safety margin above MASP).
- Alternatively, P_burst(remaining) ≥ 80% of the original API burst rating
  for the pipe in new condition, as a secondary check.
- Calculated using t_min from calliper log (worst case measurement in the
  assessed zone).

REJECTION:
- P_burst(remaining) < 1.1 × MASP — reject pipe at that depth for full-rated
  service; initiate remedial programme (liner, patch, squeeze).
- P_burst(remaining) < 80% of original API burst rating — flag for engineering
  review; may require well shut-in or operational pressure reduction.
- t_min used in calculation was not measured by a calibrated tool (estimated
  or assumed) — result is invalid; re-run calliper before accepting calculation.
- Any zone where t_min < 50% of nominal wall — do not calculate; treat as
  failed section; initiate emergency remedial action.

CLAUSE 22 — REJECTION CRITERIA FOR RECOVERED PIPE — ABSOLUTE MINIMUM WALL
[REF: API RP 5C1, Section 10.3 — Rejection Criteria; API Spec 5CT, Section 10.15
— Minimum Wall Thickness Requirement]

The absolute minimum acceptable remaining wall thickness (الحد الأدنى المطلق
لسُمك الجدار) for recovered casing or tubing being considered for re-use is
87.5% of the nominal wall thickness specified in API Spec 5CT for that size and
weight; this is the same criterion as the manufacturing minimum wall tolerance
and represents the absolute floor below which no pipe body is acceptable for
re-run service regardless of the load calculation result. In practice, a field
rejection criterion of 80% of nominal wall (20% wall loss) is used as a
planning threshold to trigger formal engineering review; the absolute rejection
below 87.5% is non-negotiable at wellsite level without written engineering
exception from the company well integrity authority. Calculate the absolute
minimum wall thickness for each size and weight using:
    t_min_absolute = 0.875 × t_nominal
For example:
    9-5/8 in, 47.0 lb/ft (t_nominal = 0.472 in): t_min = 0.413 in (10.49 mm)
    7 in, 26.0 lb/ft (t_nominal = 0.362 in): t_min = 0.317 in (8.05 mm)
    4-1/2 in, 12.60 lb/ft (t_nominal = 0.271 in): t_min = 0.237 in (6.02 mm)
Recovered pipe meeting the 87.5% minimum wall criterion still requires passing
all other acceptance criteria in Clauses 05, 07, 17, and 20 before it can
be cleared for re-run.

ACCEPTANCE:
- Remaining wall thickness at all measured points ≥ 87.5% of nominal wall
  thickness per API Spec 5CT for the applicable size and weight.
- All other criteria per Clauses 05, 07, 17, 20 simultaneously satisfied.
- Written re-run clearance issued by casing design engineer.

REJECTION (ABSOLUTE — NO EXCEPTIONS AT WELLSITE LEVEL):
- Any measured remaining wall thickness < 87.5% of nominal wall at any point
  on the pipe body — reject joint; do not re-run under any circumstances
  without written engineering exception from company well integrity authority.
- Wall loss > 20% of nominal at any measured point — mandatory engineering
  review before any re-run decision; field supervisor cannot clear this.
- Any joint where the minimum wall cannot be confirmed (no calliper or UT data)
  — treat as rejected until measurement is available.
- Any recovered joint cleared verbally without written engineer sign-off —
  not accepted; written clearance mandatory.
