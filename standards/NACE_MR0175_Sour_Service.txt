[STANDARD: NACE MR0175 / ISO 15156]
[TITLE: Petroleum and Natural Gas Industries — Materials for Use in H2S-Containing Environments in Oil and Gas Production]
[PARTS: Part 1 (General Principles), Part 2 (Carbon and Low Alloy Steels), Part 3 (CRAs and Other Alloys)]

[CLAUSE: Part 1 — 3.1 — Sour Service Definition]
SOUR SERVICE is defined as any environment containing H2S (hydrogen sulfide) that meets or exceeds the threshold conditions where H2S can cause sulfide stress cracking (SSC), hydrogen-induced cracking (HIC), or stress-oriented hydrogen-induced cracking (SOHIC) in susceptible materials.

THRESHOLD CONDITIONS (Part 1 — Annex A):
H2S partial pressure ≥ 0.0003 MPa absolute (0.05 psi absolute, 0.3 mbar) in any gas phase.
If liquid phase present: H2S partial pressure threshold still applies to the co-existing gas phase.
AQUEOUS SYSTEMS: If liquid water is present AND the gas phase H2S partial pressure meets the threshold, the system is sour service.
ACCEPTANCE: Sour service correctly identified and appropriate materials selected.
REJECTION: Equipment in sour service using non-qualified materials. Ignoring H2S partial pressure calculation and assuming non-sour.
---

[CLAUSE: Part 1 — 4.1 — General Principles for Material Selection]
Materials for sour service shall be selected to resist:
SSC — Sulfide Stress Cracking: primarily affects high-strength steels and hard welds. Mechanism: H2S causes hydrogen absorption, leading to cracking under tensile stress. Most common in cold service (below 65°C/150°F).
HIC — Hydrogen-Induced Cracking: hydrogen collects at inclusions in steel, forms blisters and internal cracks. NOT stress-dependent — can occur without applied load. Worst in high H2S partial pressure.
SOHIC — Stress Oriented HIC: array of HIC cracks linked by SSC cracks. Occurs at stress concentrations (welds, notches).

MATERIAL QUALIFICATION:
Materials shall be qualified by one of:
a) Listed in NACE MR0175 / ISO 15156 tables as pre-qualified for sour service
b) Tested per the standard's test methods and documented in a qualification test report
ACCEPTANCE: Materials used in sour service are either pre-qualified by the standard's tables or have documented qualification test reports.
REJECTION: Using materials assumed to be acceptable without verification against standard tables or test reports.

[CLAUSE: Part 2 — 7.1 — Carbon and Low Alloy Steel Requirements (The 22 HRC Rule)]
HARDNESS LIMITS FOR SOUR SERVICE — CARBON AND LOW ALLOY STEELS:
This is the most widely referenced requirement in NACE MR0175:

MAXIMUM HARDNESS:
- Base metal: ≤ 22 HRC (Rockwell C) = 237 HB (Brinell) = 250 HV10 (Vickers) at any location
- Weld metal: ≤ 22 HRC at any location in the weld deposit
- Heat-affected zone (HAZ): ≤ 22 HRC at any location
- This limit applies across the ENTIRE cross-section, not just the surface

HARDNESS TESTING REQUIREMENTS:
- Production hardness testing is required — hardness values from mill certificates alone are not sufficient
- Test method: Rockwell C (HRC), Brinell (HB), or Vickers (HV) per applicable ASTM standard
- Convert between scales using ASTM E140
- For welds: hardness survey per weld cross-section (minimum: 3 indentations weld metal, 3 in each HAZ, 3 in each base metal)

WHY 22 HRC: Above this hardness, steels become susceptible to SSC in H2S environments. This is an empirical threshold established by extensive field and laboratory experience.
ACCEPTANCE: All hardness measurements ≤ 22 HRC (or equivalent) at all locations.
REJECTION: Any single hardness measurement >22 HRC in any part of any pressure-containing or pressure-controlling component in sour service. Any weld hardness survey showing >22 HRC in weld, HAZ, or base metal.

[CLAUSE: Part 2 — 7.2 — Carbon Steel Pipe and Plate for Sour Service]
PLATE AND PIPE:
Must be manufactured by killed steel practice (aluminum-killed, fine-grain). Steel cleanliness requirements to resist HIC:
- Sulfur content: ≤ 0.003% (mass fraction) for HIC-resistant steel
- Phosphorus content: ≤ 0.020% (mass fraction)
- Carbon Equivalent (CEIIW): ≤ 0.43 for pipes/plates

HIC TESTING:
For HIC-resistant steel: Test per NACE TM0284. Criteria:
- Crack Length Ratio (CLR): ≤ 15%
- Crack Thickness Ratio (CTR): ≤ 5%
- Crack Sensitivity Ratio (CSR): ≤ 2%
ACCEPTANCE: Chemistry within limits. HIC test passes all three criteria.
REJECTION: Steel with sulfur >0.003% or phosphorus >0.020% used in sour service without HIC testing and qualification. HIC test CLR >15%, CTR >5%, or CSR >2%.
---

[CLAUSE: Part 2 — 8.1 — Welding Requirements for Sour Service]
SOUR SERVICE WELD REQUIREMENTS:
1. WPS shall be qualified specifically for sour service — the WPS shall include a requirement to verify hardness of the completed weld.
2. PWHT: Post-weld heat treatment is recommended and often required for:
   - Carbon steel with CEIIW >0.35 or wall thickness >19mm
   - Any alloy steel in sour service
   - Any weld where HAZ hardness exceeds 22 HRC without PWHT
3. Filler metals: Select filler metals that deposit weld metal with ≤22 HRC hardness in the as-welded condition. Pre-qualification of filler metals by hardness testing on a representative coupon.
4. Hardness verification: Every production weld or batch of production welds (per qualified procedure) shall have hardness survey performed.

PREHEAT FOR SOUR SERVICE WELDS:
Higher than standard minimum preheat recommended to ensure slow cooling and avoid hard HAZ:
- Carbon steel <12mm: minimum 10°C (50°F) preheat
- Carbon steel 12–25mm: minimum 93°C (200°F) preheat
- Carbon steel >25mm: minimum 121°C (250°F) preheat
ACCEPTANCE: PWHT performed where required. Hardness survey of all production welds ≤22 HRC. Preheat per above.
REJECTION: Any weld hardness >22 HRC without PWHT. Skipping hardness survey. PWHT skipped on thick or high-CE material. Filler metal not pre-qualified for hardness.

[CLAUSE: Part 2 — Table A.2 — Pre-Qualified Carbon Steel Requirements Summary]
PIPE AND TUBULAR PRODUCTS pre-qualified for sour service (selected requirements):
- API 5L Grade B, X42, X52, X60, X65 (seamless or welded per specific chemistry requirements)
- API 5CT grades for casing and tubing (specific grades only)
- Carbon steel castings: maximum hardness 22 HRC
- Carbon steel forgings: maximum hardness 22 HRC
All pre-qualified materials: maximum 22 HRC, sulfur ≤0.003%, fine-grain killed steel.

MATERIALS REQUIRING ADDITIONAL QUALIFICATION (NOT pre-qualified, test required):
- High-strength steels above X65
- CrMo alloy steels (must test per NACE TM0177)
- Any material that has been cold-worked (cold-worked materials can have locally elevated hardness)
ACCEPTANCE: Material is in the pre-qualified list at the correct grade with all chemistry requirements met.
REJECTION: High-strength steel above X65 used in sour service without documented qualification tests. Cold-worked material in sour service without hardness verification at all cold-worked areas.

[CLAUSE: Part 2 — 9.1 — SSC Testing for Non-Listed Materials]
Materials not pre-qualified must be tested per NACE TM0177:
METHOD A: Tensile test in H2S environment
METHOD B: Bent beam test
METHOD C: C-ring test
METHOD D: Double cantilever beam fracture mechanics test

ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA FOR METHOD A (most common):
Test specimen loaded at 80% of specified minimum yield strength (SMYS). Exposed to H2S test solution per TM0177 for 720 hours. No fracture = pass.
ACCEPTANCE: No fracture in 720 hours at 80% SMYS.
REJECTION: Any fracture before 720 hours. Test specimen hardness >22 HRC before test (invalid test — material already failed the hardness criterion).

[CLAUSE: Part 3 — 7.1 — Austenitic Stainless Steels in Sour Service]
CHLORIDE STRESS CORROSION CRACKING (CSCC) RISK:
Austenitic stainless steels (304, 316, 321, 347) are resistant to SSC but are susceptible to chloride-induced stress corrosion cracking in hot environments with chlorides present.
TEMPERATURE THRESHOLD: Austenitic SS should not be used in environments containing both H2S and chlorides above:
- 60°C (140°F) for 316L in moderate chloride
- Above these temperatures without specific qualification testing

DUPLEX STAINLESS STEEL (22Cr, 25Cr):
Can be used in sour service with restrictions:
- Maximum H2S partial pressure limits per standard tables
- Maximum temperature limits per alloy
- Must be tested if conditions exceed table limits
ACCEPTANCE: Austenitic SS within temperature and chloride limits. Duplex SS within H2S partial pressure and temperature limits.
REJECTION: 316L used above 60°C in H2S + chloride environment without engineering qualification. Duplex SS in sour service above tabled H2S limits without qualification testing.
